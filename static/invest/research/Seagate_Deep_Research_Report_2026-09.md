## Executive Summary <!-- report-module:overview -->

**One-line thesis:** Seagate is the only nearline HDD maker already shipping HAMR in volume — about 40% of its nearline exabytes ran on HAMR exiting June 2026 (call) and its 44TB-class Mozaic 4 is ramping at the two largest cloud providers — but on FY2026 figures that lead shows up as **price, not cost**: in Q4 FY2026 Seagate earned $16.65M of revenue per exabyte against Western Digital's $16.22M, spent $7.87M per exabyte against $7.40M, and kept almost exactly the same gross profit per exabyte ($8.77M versus $8.82M). At the 2026-09-22 close of $919.84 the market is already paying 31.5x guidance-annualized EPS for the moment that cost advantage arrives.

**Investment judgment:**
- **Stance:** cautious
- **Confidence:** medium — the technology lead and the demand allocation are documented in primary sources; the negative skew comes from the exit multiple, which is an assumption, and from a cost crossover that has been guided but not yet reported
- **Key catalysts:** Q1 FY2027 results (expected October 2026, date not yet announced; guided non-GAAP operating margin of about 50%, implying a gross margin near 57.3%); HAMR share of nearline exabytes (40% at June 2026, prior target 70% by June 2027, call); Mozaic 4 reaching 50% of HAMR exabytes by the end of calendar 2026; Western Digital's 44TB HAMR, which it says ships in the first half of calendar 2027 (WDC call)
- **Monitoring window:** 12-18 months (through the HAMR 70% milestone and the Mozaic 5 qualification window)

## 1. Business Overview and Unit Economics <!-- report-module:business -->

Seagate Technology Holdings plc is incorporated in Ireland with its principal executive office in Singapore (121 Woodlands Avenue 5). It designs and manufactures hard disk drives and storage systems, making its own read/write heads and recording media, with component, sub-assembly and final assembly in China, Malaysia, Northern Ireland, Singapore, Thailand and the United States. At 2026-07-03 it employed about 30,000 people (about 25,000 in Asia) and held about 3,036 US patents. The 10-K describes two end markets: **Data Center** (nearline products sold into cloud, enterprise and VIA customers, plus systems) and **Edge IoT** (everything else — NAS, video and image, client, consumer). The data-center portfolio now reaches **44TB per drive**; the Edge IoT portfolio spans 1TB to 32TB.

**Basis warnings.** First, FY2026 had 53 weeks and the extra week fell in Q1 FY2026, which had 14 weeks (Q1 FY2026 10-Q). FY2025 had 52 weeks. The 34% full-year growth therefore contains roughly one week of calendar effect, and the Q1 FY2027 guide of +56% Y/Y at the midpoint (call) is measured against a 14-week base — it **understates** underlying growth. Western Digital closed FY2026 on the same day, 2026-07-03, also with a 14-week Q1, so the cross-company comparison below is not distorted. Second, Data Center / Edge IoT is a disclosure of end markets, not ASC 280 segments; Seagate reports one segment.

The direction is clear and has two speeds. **For the full year, FY2026 was almost entirely a volume year**: exabytes up 32.6%, blended revenue per exabyte up only 1.1% ($15.29M to $15.46M). **In the most recent quarter, price came back**: Q4 blended revenue per exabyte was up 10.3% Y/Y ($15.09M to $16.65M), which matches the "10% year-over-year price per exabyte growth in June" put to the CFO on the call and confirmed by him ("You are correct"); analysts on the same call read the Q1 FY2027 guide as implying about 20% Y/Y — that figure is an analyst's inference, not company guidance.

**The mix trap in the blended number.** Seagate's blended revenue per exabyte ($15.46M in FY2026) is higher than Western Digital's ($14.82M), but that is mostly **mix**: Edge IoT is 20% of Seagate's revenue against 11% Client-plus-Consumer at WDC, and small-capacity Edge IoT drives earn about $25.8M per exabyte ($2.425B / 94 EB). On a data-center-only basis Seagate earned $14.08M per nearline exabyte in FY2026 ($9.771B / 694 EB summed quarterly) against WDC's $14.67M ($11.490B Cloud revenue / 783 nearline EB). The definitions differ — Seagate's Data Center includes enterprise and VIA nearline plus systems, WDC's Cloud is an end-market disaggregation — so the 4% gap is indicative only. It is enough to rule out the simple story that "HAMR lets Seagate charge more per terabyte for the same product" on the full-year evidence.

Two different HAMR ratios exist and must not be merged: **HAMR as a share of nearline exabytes** (40% at June 2026, 70% targeted by June 2027) and **Mozaic 4 as a share of HAMR exabytes** (50% targeted by the end of calendar 2026). The CEO answered "we're on target for all the metrics" to a question that blended the two, which does not separate them.

**Against Western Digital.** WDC said on its 2026-08-05 Q4 FY2026 call that it is "on track to ship our 44 terabyte HAMR product in the first half of calendar 2027" and that "we will have 50 terabyte products coming out towards the second half of calendar year 2027" (call; third-party transcript, see appendix). Its official earnings presentation lists only the 40TB-class ePMR ramp. The correct comparison is therefore: **Seagate's HAMR is already in volume production and 44TB Mozaic 4 has shipped since the March 2026 quarter, against WDC's 44TB HAMR in the first half of calendar 2027 (call)** — about one product generation at 44TB. But at the next node the stated schedules converge: Seagate's Mozaic 5 / 50TB qualification shipments start in late calendar 2027, and WDC's 50TB is also slated for the second half of calendar 2027. The lead that matters for valuation is roughly one generation today and, on announced roadmaps, close to zero at 50TB — unless Seagate's 50TB ramps faster once qualified. Any comparison that omits WDC's stated 44TB date overstates the gap by one product generation.

**Capital intensity is rising, not falling.** FY2026 capital expenditure was $569M, 4.7% of revenue (FY2025 $265M, 2.9%), against depreciation and amortization of $276M — a 2.06x ratio. WDC's FY2026 capex was 3.2% of revenue and roughly equal to its D&A. The CFO explained the investment as "additional tools and technology" for HAMR heads and media because "manufacturing cycle time increase" as products move from 3TB to 4TB to 5TB per disk (call). HAMR is "capital efficient" relative to adding drive units, not relative to the ePMR path WDC is still running.

## 2. Industry and Competitive Position <!-- report-module:competition -->

Nearline HDD is a three-player market. Seagate's 10-K names "HDD manufacturers such as Western Digital Corporation and Toshiba Electronics Devices & Storage Corporation along with NAND flash suppliers that provide and enable alternative storage technologies". The first half of that sentence is same-medium share competition; the second is cross-medium substitution by QLC and enterprise SSD.

### Competitive landscape (FY2026; Seagate and WDC fiscal years both ended 2026-07-03)

| Player | Position | FY2026 revenue | FY2026 non-GAAP gross margin | FY2026 exabytes | Technology path | Note |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| Seagate | One of two nearline leaders | $12.195B | 46.1% | 789 EB | HAMR (Mozaic 3+/4+) in volume; Mozaic 5+ late CY2027 | Q1 FY2027 revenue guide $4.1B |
| Western Digital | One of two nearline leaders | $12.919B | 49.1% | 872 EB | 40TB-class ePMR shipping; 44TB HAMR 1H CY2027, 50TB 2H CY2027 (call) | Q1 FY2027 revenue guide $4.1B |
| Toshiba Electronic Devices & Storage | Third HDD maker | Not independently sourced | — | — | — | Not at the scale of the other two |
| SanDisk | Cross-medium (NAND) | $20.248B | — | — | Enterprise SSD | See [sandisk-2026](/invest/research/reports/view.html?id=sandisk-2026) |
| Micron, Kioxia and other NAND/DRAM makers | Cross-medium / hot tier | — | — | — | — | See [micron-2026](/invest/research/reports/view.html?id=micron-2026) and [kioxia-2026](/invest/research/reports/view.html?id=kioxia-2026) |

**The counter-intuitive fact the report has to face.** Western Digital shipped more exabytes (872 versus 789), sold them for less per exabyte on a blended basis ($14.82M versus $15.46M) and still earned a gross margin 3.0 points higher (49.1% versus 46.1%). The arithmetic resolves it: Seagate's cost of revenue per exabyte was **$8.33M against WDC's $7.54M** in FY2026 (about 10% higher), while gross profit per exabyte was nearly identical ($7.13M versus $7.27M). In Q4 the gap narrowed but kept the same shape: cost per exabyte $7.87M versus $7.40M, gross profit per exabyte $8.77M versus $8.82M. **On reported numbers, HAMR is today a price and mix lever, not a cost lever.** Three reasons are visible in the primary sources: (1) Seagate is mid-transition — the CEO said "we have to actually put our factories on pause to go through the product transition" and "there's yield issues" (call); (2) it "pushed maybe PMR a little bit harder than we thought" (call), so it is running two recording technologies at once; (3) its Edge IoT mix is larger. The per-share gap goes the other way: Seagate has about 228M shares against WDC's roughly 365M, so similar absolute profit produces much larger EPS.

**The crossover is guided, not reported.** Q1 FY2027 guidance implies Seagate is about to pass WDC on margin: about 50% non-GAAP operating margin on $4.1B with about $300M of operating expense implies a non-GAAP gross margin of about **57.3%** (an analyst on the call read it as "mid-57%"; the company did not give a gross margin figure), against WDC's guided 55%-56%. On identical $4.1B revenue guides, Seagate's guidance implies about $1.686B of non-GAAP net income ($7.30 x 231M) against WDC's roughly $1.552B ($4.00 x 388M). If Q1 prints that way, it is the first reported quarter in which HAMR shows up as a cost advantage. That is why the gross-margin crossover is a monitoring item (see §9), with a trigger in both directions.

**Market reaction as a revealed preference.** WDC fell 13.0% on 2026-08-06, the day after its Q4 call ($519.17 to $451.52), while Seagate rose 1.8% ($837.66 to $852.95); Seagate itself rose 2.3% on 2026-07-29 after its own release ($747.30 to $764.43). At the 2026-09-22 closes, Seagate trades at 31.50x guidance-annualized EPS against WDC's 29.04x and at 17.38x FY2026 EV/Sales against WDC's 13.09x. The HAMR premium is priced mostly in the sales multiple, less in the earnings multiple, because Seagate's per-share earnings are already higher.

**Cross-medium substitution: not visible in FY2026.** If QLC enterprise SSDs were eroding the capacity tier, Seagate's nearline exabyte growth would lag or revenue per exabyte would be forced down. Neither happened: nearline exabytes grew 42.3% Y/Y in Q4 (137 to 195) and Q4 blended revenue per exabyte rose 10.3%. Over the same period SanDisk's datacenter revenue grew sharply (sandisk-2026), so the hot tier and the capacity tier rose together. Seagate itself argues the tiers are complementary, citing a white paper with SK hynix on spilling KV cache from memory to SSD to HDD for inference workloads (call); that is a vendor claim and is not treated as evidence here.

**Moat assessment, both sides.** In favor: new entrants in nearline HDD effectively do not exist; HAMR required years of photonics, media and laser integration that only Seagate has in volume; and the three-player structure has held supply discipline since FY2024. Against: the moat protects "no fourth player", not "no price competition" — FY2024 showed what happens when demand falls; WDC's own HAMR and 50TB dates are within the same calendar year as Seagate's Mozaic 5; and customer concentration is rising (one customer at 14% of revenue in FY2026 against none above 10% in FY2024). Unlike WDC, Seagate's 10-K does not disclose its top-ten customer share, so the degree of concentration beyond the single 14% customer is **unknown**.

## 5. Bull Case <!-- report-module:bullBear -->

1. **Areal density is working as a supply lever, and nobody else has it in volume yet.** FY2026 exabytes grew 32.6% (nearline 39.8%) with flat drive units and heads-and-disks up 15%-20% (CFO, call). HAMR reached about 40% of nearline exabytes exiting June 2026, Mozaic 3+ is in production at every major cloud customer, and Mozaic 4 (44TB) is ramping at the two largest. WDC's 44TB HAMR is a first-half-2027 event (WDC call). For roughly four quarters, Seagate is the only supplier able to add exabytes by density at 44TB.

2. **The cost crossover is guided for the next quarter.** Q1 FY2027 guidance implies a gross margin near 57.3% against WDC's guided 55%-56%, on identical $4.1B revenue. If Mozaic 4 mix does what the CEO and CFO describe ("moving the mix from 3 terabyte to 4 terabyte per disk is, of course, giving us another boost in term of profitability", call), FY2026's cost-per-exabyte disadvantage ($8.33M versus $7.54M) reverses in FY2027 — and the market's HAMR premium becomes earned.

3. **Demand is allocated and priced ahead.** "The vast majority of our nearline exabytes are now allocated into calendar 2028", customers are extending planning horizons "through 2029 and beyond", and build-to-order contracts "define both product configuration and pricing terms, covering the entirety of calendar 2027" (CEO, call). Price per exabyte was up 10.3% Y/Y in Q4 on this report's calculation, and the CEO says customers are "signing up to pricing that is even higher than some of the contractual stuff that we had done together" (call).

4. **The balance sheet is done and dilution is closed.** Net leverage is 0.4x, about $2.6B of debt has been retired between the start of FY2026 and 2026-09-08 ($1.4B in FY2026, $1.0B in July, $186M of exchangeable notes by September), the 8%-plus coupons are gone, and no convertible instrument remains after 2026-09-08. From here, per-share growth equals net-income growth less stock compensation, plus whatever $4.8B of authorization buys back.

5. **Operating leverage is extreme and visible.** Operating expense has been $286M-$296M for five quarters; Q4 incremental non-GAAP operating margin was 83% Y/Y ($979M of operating income on $1.185B of revenue growth). Q1 guidance extends this: about $300M of operating expense on $4.1B of revenue.

6. **A shallower trough than the peer.** Seagate's FY2024 GAAP operating margin was +6.9% against WDC's -6.4%. The through-cycle earnings floor is higher than the paired WDC report can claim.

**Valuation implication of the bull case:** in the §8 grid, the bull scenario — 1,578 EB (26% CAGR, the upper end of management's mid-20% nearline target), $18.0M revenue per exabyte, 60% gross margin, $1.5B operating expense, 222M shares — gives FY2029 EPS of $58.43 and, at a 20x exit, **$1,169 per share**: a total return of +28.0% including dividends, **+9.3% annualized**. That clears an 8% hurdle by 1.3 points and does not reach 10%. The bull case needs management's plan to be hit in full and a 20x multiple three years out, and it still only just pays.

## 6. Bear Case <!-- report-module:bullBear -->

1. **The HAMR premium is being paid before the HAMR cost advantage exists.** On FY2026 figures Seagate's cost per exabyte was about 10% above WDC's, and gross profit per exabyte was the same ($7.13M versus $7.27M; Q4 $8.77M versus $8.82M). Its higher blended price is mostly Edge IoT mix: on a data-center-only basis it earned $14.08M per nearline exabyte against WDC's $14.67M. The market pays 31.5x forward earnings and 17.4x sales for a cost curve that is guided, not yet reported.

2. **The lead narrows at the next node.** On stated schedules, Seagate's Mozaic 5 / 50TB qualification shipments begin in late calendar 2027 and WDC's 50TB products arrive in the second half of calendar 2027 (WDC call). If WDC's HAMR qualifies on time, the one-generation lead at 44TB could be a same-year race at 50TB — just as this report's valuation horizon runs out.

3. **Visibility is a claim, not a disclosure.** "Allocated into calendar 2028" comes from the earnings call. In the 10-K, Seagate applies the optional exemption and does not disclose remaining performance obligations because "all non-cancellable purchase orders are less than one year in duration". That is a different disclosure from WDC's "not material", but it leaves the same gap against SanDisk's $59.8B of NBM remaining performance obligations (sandisk-2026). The long-term agreements' volumes, tenors and pricing floors are not quantified anywhere this report can audit.

4. **Capital intensity is rising into the HAMR ramp.** Capex doubled to $569M (4.7% of revenue, 2.06x D&A) and the 10-K says FY2027 will be higher still, within 4%-6%. At 6% of Q1-guided annualized revenue ($16.4B) that would be about $980M. The "capital-efficient" framing is relative to adding units, not relative to a peer extending ePMR at 3.2%.

5. **Customer concentration is rising and less disclosed.** One customer was 14% of FY2026 revenue (10% in FY2025, none above 10% in FY2024), and unlike WDC, Seagate does not disclose its top-ten share. The Mozaic 4 ramp is concentrated at "the two largest global CSPs" (supplemental). One hyperscaler shifting a build by two quarters lands directly in the numbers.

6. **Part of FY2026 cash flow is deferred tax.** Non-current income tax payable rose from $1M to $436M; the $528M working-capital inflow from accrued expenses and taxes is not recurring cash. Adjusted for it, the FCF yield at $919.84 is about 1.27%.

7. **This is still a cycle.** Gross margin moved from 23.4% to 52.7% in eight quarters. Pricing actions, not only mix, drove FY2026's 11-point gross-margin gain ("primarily driven by pricing actions undertaken by the Company and product mix shift", 10-K). Pricing that rose on a supply shortage can fall when the shortage ends — and HAMR itself adds supply.

**Valuation implication of the bear case:** the bear scenario — 1,200 EB (15% CAGR), revenue per exabyte back to $13.5M, 42% gross margin, $1.3B operating expense, 230M shares — gives FY2029 EPS of $19.32 and, at 14x, **$270 per share**: -69.6% including dividends, -35.0% annualized. It still assumes FY2029 revenue of $16.20B, 32.8% above FY2026, with a gross margin between FY2025's 35.2% and FY2026's 45.6%. It is not a replay of FY2024.

## 7. Key Uncertainties and Thesis-Breaking Conditions <!-- report-module:uncertainties -->

**The core uncertainty is not whether HAMR works — it does — but whether it becomes a cost advantage before the price cycle turns, and what multiple the market pays when it does.** By §8, earning 8% annualized from $919.84 on the base-case FY2029 EPS of $40.73 requires the market to still pay **27.7x** in mid-2029.

The unknowns, each with a confirmation point:

1. **Does the cost crossover print?** Known: FY2026 cost per exabyte $8.33M against WDC's $7.54M; Q1 FY2027 guidance implies about 57.3% gross margin. Unknown: whether Mozaic 4 yields deliver it. Confirmation: Q1 FY2027 results (October 2026).

2. **HAMR penetration.** Known: about 40% of nearline exabytes at June 2026 (call). Unknown: whether the 70% target for June 2027 and the 50% Mozaic 4 share of HAMR exabytes by end-2026 hold; the CEO conceded PMR was pushed "a little bit harder than we thought". Confirmation: the January 2027 and July 2027 calls.

3. **Revenue per exabyte.** Known: Q4 $16.65M, +10.3% Y/Y; FY2026 $15.46M. Unknown: how much is shortage pricing versus mix. Confirmation: Q1 and Q2 FY2027 revenue divided by exabytes.

4. **Visibility.** Known: "allocated into CY28" (call); remaining performance obligations not disclosed (exemption). Unknown: tenor and enforceability. Confirmation: any 10-Q quantifying long-term agreements, or management softening the "allocated" language.

5. **WDC's HAMR timetable.** Known: 44TB 1H CY2027, 50TB 2H CY2027 (WDC call). Unknown: whether it holds. Confirmation: WDC qualification milestones in 2027.

6. **The exact share count.** Known: 226,644,518 at 2026-07-31 plus 1,647,862 on 2026-09-08. Unknown: shares from the August settlement and September vesting. Confirmation: the Q1 FY2027 10-Q cover.

**Thesis-breaking conditions (any one moves the stance to bearish-avoid):**
- Nearline exabytes decline sequentially for two consecutive quarters, or FY2027 nearline exabyte growth falls below 15%
- Blended revenue per exabyte falls below $15.5M for two consecutive quarters
- Q1 FY2027 non-GAAP operating margin comes in below 48%, or non-GAAP gross margin declines sequentially
- The June 2027 target of 70% of nearline exabytes on HAMR is missed or withdrawn, Mozaic 4 is below 50% of HAMR exabytes exiting calendar 2026, or Mozaic 5 qualification slips beyond calendar 2027
- Management drops the "allocated into calendar 2028" framing, discloses order push-outs or cancellation charges, or a single customer exceeds 20% of revenue

**Upgrade conditions (to neutral-watch or constructive):**
- Two consecutive quarters of non-GAAP gross margin at or above 57% with revenue per exabyte at or above $17.0M and nearline exabyte growth at or above 25%
- HAMR reaches 70% of nearline exabytes by June 2027 with Mozaic 5 qualification shipments on schedule
- Or a price decline to around $650, where the base scenario's 16x exit value sits, without a change in fundamentals

## 10. Conclusion <!-- report-module:conclusion -->

**(1) The question this report answers on the AI infrastructure chain.** Seagate carries the `common-constraint` role in the memory-storage layer. The shared constraint is nearline exabyte supply: Seagate says the vast majority of its nearline exabytes are allocated into calendar 2028, drive units are flat, and the only way to add exabytes is more data per disk. Whether hyperscalers, neoclouds and model developers can keep expanding their capacity tier therefore depends on whether HAMR can carry that supply curve — a constraint that cuts across the demand, cloud and memory-storage layers, not a single-company question. The layer's `dashboard` (micron-2026) tracks memory pricing, and WDC's `architecture-check` tests HDD against SSD; neither measures whether the capacity tier's supply can grow by density. FY2026's answer is **yes, so far, at a rising capital cost**: exabytes +32.6% and nearline +39.8% on flat units, with capex doubling to 4.7% of revenue. The answer holds for FY2026 only.

**(2) The expectation gap.** At $919.84 the market pays 31.5x guidance-annualized EPS; a 12-15x through-cycle multiple needs sustainable EPS of $76.65 to $61.32, 2.63x to 2.10x the run rate. Our base case reaches $40.73 by FY2029, 1.39x. Earning 8% on that path needs a 27.7x multiple in mid-2029.

**(3) Stance and confidence.** The 30 / 50 / 20 grid weights to $730.52; with $8.88 of dividends that is 19.6% below $919.84, or **-7.6% annualized**, 15.6 to 17.6 points below an 8%-10% required return. Lifting every exit multiple by 4x gives -0.5%; adding higher revenue per exabyte and margins as well gives +5.2%. The skew is negative even with the bull tilt, but the bull scenario returns +9.3% and the bear requires gross margin to fall from 52.7% to 42%, so the stance is **cautious / medium conviction**, not bearish-avoid. Confidence is medium because the decisive variables — the exit multiple and a cost crossover that is guided but not reported — are not yet observations.

**(4) Upgrade and downgrade triggers.** Upgrade to neutral-watch or constructive: two quarters of non-GAAP gross margin at or above 57% with revenue per exabyte at or above $17.0M and nearline exabyte growth at or above 25%; HAMR at 70% of nearline exabytes by June 2027 with Mozaic 5 on schedule; or a price near $650 without a change in fundamentals. Downgrade to bearish-avoid: two sequential declines in nearline exabytes or FY2027 growth below 15%; revenue per exabyte below $15.5M for two quarters; Q1 FY2027 operating margin below 48% or a sequential gross-margin decline; a missed HAMR milestone or Mozaic 5 slipping beyond CY2027; or loss of the allocation framing or one customer above 20%.

Seagate has the better technology position in nearline HDD and a finished balance-sheet repair. But the HAMR lead is today a price-and-mix advantage, not yet a cost advantage; it is about one generation wide at 44TB and narrows at 50TB; and the market is already paying for the cost advantage to arrive and last.
