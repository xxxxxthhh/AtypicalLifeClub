## Executive Summary <!-- report-module:overview -->

**One-line thesis:** Zhongji Innolight is the world's largest shipper of high-speed pluggable optical modules, and its first half of 2026 proved that AI cluster expansion really does reach the physical link layer — units +109.8%, derived blended ASP +36.6%, and module gross margin at 46.59%, three curves rising at once. But roughly half of that result comes from product-mix uplift and an all-time-high gross margin rather than durable pricing power, so the 52.3x trailing P/E implied by the RMB 908.90 close is a bet that the margin curve does not revert.

**Investment judgment:** **Neutral-watch / medium conviction.** At the **RMB 908.90** Shenzhen close on 2026-09-09, 1,177,909,641 shares outstanding give a market capitalization of **RMB 1,070.60B** (about **USD 158.0B** at the 2026-09-09 CFETS USD/CNY central parity of 6.7769). Trailing-twelve-month (2025Q3–2026Q2) revenue is RMB 65.229B and parent net profit RMB 20.453B, for **52.3x TTM P/E and 16.4x TTM P/S**; on an H1-annualized basis **39.2x**, on a Q2-annualized basis **33.8x**. Reverse test: exiting in 3.5 years (end-2029) at a 25x multiple with an 8%–10% required return needs parent net profit to compound **17.7%–19.9%** from the Q2-annualized RMB 31.667B. My base case is **about 15%** (revenue compounding 25%, net margin easing from 32.68% to 27%). The expectation gap is mildly negative but small, and its entire content sits inside the gross-margin normalization assumption — which is why the stance is neutral rather than cautious.

**Chain job:** This report is the `optical` layer's **architecture check**. Coherent already carries the layer's order-velocity dashboard on a quarterly cadence; Innolight reports semi-annually, so its comparative advantage is not timeliness but that it is the only report in the book that discloses **pluggable-module capacity, output and unit sales** — allowing a blended ASP to be derived — alongside a complete pluggable / XPO / NPO / CPO / OCS roadmap. The question it answers is: **as scale-up begins to demand roughly ten times the optical bandwidth, is the pluggable path still winning?** — not a second reading of buildout speed.

## 1. Business Overview and Chain Role <!-- report-module:business -->

The company describes itself as "a leading global provider of optical interconnect solutions"; in substance it is a single-product business. Optical transceiver modules generated **RMB 41.355B** in the first half of 2026, or **99.0%** of revenue; the remainder — TO-CAN packages, optical sub-assemblies (OSA) and fiber-to-the-vehicle (FTTV) automotive optics — totalled **RMB 422.7M** (+18.3%). The product line spans 100G to 1.6T in OSFP, QSFP-DD and coherent ZR/ZR+ form factors. In the first half, **1.6T silicon-photonics modules entered volume ramp with shipments climbing quarter by quarter**, and the company calls them the core driver of revenue growth — but the interim report **does not disclose 1.6T revenue or its share of the mix**.

## 2. Industry and Competitive Position <!-- report-module:competition -->

The demand anchor is hyperscaler capital expenditure. Citing the companies' own filings, Innolight reports that in **Q2 2026 Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and Google spent a combined USD 164.9B on capex, up about 86% year over year**; in Q1 2026 Alibaba, Tencent and Baidu spent a combined RMB 64.7B, up about 18%. Third-party sizing (CIC) puts the global optical-interconnect market at **USD 24.8B in 2025** rising to **USD 111.0B by 2030**, a **31.6%** CAGR over 2026–2030, with the datacom portion going from USD 19.4B to USD 98.6B at a **33.8%** CAGR, and silicon-photonics penetration of datacom optical interconnect rising from about **38%** in 2025 to about **73%** in 2030. These are sell-side-style third-party forecasts, not realized data; this report treats them only as a ceiling reference.

The moat is three layers stacked: **forward-looking R&D** (first to ship silicon-photonics 400G, 800G and 1.6T modules, first to develop and apply coherent-Lite, founding participant in both the XPO MSA and the Open CPX MSA); **customer qualification cycles** (cloud buyers require supplier qualification plus product-code certification); and **volume manufacturing with yield** (capacity roughly doubled in a half year while gross margin still reached 46.59%). The other side is equally clear: this remains build-to-order component manufacturing, the end customers are a handful of cloud providers designing their own ASICs and network architectures, and above 200G the DSP, driver and TIA electrical chips still come mainly from overseas suppliers.

## 5. Bull Case <!-- report-module:bullBear -->

**Core: three independent pieces of demand-side evidence point the same way, and the supply side has already expanded ahead of them.**

1. **Volume is ramping, not just price.** First-half unit sales were 18.99M, up 109.8%, and half-year capacity of 22.15M units already equals 79% of the whole of 2025's 28.06M. This is direct evidence of pluggable modules being consumed inside AI clusters — not an order-book figure.
2. **Share is concentrating.** Over the same period Eoptolink grew 100.34% and T&S 15.15%, against Innolight's 182.49%. Nearly a two-fold growth gap under identical demand conditions says high-end 800G/1.6T share is concentrating at the top, and CIC's "first for five consecutive years" still holds through this acceleration.
3. **The company has bet on both architecture paths.** It was first to ship silicon-photonics 400G, 800G and 1.6T modules and first to develop coherent-Lite, and it is a founding participant in both the XPO MSA and the Open CPX MSA. At OFC 2026 it demonstrated a 12.8T 8xDR8 XPO module, a 6.4T 4xDR8 NPO module and a TFLN MZM-based 1.6T OSFP 2xLR4. Even if scale-up eventually migrates to NPO/CPO, the company sits on the standards-setting side.
4. **The balance sheet was just re-armed.** Pro-forma net cash is RMB 60.468B, or 5.6% of market capitalization, with 65% of the raise earmarked for R&D and global capacity and none of it drawn as of the interim report — capacity expansion needs no external leverage.

**Upside valuation implication (not a price target):** if revenue compounds about 32% from the Q2-annualized RMB 89.13B through 2029 (just below CIC's 33.8% datacom market CAGR) and net margin holds at 30%, 2029 parent net profit would be about RMB 70.6B; at a 28x exit multiple that is about RMB 1,976.8B of equity value, roughly **+19% annualized** over 3.5 years against RMB 1,070.6B today. This scenario requires gross margin not to revert and is the most assumption-sensitive of the three.

## 6. Bear Case <!-- report-module:bullBear -->

**Core: this is a cycle-peak income statement priced at growth-stock multiples, with margin, customer structure and geopolitics all fragile at once.**

1. **Net margin sits at a high after doubling in three years.** Net margin was 20.28% / 21.67% / 28.24% / **32.68%** in 2023 / 2024 / 2025 / 2026 H1, and module gross margin of 46.59% is the highest on disclosed record. This is a product of supply tightness, not an efficiency revolution. A return to full-year 2025's 42.61% is a 3.98-point gross-margin gap, which at the period's roughly 15% effective tax rate cuts net margin by about 3-4 points all else equal; a return to 2025 H1's 39.96% is a 6.63-point gap, or roughly 5-6 points of net margin - at 52.3x trailing earnings, that is a de-rating in reverse.
2. **The ASP contribution disappears first.** Some +36.6% of the revenue growth came from mix. Once 1.6T is the bulk of shipments there is no higher price tier left to mix into, while 800G enters annual price erosion. This is **a question of timing, not of probability**; only the pace is unknown.
3. **Customer concentration is near its limit.** The top five customers were **75.98%** of 2025 revenue (largest 24.06%), and the top five were **72.49%** of receivables and contract assets at 2026-06-30 (largest 20.86%). The company does not name customers. An architecture change or supplier switch at any one of them would rewrite the volume curve directly — precisely what the "customer concentration" clause of the `chip-vs-downstream` rule is meant to catch.
4. **Cash conversion of 13.2%.** First-half operating cash flow was RMB 1.800B against RMB 13.651B of parent net profit. Receivable days (about 65) and inventory days (about 160) did not deteriorate, so this is growth-financed working capital rather than a collapse in collection quality — but paying 52x earnings for profit that converts to cash at 13% leaves thin tolerance.
5. **The geopolitical leg has already been touched.** On 2026-06-08 (US time) the US Department of War added the company to the "Section 1260H List"; on 2026-06-10 the company said the designation is inconsistent with the facts and has had no material effect on operations. Section 1260H is a phased federal-procurement restriction list — not the Entity List and not an export ban — but with 94.8% of revenue overseas and 16.85M units sold abroad, any escalation toward the Entity List, export controls or tariffs would hit the revenue base directly.

**Downside valuation implication (not a price target):** if 2027–2028 brings a hyperscaler capex digestion phase while NPO/CPO takes part of the scale-up socket, revenue compounds only 8% from RMB 89.13B through 2029 and net margin falls to 18%, 2029 parent net profit would be about RMB 21.0B; at a 16x exit multiple that is about RMB 335.7B of equity value, roughly **-69% cumulative**. This scenario is conjunctive — growth, margin and multiple must all break together — so it is not the likely case, but if it happens the loss is permanent rather than a drawdown.

**What is priced in, and the expectation gap:**

The reverse arithmetic: starting from RMB 1,070.60B of market capitalization on 2026-09-09, an investor demanding 8%–10% annually and holding to end-2029 (3.5 years) needs an exit equity value of RMB 1,401.6–1,494.5B. Converting that into required 2029 parent net profit at different exit multiples and comparing with the Q2-annualized RMB 31.667B gives the market-implied net-profit CAGR: **exiting at 20x requires 25.5%–27.8%; at 25x requires 17.7%–19.9%; at 30x requires 11.8%–13.8%.** Taking a sensible center for an optical-module leader — a 25x exit at an 8%–10% required return — **today's price implies roughly 18%–20% annual compounding in parent net profit**.

My base case is **about 15%** (revenue compounding 25% with net margin easing from 32.68% to 27%, i.e. RMB 31.667B growing to about RMB 52.5B). **The expectation gap is mildly negative, but only 3–5 percentage points wide, and the entire width of that gap is the slope of gross-margin normalization — a variable with exactly one half-year observation, taken at its all-time high.** Put differently, the market is not paying an absurd price for Innolight (the implied growth rate is below CIC's own 31.6%–33.8% forecast for the industry), but it does require the company to keep compounding from a peak margin for the better part of five years. That is the whole reason this report is neutral rather than either bullish or cautious.

## 10. Conclusion <!-- report-module:conclusion -->

**(1) Chain-validation job.** In the `optical` layer this report carries the **architecture check**: not a second reading of AI buildout velocity — Coherent already does that quarterly — but a test, using the only **pluggable-module capacity/output/unit-sales** disclosure in the book and the blended ASP derived from it, of whether the pluggable path is still winning beyond scale-out as scale-up starts demanding roughly ten times the bandwidth. The first half of 2026 answers yes without ambiguity: units +109.8%, blended ASP +36.6%, gross margin 46.59% — three curves rising together. For the `chip-vs-downstream` rule this is a **positive confirmation** from the optical layer: GPU/ASIC acceleration did reach the physical link layer, so the rule's "not confirmed downstream" antecedent does not hold this period.

**(2) Expectation gap.** At RMB 908.90 (52.3x TTM, 39.2x H1-annualized, 33.8x Q2-annualized), a 25x exit at an 8%–10% required return implies roughly **18%–20%** annual parent-net-profit compounding. My base case is **about 15%**. A mildly negative gap, 3–5 percentage points wide.

**(3) Stance and conviction.** **Neutral-watch / medium conviction.** The scenario grid is 30% bull / 40% base / 30% bear: the bull case requires a 30% net margin to hold for another three and a half years, the base case lands right at the bottom edge of the required return, and the bear case needs growth, margin and multiple to break together — unlikely, but permanently loss-making if it happens. The probability-weighted skew is near zero rather than positive, so this is not a bullish call; but the 3–5 point negative gap sits entirely inside the gross-margin normalization slope, a variable with a single half-year observation, and treating that as established negative evidence would run ahead of the evidence — so it is not a cautious call either. Conviction is medium: the fact base is unusually complete (volume, price, gross margin, customer concentration and cash conversion are all disclosed first-hand by the issuer); what is uncertain is the extrapolation, not the evidence.

**(4) Upgrade / downgrade triggers.** **Upgrade to constructive** if the 2026 annual report confirms module gross margin still above 44% with unit sales still roughly doubling year over year, operating cash flow returns to above 50% of parent net profit, top-five customer concentration stops rising, and Section 1260H does not escalate. **Downgrade to cautious** if module gross margin falls below 40% (or consolidated gross margin in the Q3 report drops more than three points sequentially), half-year unit-sales growth falls below 30% year over year, operating cash flow stays below 30% of parent net profit for two consecutive periods, or Section 1260H escalates to the Entity List or export controls, or any customer above 10% of revenue explicitly reallocates supply share.
