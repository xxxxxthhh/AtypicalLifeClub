With this report no layer in the book's schema is empty. The queue label that commissioned it asks a specific question: advanced packaging is named again and again as the binding constraint on AI accelerator supply - but is that constraint at the OSAT layer where Amkor sits, or is it entirely inside TSMC's CoWoS/3DFabric capacity? ## 1. Business Overview

Amkor Technology is an OSAT - an outsourced semiconductor assembly and test provider. It does not design chips and does not fabricate wafers. It takes finished wafers from its customers or their foundries, bumps and probes them, cuts them into die, encases those die in a package that connects them to a board, tests the result, and ships it. It is the step between the fab and the board, and it is sold as a service on a purchase-order basis.

The company describes itself as "the world's largest U.S. headquartered OSAT." It employed 30,800 people at 2025 year-end, of whom approximately 95% are in the Asia-Pacific region, 4% in Europe and 1% in the United States, spanning 12 countries. Manufacturing sits in China, Japan, Korea, Malaysia, the Philippines, Portugal, Singapore, Taiwan and Vietnam, with the Arizona Facility under construction in the United States. Executive offices are in Tempe, Arizona and in Singapore. Amkor reports as a **single operating segment**.

**Revenue structure.** The company gives two cuts of revenue and no segment P&L. Both matter, and one of them is routinely misread.

| Cut | Q2 2026 | Q1 2026 | Q2 2025 | What it actually means |
|-----|---------|---------|---------|------------------------|
| Advanced products | $1,557M (82.0%) | $1,372M (81.4%) | $1,228M (81.3%) | The filing defines this as **"flip chip, memory and wafer-level processing and related test services"** - a bucket dominated by smartphone flip chip, not by AI packaging. See §3 |
| Mainstream products | $341M (18.0%) | $313M (18.6%) | $283M (18.7%) | "All other wirebond packaging and related test services" |
| Packaging services | 88% | 89% | 88% | The core business |
| Test services | 12% | 11% | 12% | The attached, higher-margin service |

**End markets.** This is the cut that tells you what Amkor really is.

| End market | Q2 2026 | Q1 2026 | Q2 2025 | FY2025 | FY2024 | FY2023 |
|------------|---------|---------|---------|--------|--------|--------|
| Communications (smartphones, tablets) | 42% | 44% | 40% | 46% | 48% | 50% |
| Computing (data center, infrastructure, PC/laptop, storage) | 22% | 21% | 22% | 20% | 19% | 16% |
| Automotive, industrial and other | 22% | 21% | 20% | 19% | 18% | 21% |
| Consumer (AR & gaming, connected home, wearables) | 14% | 14% | 18% | 15% | 15% | 13% |

Three structural points frame everything below.

**First, this is a smartphone company with an AI option attached, not the reverse.** Communications was 42% of Q2 revenue and has been the largest end market in every period shown. Computing - which the company defines to include *PC/laptop and storage as well as data center and infrastructure* - was 22%. AI data-center packaging is a subset of a subset: it is plausibly under 15% of total revenue, and Amkor does not break it out. Any reading of this company as an AI pure-play is a reading the disclosure does not support.

**Second, the economics are subcontractor economics.** Q2 2026 gross margin of 16.8% was a multi-year high and is the best number in this report. Set it against TSMC's 67.7% in the same quarter (per `tsmc-2026`). Cost of sales in Q2 was 52.6% materials, 10.0% labour, 8.6% depreciation and 12.0% other manufacturing - more than half of revenue is pass-through material, and the incremental margin comes almost entirely from loading fixed assets harder. The company states plainly that it has "high fixed costs and the absence of any material backlog," that customers "do not commit to purchase any significant amount of packaging or test services or provide us with binding forecasts," and that it "sometimes experience[s] double booking by customers."

**Third, the customer list is short, and the top of it is one company.** Direct sales to **Apple were 29.8%** and to **Qualcomm 11.1%** of FY2025 net sales; the ten largest customers were 72%. That concentration has been *improving* - top ten fell to 68% in Q1 2026 and 66% in Q2 2026 - and that improvement is a genuine datapoint for the bull case, driven by computing and automotive growing faster than communications. But a single customer at roughly 30% of sales in a business with no backlog and no binding commitments is the most load-bearing disclosure Amkor makes.

## 2. Industry & Competitive Position

The OSAT industry exists because packaging and test are capital-intensive, cyclical and increasingly specialized, and because integrated device manufacturers cannot keep such assets loaded across a cycle. It is consolidated at the top and structurally low-margin.

| Competitor | Scale and overlap | Read-through to this coverage |
|------------|-------------------|-------------------------------|
| ASE Technology (ASX) | The clear #1 OSAT, roughly 2.5-3x Amkor's revenue on third-party estimates; competes across the full range including advanced packaging and test | The alternative source for every socket Amkor holds, including Apple's. Not in this coverage |
| **TSMC (2330.TW / TSM)** | Not an OSAT, but the owner of CoWoS and 3DFabric - the advanced packaging that AI accelerators actually queue for, sold bundled with the wafer | `tsmc-2026`. **The competitor that matters most**, and the reason §3 exists |
| JCET | The largest Chinese OSAT, roughly $5B revenue on third-party estimates, growing materially faster than the market | The price-competition and share-shift vector, and the reason Amkor's China exposure carries export-control risk its non-US competitors may not |
| Powertech, TFME, ChipMOS, Hana Micron | Second-tier and specialist OSATs, strong in memory and test | Where mainstream wirebond pricing is set |
| IDM in-house capacity | Intel (Foveros/EMIB), Samsung, and memory makers package much of their own output | Structural ceiling on the outsourcing rate; the filing names "the internal capabilities of integrated device manufacturers" as a competitor |
| Substrate suppliers (Ibiden, Shinko, AT&S) | Upstream of Amkor; FCBGA substrate capacity has been its own periodic constraint | Not covered here; flagged because substrate scarcity is often what people mean when they say "packaging bottleneck" |

**Where Amkor's position is genuinely strong.** It is the largest US-headquartered OSAT at a moment when US-headquartered matters more than it has in thirty years. It has a real technology portfolio - high-density fan-out (HDFO, including SWIFT and S-Connect), 2.5D integration with silicon interposers, advanced flip chip, fine-pitch bumping, wafer-level processing and advanced system-in-package. It has a geographically diverse footprint across nine countries that lets customers dual-source across sites, which is exactly what customers are now paying for. And it has decades-long relationships with Apple, Qualcomm and the automotive semiconductor base.

**Where it is not strong.** It is the number two in a market whose number one is 2.5-3x its size and whose most valuable segment - AI accelerator packaging - is controlled by a foundry that does not sell packaging separately. Its gross margin at a cyclical high is 16.8%. It has no backlog. Its pricing has faced, in the filing's own words, "historical downward pressure." And its largest customer can move volume to ASE at the next product generation without breaching anything, because there is nothing to breach.

**The FX and geography tail.** Approximately 90% of FY2025 net sales were denominated in US dollars but only ~60% of cost of sales and operating expenses were; a 10% appreciation of foreign currencies against the dollar would have reduced FY2025 operating income by approximately **$163 million**, against actual FY2025 operating income of $467.4M. That is a 35% swing from a currency move, and it is a permanent feature of an Asian cost base with dollar pricing.

## 3. Where Is the Advanced-Packaging Bottleneck?

This is the question the coverage queue commissioned, it is the reason the `advanced-packaging` layer exists in the book's schema, and it deserves a direct answer. The answer this report reaches is: **the AI advanced-packaging bottleneck is overwhelmingly TSMC's, not Amkor's. Amkor is a downstream beneficiary of a rising tide rather than a gate of its own - with one narrow, real and separately-priced exception: US-located advanced packaging capacity, which is genuinely scarce and which customers are now paying cash in advance to reserve.**

Those are two different claims and both need their evidence.

### 3.1 The claim that Amkor is not the gate

| Test | Evidence | Verdict |
|------|----------|---------|
| Is the "advanced packaging" number actually AI packaging? | The Q2 release defines advanced products as "flip chip, memory and wafer-level processing and related test services" - 82.0% of revenue. But communications is 42% of revenue and computing (which *includes PC/laptop and storage*) is 22%. The 82% is mostly smartphone flip chip | **No.** 82% "advanced" is a definitional artefact, not an AI reading |
| Is the network running out of capacity? | Management said on the Q2 call that factory utilization improved "from the 50% range into the 70% range," with "several technology platforms running at full capacity" - none of which were named | **No.** A network at 70% is not a gate. Some platform-level tightness exists but is undisclosed |
| Is there a queue? | The filings state Amkor's business "does not typically operate with any material backlog," that customers do not provide binding forecasts, and that double booking occurs | **No.** A gate has a queue; Amkor discloses it has none |
| Is demand outrunning supply *this quarter*? | Q3 2026 revenue is guided to $1.95-2.05B against $1,987.0M actual in Q3 2025: **-1.9% to +3.2% year over year** | **No.** Binding constraints do not guide flat |
| Who owns the AI packaging that is actually scarce? | CoWoS and 3DFabric are TSMC's, sold bundled with the wafer. `tsmc-2026` carries "advanced packaging is a second bottleneck" for the foundry layer and notes that TSMC's own Q2 2026 release, presentation and management report **do not quantify CoWoS scheduling** | **TSMC.** The gate is one layer up and it is not sold separately |

### 3.2 The inversion: Amkor is being gated *by* another layer

The most instructive fact in the Q3 guide is not that it is soft. It is *why* it is soft. Management attributed the coming decline in communications revenue to memory supply constraints, with Android smartphone volumes already down roughly 20% on that basis, alongside manufacturing transitions.

Read that plainly: **the binding constraint visible in Amkor's own guidance is memory, not packaging.** Amkor has the packaging capacity; its customers cannot get the DRAM to build the phones. That is a direct, same-quarter cross-check with `micron-2026` and `sk-hynix-2026`, whose layer this book has already scored as genuinely gating, and it is the cleanest available evidence that the OSAT layer sits downstream of the constraint rather than at it. A company that is itself the bottleneck does not guide revenue down because someone else's component is short.

### 3.3 The narrow exception that is real: US-located capacity

Having said all of that, it would be wrong to flatten this to "Amkor is irrelevant overflow." Something genuinely scarce is being priced here, and it is not global packaging capacity - it is **domestic** packaging capacity, and the evidence is customers paying cash years in advance.

| Evidence | Detail | Source quality |
|----------|--------|----------------|
| NVIDIA prepayment | The 10-Q filed 2026-07-28 states that in July 2026 Amkor "separately entered into an advance payment agreement with a customer pursuant to which we expect to receive approximately **$1.5 billion in 2027**." Coverage of the July 23, 2026 announcement identifies the counterparty as NVIDIA, in a multi-year advanced packaging and development partnership | **Amount and timing: primary (10-Q), counterparty anonymous in the filing.** Counterparty identity: press release and coverage |
| The prepayment is for US services specifically | On the Q2 call the CEO described it as a prepayment "received in 2027 and return[ed] back to the customer as we provide the services in the U.S." | Earnings-call coverage |
| Other advance payments | The 10-Q also discloses ~$300M of advance payments expected over two years, all requiring standby letters of credit, of which **$100.0M was received in July 2026** | Primary (10-Q) |
| Phase 1 is spoken for | Management said on the Q2 call that "our projections now show phase 1 as fully committed" - before a single wafer has been packaged there | Earnings-call coverage |
| Apple as anchor | Apple is described as Amkor's "first and largest customer" for the Arizona campus, packaging Apple silicon from TSMC's nearby Arizona fabs | Company announcement and coverage |

That is a real constraint with a real price. But note carefully what kind of constraint it is: **contractual and geographic, not technological.** Nobody is short of the ability to package a chip; they are short of the ability to package it inside the United States, next to a TSMC Arizona fab, under a supply chain that survives a Taiwan contingency. That scarcity was created by policy and geopolitics, not by physics, and it is being resolved by a $7 billion capital program that will take until 2028 to produce.

It also does not flatter the near-term economics. The $1.5B prepayment is a **contract liability repaid in services**, not equity and not revenue; it reduces the funding risk of the build without reducing the enterprise value or adding a dollar of profit.

### 3.4 Therefore

The `advanced-packaging` layer's value to this book is an **architecture read, not a constraint read.** It tells us whether AI packaging stays captive inside the foundry - where TSMC sells it bundled and where the queue actually forms - or whether a merchant OSAT can hold a durable, separately-priced share of it, and whether onshoring changes that answer. That is exactly what the `architecture-check` role is for, and it is why the role was chosen over `common-constraint`. Adding this layer to the book's list of gating variables would have been falsified by the report's own evidence within one section.

This is the book's **fourth honest negative finding**, alongside `cameco-2026` (uranium is not the binding constraint) and `alab-2026` (rack fan-out is a distinct socket but not a distinct bottleneck). Anyone using Amkor's revenue as a proxy for AI packaging tightness is reading a number that is 42% smartphones and currently being throttled by a memory shortage.

**The capital cycle, in one paragraph.** Amkor spent $743.8M in 2024 and $904.6M in 2025. It has guided $2.5-3.0B for 2026, of which only $688.4M had been spent in cash through June - implying **$1.81-2.31B of cash capex in the second half alone**. It funded the front of this with $1.15 billion of **0.00% coupon convertible senior notes due 2031** issued in May 2026 (conversion price ~$106.37, capped calls purchased for $56.4M covering ~10.8M shares with a $139.50 cap), and it has contracted a further ~$1.8B of customer prepayments ($1.5B from the NVIDIA agreement in 2027, ~$300M over two years of which $100M arrived in July 2026). Management has allocated 65-70% of the 2026 budget to facilities expansion and 30-35% to HDFO, test and other advanced packaging capacity.

**Is the burn de-risked?** Mostly, on cash; not at all, on earnings. Count the committed external funding: $1.15B of converts already raised at a zero coupon, plus roughly $1.8B of customer prepayments, plus up to $407M of CHIPS direct funding, plus - by far the largest item - a **35% investment tax credit** on qualified US semiconductor manufacturing investment under the CHIPS Act as amended by the OBBBA. Against a $7B two-phase Arizona campus, a 35% credit is potentially an order of magnitude larger than the $407M headline grant everyone quotes. So the *financing* of the build is largely arranged. The problem is timing and the P&L: the ITC attaches to property placed in service, so it lands in 2028 and beyond - precisely when management has already told investors that underutilized Arizona capacity will be a headwind to **both gross margin and operating margin** in 2027 and 2028.

**The cyclicality anchor nobody should skip.** Amkor's peak EPS was **$3.11 in FY2022** on revenue of $7,091.6M at an 18.8% gross margin. Trailing EPS today is $2.23 on higher revenue of $7,457.7M at a 15.5% trailing gross margin. Four years of revenue growth have not yet restored 2022 earnings. This is what a cyclical, price-pressured subcontractor looks like across a cycle, and it is the correct prior for any forward estimate.

## 6. Bull Case

**1. The margin inflection is real, mechanical and not yet finished.** Gross margin went 12.0% → 14.2% → 16.8% over three quarters and is guided to 18.5-19.5% in Q3 - a level Amkor has not sustained since 2022. Roughly two-thirds of the Q2 step-up came from volume and utilization, one-third from mix, and utilization only moved from the 50% range into the 70% range. There is measurable headroom left in the existing asset base before a dollar of Arizona is needed.

**2. Earnings are inflecting far faster than revenue, which is the correct shape.** Q3 is guided to roughly flat revenue year over year and EPS of $0.72-0.82 against $0.51 - roughly 50% earnings growth on no revenue growth. That is what mix shift into HDFO, 2.5D and test looks like in a fixed-cost model, and it is a more durable source of value than volume.

**3. Computing is accelerating hard underneath the flat headline.** Management guided computing revenue to grow **nearly 30% sequentially** in Q3 on AI data-center demand and an HDFO CPU ramp, with four 2.5D products and four HDFO products launching in 2026. The flat total masks a violent internal mix shift from smartphones to AI/HPC - which is precisely the transition the bull case requires, happening in the disclosed numbers.

**4. Customers are pre-paying, which is the strongest form of demand evidence available.** NVIDIA's ~$1.5B 2027 prepayment and ~$300M of other advance payments are cash committed against future capacity by counterparties who could simply have placed purchase orders. Phase 1 is described as fully committed before production. Apple is the anchor customer. In a business with **no backlog**, prepayments are the only forward visibility that exists, and Amkor has just acquired more of it than at any time in its history.

**5. The subsidy stack is larger than the headline.** The $407M CHIPS direct funding is the number that gets quoted; the **35% investment tax credit** on qualified US manufacturing investment under CHIPS as amended by the OBBBA is the one that matters against a $7B campus. Add non-monetary state land grants (56 acres at $36.9M fair value acquired for $4.2M cash in 2024, exchanged in December 2025 for ~104 acres plus a right of first refusal on ~52 more).

**6. The valuation does not demand heroics.** At $48.26 the enterprise is $11.96B against $1,391.5M of trailing EBITDA - 8.6x. To earn 8% a year for three years to a terminal 8x EV/EBITDA requires EBITDA to reach roughly $1.9B by 2029, about an 11% compound rate; at a stingier 7x terminal it needs roughly 16%. Annualizing the Q3 guide already implies an EBITDA run-rate near $1.7B. The bar the price sets is low.

**7. Concentration is improving, not worsening.** Top-ten customers fell from 72% of FY2025 sales to 68% in Q1 and 66% in Q2, as computing and automotive outgrew communications. The most-cited structural weakness has been moving the right way for two consecutive quarters.

## 7. Bear Case

**1. The AI packaging that is scarce is not Amkor's to sell.** CoWoS and 3DFabric belong to TSMC and ship bundled with the wafer. Amkor's 2.5D and HDFO are real capabilities, but in the AI accelerator socket they are a second source to a foundry that has every incentive to keep the value inside its own bill. `tsmc-2026` already assigns advanced packaging to the foundry layer as a second bottleneck. Owning AMKR for the AI packaging bottleneck may be owning the wrong company.

**2. It is a smartphone company being throttled by someone else's shortage.** Communications is 42% of revenue, is guided down in Q3, and is down because Android volumes fell ~20% on **memory** supply constraints. Amkor's revenue is hostage to a component it does not make and a cycle it does not control. Total revenue growth is guided to roughly zero year over year one quarter after printing +25.6%.

**3. The free-cash-flow profile is genuinely alarming in isolation.** Trailing FCF is -$172.4M. FY2026 capex of $2.5-3.0B against roughly $1.2-1.4B of operating cash flow implies FY2026 FCF of approximately **-$1.0B to -$1.7B**, or 8-14% of the entire market capitalization, in one year - and 2027 will not be much better. Net cash has fallen from roughly $546.1M at year-end 2025 to $26.5M - a decline of about $520M on a consistent leases-exclusive basis - despite raising $1.15B of converts.

**4. Management has pre-announced the margin damage.** Underutilized Arizona capacity is guided to be a headwind to both gross and operating margin in **2027 and 2028**. The asset consuming all the cash today is expected to *dilute* returns for two years after it opens. A capital program that hurts the P&L on arrival is the hardest kind for a low-margin business to carry.

**5. Apple can leave, cheaply, at any time.** Apple was 29.8% of FY2025 sales. There is no backlog, no binding forecast and no minimum commitment. ASE can do the same work. Apple's own July 2026 commitment of $30 billion to Broadcom for US chipmaking shows it is willing to spread onshoring spend across suppliers and that it holds the leverage in every one of those negotiations. A 500bp shift in Apple's allocation is roughly $335M of annual revenue at a fixed cost base.

**6. Structural margin ceiling.** The best gross margin Amkor has printed in years is 16.8%. The filing concedes "historical downward pressure on the prices of our packaging and test services," that more than half of cost of sales is pass-through material, and that advanced packaging "tends to have higher material costs." A 10% adverse currency move alone would have cost $163M of FY2025 operating income against $467M reported. This is a business where a good year is a mid-teens gross margin.

**7. The subsidy is conditional and undelivered.** The $407M CHIPS award was signed in December 2024 and **no funds have been received to date**. It is conditioned on construction and production milestones, carries audit rights and clawbacks, and restricts certain activities. The 35% ITC only attaches when property is placed in service - 2028 at the earliest. Every dollar of the subsidy stack is a future dollar contingent on execution that has not happened.

**8. The cycle has not been kind before.** Peak EPS was $3.11 in 2022; trailing EPS is $2.23 on higher revenue. Four years of growth have not restored the prior peak. An 8.6x EV/EBITDA multiple on a cyclical at what may be a mid-cycle margin, entering its heaviest-ever capex phase, is not obviously cheap.

## 11. Conclusion

Amkor enters this book as the first and only node in the `advanced-packaging` layer, completing the coverage schema, and it earns that place by answering the question the layer was created to ask - just not in the affirmative. **Advanced packaging is a real bottleneck in the AI chain, and it is overwhelmingly TSMC's.** CoWoS and 3DFabric are sold bundled with the wafer and are where the queue actually forms. Amkor's headline "82% advanced products" is a definitional artefact covering flip chip, memory and wafer-level processing - a bucket led by smartphones, in a company where communications is 42% of revenue and computing (including PC and storage) is 22%. Its network runs in the 70% utilization range, it discloses no material backlog and no binding customer forecasts, and its next quarter is guided to **-1.9% to +3.2% year over year**. Most tellingly of all, the constraint visible in Amkor's own guidance points the other way: communications revenue is falling because Android volumes are down roughly 20% on a **memory** shortage. Amkor is currently being gated by another layer, not gating one.

The honest exception, and it is a real one, is geography. Something genuinely scarce is being priced - **US-located** advanced packaging - and the evidence is customers paying cash years ahead: a ~$1.5 billion prepayment expected in 2027 explicitly for services provided in the United States, ~$300 million of other advance payments with $100 million already received, Phase 1 described as fully committed before it exists, and Apple as anchor customer for a campus next to TSMC's Arizona fabs. That is a contractual and geopolitical constraint, not a technological one, and it is being resolved by $7 billion of capital that produces nothing until 2028.

At the company level the operating evidence is the best in years: revenue up 25.6% to a record $1,898.0M, gross margin inflecting 12.0% → 16.8% with 18.5-19.5% guided, operating margin up from 6.1% to 10.5%, EPS from $0.22 to $0.70, all on clean GAAP with no non-GAAP EPS to argue about, and against a Q2 2025 base that itself contained a $32M one-off benefit. Concentration is improving - top ten from 72% to 66% in two quarters.

At the stock level the tension is unusually clean. At $48.26 - 48.4% below the June 22 close - AMKR trades at about 21.6x trailing GAAP earnings, 8.6x EV/EBITDA, 1.60x EV/sales and 2.58x book, and the reverse test needs only about 11% compound EBITDA growth over three years to deliver 8% a year. That is a low bar and our base case clears it. But EV over EBITDA-minus-capex is $11.96 billion over $24.6 million, trailing free cash flow is negative $172.4 million, FY2026 free cash flow is heading for roughly negative $1.0-1.7 billion, net cash has fallen from about $546 million to $26.5 million on a consistent leases-exclusive basis despite raising $1.15 billion of zero-coupon converts, the $300 million buyback authorized in April sits entirely unused, and management has already told investors that the asset consuming all of this cash will drag both gross and operating margin in 2027 and 2028.

The initiation stance is **neutral watch, medium conviction**, from a 30% bull / 40% base / 30% bear grid. Neutral watch because the valuation is genuinely undemanding and the cash profile is genuinely bad, and neither cancels the other - the market is not mispricing the earnings, it is declining to pay for earnings that are fully consumed by an irreversible build. Medium rather than high conviction because the three variables that settle this - Arizona utilization in 2028, whether Apple stays near 30%, and how long the memory shortage throttles handsets - all sit outside the current disclosure window. Medium rather than low because the fact base is three days old, primary, and unusually clean.

Upgrade trigger: gross margin holds above 19% for two consecutive quarters while computing grows toward 30% of revenue and total revenue returns to double-digit year-over-year growth; the ~$1.5B prepayment is received on schedule and the first CHIPS disbursement or ITC benefit converts to cash; FY2027 capex guidance normalizes below ~$2.0B so that free cash flow is visibly on a path to positive by 2029; and Apple's disclosed share stays below ~30% without a revenue hole - upgrade to constructive. Downgrade trigger: gross margin falls back below ~15% for two consecutive quarters or utilization returns to the 50-60% range; FY2026 capex exceeds the $3.0B top of the guide or FY2027 capex guidance exceeds ~$2.5B; the ~$1.5B prepayment slips beyond 2027; Apple's disclosed share exceeds ~35% or falls sharply without replacement; or total revenue growth turns negative year over year for two consecutive quarters - downgrade to cautious.
