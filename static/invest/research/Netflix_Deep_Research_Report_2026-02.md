## 1. Business Overview

### Company Profile

Founded in 1997 and headquartered in Los Gatos, California, Netflix is the world's largest streaming video platform. The company went public on NASDAQ in 2002 and is currently led by co-CEOs Ted Sarandos (content) and Greg Peters (product & operations), with founder Reed Hastings serving as Executive Chairman.

### Business Model

Netflix's revenue comes from three segments:

**1. Subscription Revenue (~97% of 2025 revenue)**
- Standard with Ads: $7.99/month (US)
- Standard: $17.99/month
- Premium: $24.99/month
- 325 million paid subscribers across 190+ countries

**2. Advertising Revenue (rapidly growing)**
- 2025 ad revenue exceeded $1.5B, up 150% YoY
- 190 million monthly active ad-tier viewers
- ~~2026 target: double to ~$3B~~ **Update (2026-10-22):** 2026 now expected at ~$2.7B (Q3 letter); the ad tier launches in 15 new countries in 2027
- ~~Expected to reach ~15% of total revenue by 2026~~ **Update (2026-10-22):** about 5% of FY2026 revenue (~$2.7B / $51.1B); the old ~15% figure did not reconcile with the ~$3B target either

**3. Other Revenue**
- Gaming (mobile, currently small scale)
- Merchandise and experiences
- Content licensing (beginning to license select content to third parties)

### Content Ecosystem

- **Original Content:** World's largest original content investor, ~$18B content spend in 2025
- **Licensed Content:** Adopting bidirectional licensing strategy
- **Sports/Live:** Entering live sports (boxing events, etc.)
- **Gaming:** Mobile games as subscription value-add
- **Update (2026-10-22) - licensing:** new licensing deals with Disney (including all five Ice Age films) and with Globo in Brazil (the largest Netflix–Globo license to date), plus the TF1 channel integration in France, which management says is generating incremental viewing (Q3 letter and interview)
- ~~**WBD Acquisition:** Set to acquire HBO, Warner Bros. Studios, HBO Max, and DC Universe~~ **2026-06-26 correction:** Netflix is no longer acquiring WBD; HBO/Warner/DC assets should be treated as external competitive assets, not Netflix-owned upside.

## 2. Industry & Competitive Position

### Market Size

The global video streaming market was valued at approximately $811B in 2025, projected to reach $3.4 trillion by 2034. Netflix leads the US market with 20-22% share.

### Competitive Landscape

| Platform | US Market Share | Global Paid Subs | Core Advantage |
|----------|----------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Netflix** | 20-22% | 325M | Original content, global reach, recommendation algorithm |
| **Amazon Prime Video** | 19-22% | ~200M+ | Prime bundling, e-commerce ecosystem |
| **Disney+** | ~14% | ~126M | IP portfolio (Marvel, Star Wars, Pixar) |
| **Max (HBO)** | ~8% | ~110M | Premium series; ~~WBD is now a Paramount Skydance acquisition target~~ **Update (2026-10-22):** WBD became a wholly owned subsidiary of Paramount Skydance (now Skydance Corp, NYSE: SKYD) on 2026-10-06; not a Netflix asset |
| **Apple TV+** | ~5% | N/A | Apple ecosystem, high quality but limited catalog |

### Moat Assessment: 🏰 Strong

**Strengths:**
- **Content Exclusivity (Strong):** Massive exclusive original library; ~~WBD acquisition adds HBO and other premium IPs~~ no WBD synergies should be assumed after Netflix exited the bidding war
- **Scale Economies (Strong):** 325M subscribers amortize $18B content costs; per-user cost far below competitors
- **Brand Recognition (Strong):** "Netflix" is synonymous with streaming
- **Data Advantage:** Massive behavioral data drives content decisions and recommendations
- **Global Network:** 190+ country coverage with local content production capabilities

**Weaknesses:**
- **Rising Content Costs:** Competition inflates talent and IP prices
- **Slowing User Growth:** Developed markets approaching saturation
- **Post-WBD catalyst gap:** Walking away avoids deal debt, but leaves Netflix without the HBO/DC franchise injection that earlier bull cases assumed
