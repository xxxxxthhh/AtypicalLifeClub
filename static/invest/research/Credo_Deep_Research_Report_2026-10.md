## 1. Business overview and segment economics <!-- report-module:business -->

Credo is a fabless supplier of high-speed connectivity. It combines SerDes, DSPs, retimers, error correction and diagnostic software into chips and connection systems, earning revenue from products and associated IP. Buyers include manufacturing partners and end customers; shipment destination is not the location of ultimate demand. Economics depend on port counts, speeds, distances, value captured per port and the share retained after platform qualification.

There is one reportable segment and no current revenue or profit breakdown by product family. **AEC shipments contributed more than 90% of the $255.9M year-over-year revenue increase in Q1; this does not mean AECs represented more than 90% of total revenue.** The comparable FY2026 statement was more than 99% of the revenue increase. Neither statement supports an invented precise AEC/optical mix, and a longer product catalog does not establish economic diversification. ## 2. Industry, competitive position and comparison with ALAB <!-- report-module:competition -->

Connectivity is not a single socket. Ethernet scale-out, PCIe/CXL, proprietary scale-up fabrics and chip-memory interfaces solve different problems; one rack can contain CRDO, ALAB, switching systems and optical transceivers. Faster signaling can increase demand for conditioning while also encouraging topology changes or endpoint integration. Engineering difficulty creates value, but qualification, standards, endpoint SerDes and purchasing power determine who captures the profit.

|---|---|---|---|
| Established growth driver | AECs; Q1 revenue $479.0M, +114.7% year over year | PCIe conditioning / fabrics; Q2 revenue $392.4M, +104.5% | Both show real ramps; different quarters and absolute revenue do not establish market shares |
| Ethernet copper connectivity | ZeroFlap AECs between boards, within and around racks | Taurus smart cable modules | Most direct competitive overlap; compare end power, distance, failure rates and total cost |

## 5. Bull case <!-- report-module:bullBear -->

The necessary condition is that incremental optical profit exceeds displaced copper or externally sold component profit; all additional BOM spending cannot be counted as net new value.

## 6. Bear case <!-- report-module:bullBear -->

**First, concentrated customers make platform decisions decisive.** Four end customers contribute 84%, including one at 33%. Qualification creates switching costs while giving a few buyers leverage over pricing, inventories and second sourcing. If one platform uses fewer AECs, shorter passive links or more optics, replacement business need not arrive in the same quarter. Anonymous lists cannot establish a named customer being locked in for years.

**Second, growth and cash per share can diverge.** Q1 absorbed $151.3M of working capital and incurred $88.0M SBC, leaving FCF after an economic SBC charge slightly negative. Subsequent deposits require another $102.4M. Acquisitions can increase sales while reducing GAAP returns and consuming cash. Maintaining R&D through equity awards costs shareholders dilution or cash for repurchases. The main scenario grid expresses equity cost through future net shares; FCF-SBC is a separate check and is not deducted again in that grid.

**Third, hedging copper with optics requires investment.** Micro-emitters, SiPho, DSPs, modules and PCIe cross different supply chains and qualification processes.
