## Executive Summary <!-- report-module:overview -->

**One-line thesis:** ASE is the world's largest merchant assembly-and-test platform. Its Q2 2026 computing mix, advanced-packaging portfolio and margins all moved higher, proving that an OSAT can capture valuable AI/HPC work; but ASE still names no accelerator program packaged end-to-end outside TSMC, so it strengthens the case for a merchant architecture participant without proving a separately-owned packaging bottleneck.

**Investment verdict:** **Neutral-watch / medium conviction.** The business is improving and the price already discounts much of that improvement. At the **$37.39** close on 2026-08-07, Q2 basic-to-diluted weighted shares imply an equity-value range of **$82.0-84.2B** and enterprise value of **$88.3-90.5B**, or roughly **15.2-15.6x annualized Q2 EBITDA, 3.7x annualized Q2 revenue and 32.0x annualized diluted ADS EPS**. A reverse test says that a three-year holding period, an 11x exit multiple and an 8% annual return require about **20-21% EBITDA CAGR**. My base case is 15-18%, leaving a mildly negative expectation gap.

**Chain job:** This is the second `architecture-check` in `advanced-packaging` and directly stress-tests `amkr-2026`. ASE's scale and process breadth prove that merchant OSATs matter. The observable evidence still does not justify reclassifying the TSMC CoWoS queue as ASE's own constraint read.

## 1. Business Overview and Chain Role <!-- report-module:business -->

ASE Technology Holding combines ASE, SPIL and USI across semiconductor packaging, testing, materials and electronics manufacturing services. It is not an AI-packaging pure play. Q2 2026 consolidated mix was **52% packaging, 13% testing, 34% EMS and 1% other**. The business that matters to this book is ATM (assembly, test and materials); EMS is large but earns only an 8.9% gross margin and a 2.4% operating margin, diluting group-level advanced-packaging economics.

| Business | Q2 2026 revenue | YoY | Gross margin | Operating margin | Book interpretation |
|----------|-----------------|-----|--------------|------------------|---------------------|
| ATM | NT$126.148B | +36.3% | 27.3% | 15.7% | Main carrier of AI/HPC packaging and test |
| EMS | NT$65.789B | +11.9% | 8.9% | 2.4% | Scale and customer access, but material group-margin dilution |
| Consolidated | NT$191.064B | +26.7% | 21.0% | 11.1% | Includes eliminations; not all growth is AI |

ATM applications were **41% communications, 30% computing, and 29% automotive, consumer and other**. Computing was 24% a year earlier. By service type, bumping/flip-chip/wafer-level/SiP was 49%, wire bonding 24%, testing 19%, materials 2% and other 6%. This is closer to a high-performance-packaging read than Amkor's broad “advanced products” bucket, but it still does not separate AI data center from PCs and other computing.

ASE's VIPack portfolio covers FOCoS, FOCoS-Bridge, FOPoP, FOSiP, TSV 2.5D/3D and co-packaged optics. A 310x310mm automated panel-level line is scheduled for production in **H1 2027**. The WUS hub targets about 113,000 square metres and 2,000 jobs, with construction due by **September 2029**. A new Kaohsiung facility carries **NT$17.8B** of investment and is due in **Q2 2028**. These are capacity and roadmap evidence, not proof that customers have awarded independently-priced accelerator packages.

## 2. Industry and Competitive Position <!-- report-module:competition -->

ASE's advantages are scale, process breadth and deep coordination with foundries and customers. Its weakness is that the scarcest accelerator packaging may still be bundled by the wafer foundry. The correction to `amkr-2026` is not “OSATs are irrelevant”; it is “OSATs create value without necessarily owning the queue.”

| Player | Position | Effect on the ASE thesis |
|--------|----------|--------------------------|
| TSMC | CoWoS/3DFabric bundled with leading-edge wafers | Still dominates the ownership question; no separate CoWoS queue disclosure |
| ASE | Largest merchant OSAT, from bumping and 2.5D/3D through test and systems | Proves merchant participation, but names no ex-TSMC end-to-end accelerator program |
| Amkor | US-headquartered OSAT with HDFO/2.5D and Arizona onshoring | Geography and customer prepayments create a narrow independent socket |
| JCET / Powertech / Tongfu | Alternative capacity in price-sensitive, memory and customer-specific markets | Caps pricing power, especially in traditional packaging |
| Intel / Samsung / memory-company captive packaging | IDM integration | Structural ceiling on outsourced share |

The moat is not one patent. It is multi-site yield learning, customer qualification, integrated packaging and test, and capital intensity. The counterweight is an industry with high fixed costs, substantial pass-through materials, concentration and cyclicality. ATM's top five customers were **44%** and top ten **60%** of revenue, with one above 10%. That is less severe than Amkor's named Apple exposure, but it is not diversified.

## 4. Management and Governance <!-- report-module:management -->

ASE remains founder- and core-management-led, with Jason C.S. Chang as chairman, Tien Wu in senior operating leadership and Joseph Tung overseeing finance. The research questions are more useful than biographies: does high capex convert into high-value mix, does the combined ASE/SPIL/USI platform preserve segment discipline, and do minority shareholders share the capital return on equal terms?

| Governance dimension | Current evidence | Assessment |
|----------------------|------------------|------------|
| Execution | ATM revenue +36.3%, 27.3% gross margin, 15.7% operating margin | Strong current ramp and mix execution |
| Capital allocation | Q2 equipment capex $1.695B, split nearly evenly between packaging and test | Returns remain to be demonstrated |
| Disclosure | Useful segment, application and process mix; no named AI program or advanced-packaging revenue | Good operating disclosure, weak ownership disclosure |
| Ownership and governance | Concentrated controlling influence; ADR holders sit behind Taiwan governance | Requires a continuing governance and capital-discipline discount |

Management deserves credit for not calling the entire 30% computing bucket AI. The missing disclosure is still decisive: no named customer, program, wafer source or independent pricing. For this book, that is the evidence required to upgrade `advanced-packaging-ownership`, not a cosmetic omission.

## 5. Bull Case <!-- report-module:bullBear -->

1. **Computing has moved from story to numbers.** Its ATM share rose from 24% to 30% while ATM revenue increased 36.3% and gross margin reached 27.3%. Mix and utilization improved together.

2. **ASE can absorb merchant spillover.** VIPack spans 2.5D/3D, bridge, fan-out and CPO, and the 310mm panel line starts in H1 2027. If accelerator customers demand second sourcing or larger-panel economics, ASE is closer to a complete platform than a point-tool supplier.

3. **Test may carry the better independent economics.** Test was 19% of ATM revenue, and Q2 test equipment spending of $804M nearly matched packaging at $840M. High-power chiplets and HBM raise test time and complexity even when TSMC retains package ownership.

4. **Operating leverage is visible.** Consolidated operating margin rose from 6.4% to 11.1% and ATM operating margin reached 15.7%. If 2027-2029 capacity fills, EBITDA can outgrow revenue.

The bull case requires 22-26% three-year EBITDA CAGR, a 17-19% ATM operating margin, computing above 35%, and at least one named ex-TSMC end-to-end accelerator package. Under that combination, today's 15.2-15.6x annualized EBITDA can be absorbed by earnings growth.

## 6. Bear Case <!-- report-module:bullBear -->

1. **The evidence has not crossed the ownership threshold.** ASE discloses capabilities and aggregate revenue, not programs. TSMC can keep the scarce CoWoS value in its wafer contract while outsourcing selected back-end steps; ASE revenue does not prove an independently-priced socket.

2. **Capex consumes cash first.** Q2 equipment capex of $1.695B exceeded roughly $1.45B of quarterly EBITDA. Q2 and H1 simple FCF were about -NT$32.8B and -NT$40.5B. Any delay in 2027-2029 capacity or customer conversion magnifies fixed cost.

3. **The group is not pure.** EMS is roughly 34% of consolidated revenue at an 8.9% gross margin. Even strong ATM packaging is diluted by low-margin EMS and legacy communications.

4. **Customer and geographic concentration coexist.** ATM's top five were 44% and top ten 60%, while much of the manufacturing base remains in Taiwan and Asia. Taiwan Strait, FX and governance discounts do not disappear with AI demand.

5. **The embedded growth hurdle is high.** A 32.0x annualized-quarter P/E and 15.2-15.6x annualized-quarter EBITDA multiple already demand years of strong growth. At 8-12% EBITDA CAGR and a 9-11x exit multiple, returns from today's price are weak.

## 7. Key Uncertainties and Thesis-Breaking Conditions <!-- report-module:uncertainties -->

| Uncertainty | Why it matters | When we will know | Breaking condition |
|-------------|----------------|-------------------|--------------------|
| True AI/HPC revenue | Computing includes non-AI products | Quarterly application mix and calls | Computing share and ATM margin decline together |
| Ownership outside TSMC | Determines whether the layer becomes a constraint read | Named program or supply-chain filing | Treating aggregate revenue as ownership without a named program |
| New-capacity utilization | Determines capex returns | H1 2027 panel line, Q2 2028 Kaohsiung, 2029 WUS | Milestone slip or ATM gross margin below 24% |
| Test pricing power | May be the best CoWoS-independent proxy | Test revenue, capex and utilization | Spending rises without revenue or profit following |
| Customer concentration | One allocation can move utilization | Quarterly top-five/top-ten share | Top five exceeds 50% without new customers offsetting it |

The central discipline is: **do not infer orders from capability, ownership from orders, or a bottleneck from ownership.** Each step needs separate evidence. A named accelerator program dual-sourced for end-to-end 2.5D/3D packaging outside TSMC would upgrade the architecture finding. Until then ASE is strong participant evidence.

## 10. Conclusion <!-- report-module:conclusion -->

First, the chain answer: ASE proves that merchant OSATs possess real and expanding advanced-packaging capability, but public evidence still does not show that ASE owns an independently-priced accelerator queue outside TSMC. `advanced-packaging` remains an ownership and architecture read, not a constraint read disguised by capability slides.

Second, the expectation gap is mildly negative. The current 15.2-15.6x annualized Q2 EBITDA multiple implies about 20-21% three-year EBITDA CAGR, against a 15-18% base case and negative H1 simple free cash flow.

Third, the 20% bull / 50% base / 30% bear grid supports **neutral-watch / medium conviction**. Strong Q2 execution, a rising computing mix and better ATM margins keep it above cautious; capex, missing ownership evidence and an elevated multiple keep it below constructive.

Fourth, upgrade requires computing progressing toward 35%, ATM operating margin holding above 17% for two quarters, a visible path to positive FCF, and a named ex-TSMC end-to-end accelerator program. Downgrade follows ATM gross margin below 24%, capacity milestones slipping, top-five customers exceeding 50%, or capex continuing above EBITDA without commensurate growth.
