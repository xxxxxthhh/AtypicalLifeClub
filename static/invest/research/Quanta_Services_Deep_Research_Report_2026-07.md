## Executive Summary

> **Framework note.** Quanta joins the AI-infrastructure book's **power** layer as a **common constraint**. The layer already holds eight reports and every one of them is about who owns electrons or who makes the equipment: `constellation-energy-2026` and `vistra-2026` and `nrg-2026` on generation and retail, `gevernova-2026` and `bloom-energy-2026` and `jinpan-2026` and `neov-2026` on equipment, `oklo-2026` on long-dated nuclear optionality. Not one of them covers the tier that physically builds the line, sets the substation and energises the site. Quanta is that tier. Its job in this book is to run one test the layer has never run: **is the binding constraint on getting AI data centres powered the generation, the turbine slot — or the crew, the right-of-way and the interconnection?** The answer is allowed to be unwelcome, and part of it is.

**One-line thesis:** Quanta is the largest self-perform infrastructure contractor in North America and the only listed way to own the labour and construction step between an announced megawatt and an energised one — and on its own Q2 2026 disclosure that step is **not currently the binding constraint**, because Quanta grew revenue **41%** year over year while *expanding* Electric-segment operating margin from **10.1% to 11.5%**. A binding constraint does not look like that. What Quanta's own capital allocation points at instead is the **front end** — land, right-of-way, permitting and interconnection studies — which is precisely what it bought in Q2 2026.

**Current view:** **Neutral-watch, medium conviction.** The operating evidence is close to the best in this entire book: total backlog of **$53.44B** (+49.1% year over year), 12-month backlog of **$32.31B** (+61.2%), a full-year revenue guide raised **17.9%** in five months, a Moody's upgrade to Baa2, and free cash flow that exceeds GAAP net income. The price is the problem. At the **2026-07-30 close of US$657.98** — the first completed session after the Q2 print, and **+17.3%** in a single day — the market caps Quanta at about **US$98.9B**, or roughly **74.6x** trailing-twelve-month reported earnings, **48.5x** trailing adjusted net income and **30.2x** enterprise value to trailing adjusted EBITDA on the twelve months ended 2026-06-30. The gap between those two EPS definitions — **43.2%** — is the acquisition amortisation and stock compensation that the roll-up generating the growth creates.

## 1. Business Overview

Quanta sells crews, not kit. It is a specialty contractor that self-performs the physical work of building, upgrading and maintaining electric transmission and distribution lines, substations, power generation, underground pipeline and communications infrastructure — plus, increasingly, the electrical and mechanical fit-out of data centres and advanced manufacturing plants.

**First, the segment table can no longer answer the question this book most wants to ask.** Since Q1 2025 Quanta has reported **two** segments, with the Renewable Energy Infrastructure Solutions segment folded into Electric and prior periods recast. Solar, wind, battery, transmission, distribution, substation and data-centre electrical work now all land in one 82%-of-revenue line. There is no disclosed data-centre revenue, no disclosed technology-customer concentration, and no way to compute an "AI share" of the backlog from primary filings. Anyone who tells you Quanta's growth is *x*% AI-driven is estimating, not reading.

**Second, the backlog understates the business, and management says so.** Quanta's backlog is remaining performance obligations *plus* estimated orders under master service agreements, and MSA work is booked and billed continuously. On the Q2 call management described data-centre balance-of-plant scope covering roughly 80-90% of a build and noted that hundreds of millions of dollars can be booked and billed on a site within a quarter without ever appearing in backlog (earnings-call coverage, not a filed figure — see the appendix). The arithmetic corroborates it: the 2026 revenue guide of $39.3-39.7B exceeds the entire 12-month backlog of $32.31B measured at 2026-06-30.

**Third, the growth is bought as well as built.** Quanta completed eight acquisitions in 2024, eight in 2025, and four in Q2/July 2026 — Phalcon (~4,100 employees, Northeast/Mid-Atlantic electrical, explicitly aimed at data centre and mission-critical facilities), Enerfab (~2,100, fabrication and mechanical), Percheron (~1,050, land/right-of-way, survey, geospatial and engineering design) and PSD (170, Australian transportable substations and switchgear). Aggregate upfront consideration was $1.24B ($1.07B cash, $173.3M stock) with up to $242.3M contingent, funded with debt and cash. Quanta sizes their FY2026 contribution at **$1.2-1.4B of revenue and $120-140M of adjusted EBITDA**, mostly in Electric.

## 2. Industry and Competitive Position

**The competitive claim is self-perform scale.** Quanta's differentiator is that it employs the craft workforce rather than subcontracting it, which is what lets it promise schedule certainty to a utility that cannot afford a slipped outage window. That claim is not marketing: the Q2 release states it directly ("the execution certainty our self-perform capabilities and craft-skilled workforce deliver for customers every day"), and the margin series is consistent with it.

**Where Quanta sits against listed peers.** The peer set is small and the valuation gap is the single most striking thing in it.

Two things follow. Quanta trades at roughly **1.6x to 2.5x** the forward earnings multiple of every listed peer. And Primoris, in the same end markets, is **−30.9%** year-to-date while Quanta is **+56.0%** — so the sector is not being re-rated as a group. Something specific to Quanta is being paid for.

**Barriers to entry are real but they are not a moat in the usual sense.** The barrier is a trained craft workforce, a safety record, a fleet, and bonding capacity. None of that is a patent or a network effect; it is an accumulated operating asset that a competitor could in principle rebuild given four years and capital — which, per management's own framing of apprenticeship timelines, is roughly the cycle time to make a journeyman. What Quanta has is a large head start, not an exclusion.

**The demand backdrop is genuine and it is not only AI.** Load growth in the United States is being driven simultaneously by data centres, electrification, onshoring of manufacturing, and the replacement of an ageing grid — plus renewables interconnection, which continues regardless. At Quanta's 2026 Investor Day management sized the converging utility, generation and large-load opportunity at a **$2.4 trillion** total addressable market through 2030 (third-party-reported; no Form 8-K was furnished for the Investor Day — see the appendix).

## 3. The Interconnection-Gate Test: What Actually Stops an AI Data Centre from Being Powered?

This is the section that justifies Quanta's place in this book, and its answer is partly negative. The chain role is **common constraint** because the question is whether one input gates several layers at once — the label names the *job*, not the answer.

**The claim under test.** The power layer prices generation and equipment as the scarce goods. `gevernova-2026` is explicit that gas-turbine slot scarcity gives GE Vernova pricing power and that "customers queue to reserve gas-turbine capacity years out." `constellation-energy-2026` and `vistra-2026` price the electrons. `bloom-energy-2026` prices a way to bypass the grid entirely. If the true bottleneck were instead the crew and the interconnection, then equipment orders and PPAs would be a leading indicator of nothing much, and every valuation in the layer would be discounting a schedule that cannot be met.

**The evidence, stated against the claim:**

| Observable | Q2 2026 disclosure | What it does and does not prove |
|------------|--------------------|---------------------------------|
| Revenue growth | +41.2% year over year, to $9.557B | A tier that is itself the binding constraint does not grow 41%. It grows at the rate of its constraint. |
| Electric operating margin | 11.5%, up from 10.1%; H1 10.2% from 9.2% | If craft labour were scarce enough to bind, you would see scarcity pricing *and* volume rationing. Quanta shows scarcity pricing **and** volume expansion — the signature of a supplier with headroom, not a chokepoint. |
| Guidance raised twice | Revenue guide $33.25-33.75B (Feb) → $34.7-35.2B (Apr) → $39.3-39.7B (Jul) | The +$6.0B midpoint raise is roughly **$4.7B organic** after backing out the $1.2-1.4B acquisition contribution the company itself sizes. Quanta discovered it could do far more work than it thought — not less. |
| Firm-order growth | Remaining performance obligations +75.1% y/y to $33.55B, versus +19.2% for estimated MSA orders | The backlog got **harder**, not softer. Contracted, fixed-price scope grew four times as fast as management's own estimates of recurring work. |
| What Quanta bought | Percheron: land services (title and right-of-way), surveying, geospatial and engineering design — the **front end**, not the crew | The clearest revealed preference in the print. A company that believed labour was the binding constraint would buy labour. Quanta bought permitting and right-of-way capacity. |
| What Quanta also bought | Phalcon (data-centre and mission-critical electrical), Enerfab (fabrication), PSD (transportable substations and switchgear) | Fabrication and substation hardware, one step upstream. Consistent with lead-time items being tighter than crews. |
| The circuit-breaker JV | Hyosung HICO Breaker LLC, June 2026, high-voltage breakers to 800 kV, in Pennsylvania | A contractor building a factory for a component is a statement that the component, not the installer, is short. |
| Interconnection queues | Roughly 8,200 projects and ~1,312 GW of generation plus ~749 GW of storage were awaiting interconnection at end-2025 (Berkeley Lab *Queued Up*, third-party-summarised — see the appendix) | The queue is enormous and it is an administrative and study-throughput problem, not a construction-capacity problem. Nobody is waiting five years for a crew. |
| Craft-workforce headroom | Management described adding roughly 15,000 employees year-to-date, spending about $250M a year on training, and stated it is nowhere near capacity (earnings-call coverage, not a filed figure) | Directionally consistent with the margin and revenue evidence, but this is the weakest source in the report and no conclusion here rests on it alone. |

**The answer, stated plainly.** On 2026 disclosure, **construction labour is not the binding constraint on getting AI data centres powered.** Nothing here speaks to turbine availability one way or the other — Quanta's margins are evidence about Quanta's crews, not about GE Vernova's slots, and the read-through below leaves that claim standing. The tier that physically builds and connects grew 41% with expanding margins and raised its own annual guidance by 17.9% in five months. That is the profile of a supplier with slack, not a chokepoint. Where the evidence *does* point — and it points there through Quanta's own capital allocation rather than through any statement Quanta makes — is at the **front end and the long-lead hardware**: right-of-way and permitting (Percheron), fabrication (Enerfab), substations and switchgear (PSD), and high-voltage circuit breakers (the Hyosung HICO JV). Those are the things Quanta spent $1.24B and a factory on in a single quarter.

This is an inference from capital allocation, and it should be held at that level. Quanta has not said "permitting is the gate." It said Percheron "solves a major bottleneck for utility and technology projects by helping move work into the field faster and more economically" — a press-release characterisation, not a filed one. The defensible claim is that **Quanta's money is going to the front end**, and that this is informative.

**What this changes in the other reports.** Three concrete read-throughs:

1. **`gevernova-2026`'s turbine-slot scarcity argument is not contradicted, but it is narrowed.** If crews had slack while turbine slots did not, the constraint is specific to heavy rotating equipment and long-lead electrical hardware — which is exactly why Quanta is now building circuit breakers itself. GE Vernova's pricing power survives this test; the broader claim that "power equipment gates the buildout" needs the word *equipment* to mean forgings, breakers and transformers rather than the whole electrical supply chain.
2. **The power layer's generation reports can stop treating construction as an implicit risk.** `constellation-energy-2026`, `vistra-2026` and `nrg-2026` price megawatts on the assumption that they can be delivered. On this evidence that assumption is safe for the contractor step and unsafe for the interconnection-study step, which none of the three reports models and which Quanta cannot fix.
3. **The book cannot use PWR as an AI-demand thermometer, and should say so rather than pretend.** Quanta discloses no data-centre revenue, folded Renewables into Electric in Q1 2025, and tells investors that a large share of data-centre work is booked and billed without entering backlog. A rising Quanta backlog is evidence that grid capex is rising. It is **not** evidence about the AI share of it, and any report in this book that cites Quanta as AI confirmation is over-reading the disclosure.

**The honest counter-case.** Three things could make this answer wrong. Quanta is one company, and the largest one — it may have slack precisely because it can outbid smaller contractors for scarce crews, in which case the industry is constrained even though Quanta is not. The 41% growth includes acquisitions that bought other companies' workforces, which relaxes Quanta's constraint without relaxing the industry's. And a constraint that binds in 2028 is invisible in 2026 results by construction; management's own framing of a roughly four-year apprenticeship means today's hiring sets 2030 capacity, so the labour question is genuinely undecidable on one quarter of margin data.

## 5. Management and Governance

Duke Austin has run Quanta since 2016 and the compounding record is the case for him: revenue of $23.67B in 2024, $28.48B in 2025, and a 2026 guide of $39.3-39.7B — roughly a 1.7x in two years, of which a material part is acquired. CFO Jayshree Desai reported leverage improving to about 1.7x from 1.95x at year-end 2025 alongside roughly $2.8B of total liquidity (earnings-call coverage), and the Moody's upgrade to Baa2 in June 2026 is a filed, third-party confirmation that the balance sheet did not deteriorate while $1.07B of cash left for acquisitions.

Three governance observations, stated at their proper strength:

**The acquisition cadence is the strategy, and it is also the thing that makes the numbers hard to read.** Sixteen acquisitions across 2024-2025 plus four more in Q2/July 2026 means there is no clean organic growth rate in this company's public disclosure. Quanta sizes the *aggregate* FY2026 contribution of the four newest deals ($1.2-1.4B of revenue) but does not publish an organic revenue growth figure. The $4.7B organic component of the July guidance raise in this report is a derivation, not a disclosure.

**The adjusted-EPS definition adds back the cost of that strategy.** Adjusted diluted EPS excludes intangible amortisation ($157.0M in Q2, $309.3M in H1), stock compensation ($63.4M and $126.0M), acquisition and integration costs, and contingent-consideration remeasurement. Amortisation of acquired intangibles is a non-cash charge that reverses the purchase price of the growth. Excluding it is defensible as a comparability measure and indefensible as the sole basis for a 39x multiple. Note that Quanta publishes both series prominently and reconciles them fully — the disclosure is good; the question is which number an investor chooses.

**Capital allocation is coherent and aggressive.** In a single quarter Quanta authorised a $1B buyback, paid $1.07B of cash for acquisitions, committed to a manufacturing joint venture, and improved its credit rating. That is a management team pressing an advantage, not managing a mature asset. The buyback is small relative to the M&A and should be read as an offset to stock-based compensation and acquisition stock issuance ($173.3M in Q2) rather than as a return of capital.

## 6. Bull Case

**The demand is not a forecast; it is contracted.** Firm remaining performance obligations grew **75.1%** year over year to $33.55B while management's softer MSA estimates grew 19.2%. The firm share of total backlog rose from 53.5% to 62.8%. The most common bear argument against contractors — that backlog is a soft number management can inflate — is empirically wrong at Quanta this quarter: the hard component is what grew.

**Margins are expanding into the growth, not compressing.** Electric at 11.5% and Underground at 9.1% are both at or near multi-year highs, in a quarter with 41% revenue growth. Contractors that overreach show up as margin compression and project charges within two to three quarters. There is no sign of that yet.

**The self-perform craft workforce is the scarce asset in an electrification decade.** Whatever the binding constraint is today, a trained union and non-union craft base of Quanta's scale takes years and hundreds of millions of dollars a year to replicate. Management describes roughly a four-year apprenticeship and about $250M a year of training spend (earnings-call coverage). If load growth persists into the 2030s, the pricing power accrues to whoever already has the crews.

**Quanta is moving up the value chain into the actual bottleneck.** The Hyosung HICO breaker JV (800 kV, Pennsylvania), Enerfab's fabrication, PSD's transportable substations and Percheron's right-of-way capability all attack lead-time items rather than adding more of the same labour. If the front end is where the delay is, Quanta has bought the ability to sell schedule certainty rather than just hours.

**Cash flow supports the story.** H1 FCF of $1,070.4M exceeds GAAP net income; FY2026 FCF is guided to $2.00-2.50B; leverage improved to about 1.6-1.7x while spending $1.07B on acquisitions; Moody's upgraded the credit. This is not a growth story financed by working-capital sleight of hand.

## 7. Bear Case

**The multiple prices a compounder, and the business is a contractor.** 39.4x guided adjusted EPS, 56.4x guided GAAP EPS, 25.2x EV to guided adjusted EBITDA, and a 2.27% free-cash-flow yield on the guidance midpoint — for a business whose consolidated GAAP operating margin is 7.3% in its best quarter ever. Listed peers doing overlapping work trade at 15.7-24.7x forward earnings. The premium is 1.6-2.5x, and it is not supported by a structurally different margin profile; Electric at 11.5% is good for a contractor, not for a 39x multiple.

**The earnings number carrying the multiple excludes the cost of producing the earnings.** The 43.2% gap between GAAP and adjusted EPS is intangible amortisation and stock compensation. Quanta buys companies, capitalises the purchase price as goodwill and intangibles, amortises the intangibles, and then reports an EPS figure that adds the amortisation back. On that arithmetic the roll-up is free. On GAAP the same guidance is $11.41-11.92, and the stock is at 56.4x.

**Tangible book equity is negative.** Goodwill and other intangibles of $11,085.0M exceed total stockholders' equity of $9,637.9M by about $1.45B. Sixteen acquisitions in two years plus four more this quarter means a rising share of the balance sheet is purchase price rather than productive assets, and impairment risk is a live item in any demand pause.

**A backlog is not a moat.** Every dollar in that $53.44B is work Quanta must still perform at an estimated cost, on customer schedules, with weather, permitting and change-order risk. Fixed-price scope — which is precisely the component that grew 75% — carries the estimating risk. The industry's history is that the quarter which breaks a contractor is the one after the record backlog, not before it.

**"AI data centre demand" may substantially be grid capex that was going to happen anyway.** This is not a suspicion; it is a disclosure gap. Quanta reports two segments, folded Renewables into Electric in Q1 2025, publishes no data-centre revenue line, and states that much of its data-centre work never enters backlog. Utilities were going to replace an ageing grid, harden against wildfire, and interconnect renewables regardless of whether any language model was ever trained. **The filings do not permit an AI attribution, in either direction.** A thesis that requires one is unfalsifiable on this disclosure.

**The growth rate being capitalised has never been sustained.** Guided 2026 adjusted EPS of $16.45-16.95 is **+55%** over the 2025 actual of $10.75, after two guidance raises in five months. That is not a run rate; it is a step change driven partly by acquisitions completed mid-year. The multiple assumes the step repeats.

## 8. Key Uncertainties and Thesis-Breaking Conditions

| Uncertainty | Why it matters | What would resolve it |

For the bear thesis: FY2027 guidance implying continued 20%+ adjusted EPS growth with margins holding, which would make today's multiple a growth multiple rather than a contractor multiple. For the *chain* thesis specifically: any disclosure showing that Quanta is turning away work for lack of crews would reverse this report's central finding, and should be treated as a genuine falsification rather than a footnote.

## 11. Conclusion

Quanta enters this book as the power layer's second common constraint, and it earns the label by answering a question the layer had never asked and by answering part of it negatively.

**At the chain level the finding is that the build-and-connect tier has slack.** The contractor that would be the chokepoint if construction labour were binding grew revenue 41%, expanded Electric-segment operating margin from 10.1% to 11.5%, and raised its own annual revenue guidance 17.9% in five months. Constraints do not behave that way. Where Quanta's own money went in the same quarter — $1.24B into right-of-way and permitting, fabrication, transportable substations and a high-voltage breaker factory — points instead at the front end and the long-lead hardware. That narrows rather than contradicts `gevernova-2026`: equipment scarcity is real, but it means forgings, breakers and transformers, not the whole electrical supply chain. It also tells the layer's generation reports that the assumption "megawatts announced can be built" is safe for the contractor step and untested for the interconnection-study step, which no report in this book models and which Quanta cannot fix.

**At the chain level the second finding is a disclosure gap the book should stop working around.** Quanta reports two segments, folded Renewable Energy into Electric in Q1 2025 with prior periods recast, discloses no data-centre revenue, and states that much of its data-centre work is booked and billed without ever entering backlog. Quanta's rising backlog is strong evidence that grid capex is rising. It is **not** evidence about the AI share of it, in either direction. Any report in this book that cites Quanta as AI confirmation is over-reading the filings, and this report exists partly to say so.

**At the company level Quanta is what it claims to be.** Record backlog whose *firm* component grew 75%, margins expanding on both segments, free cash flow above GAAP net income, leverage improving to about 1.6x while spending $1.07B of cash on acquisitions, and a Moody's upgrade. There is no accrual game here and no working-capital sleight of hand. This is a well-run company in the best demand environment of its history.

**At the stock level the discipline point is arithmetic.** At US$657.98 — up 17.3% in one session and 56.0% year-to-date — the market pays about 74.6x trailing reported earnings, 48.5x trailing adjusted net income, 30.2x EV to trailing adjusted EBITDA, and 39.4x a 2026 adjusted-EPS guide whose definition adds back the $157M-a-quarter amortisation of the acquisitions that generate the growth. Listed peers doing overlapping work sit at 15.7-24.7x forward. And the price is 24.6x the *top* of a 2030 adjusted-EPS target four years out. Paying that for a business whose best-ever consolidated GAAP operating margin is 7.3% is paying today for a growth regime that has existed for one year.

The initiation stance is **neutral-watch, medium conviction**, from a 30% bull / 40% base / 30% bear grid whose asymmetry is in magnitude rather than probability. Medium, not high, because the operating disclosure is unusually good and the central mechanism is published quarterly by the company itself — but the AI-attribution question, which is the reason this report exists for the book, is unanswerable from the filings, and a conviction rating should reflect that. Neutral-watch, not cautious, because record backlog, 1.6x leverage, an upgraded credit and free cash flow above net income remove the downside tail that a cautious rating should imply. And neutral-watch, not constructive, because nothing in this print argues that 39.4x forward adjusted EPS is the right price one day after a 17.3% move.

Upgrade trigger: FY2027 guidance implies continued 20%+ adjusted-EPS growth with Electric-segment margin held at 11%+, the record remaining performance obligations convert without a material project charge, the RPO share of total backlog holds above 60%, and the organic component of growth remains the majority of any further guidance raise — upgrade to constructive. Downgrade trigger: a material project charge or margin reversal in Electric, FY2027 guidance implying sub-mid-teens adjusted-EPS growth, the firm RPO share of backlog falling back toward 55%, acquisitions becoming the majority of a guidance raise, or free cash flow falling below GAAP net income for two consecutive halves while goodwill and intangibles keep rising against equity — downgrade to cautious.
