## 1. Business Overview and Unit Economics <!-- report-module:business -->

AXT Inc. (Fremont, California; incorporated in Delaware, founded 1986, public since 1998) makes compound and single-element semiconductor substrates — InP, gallium arsenide (GaAs) and germanium (Ge) — and, through consolidated raw-material subsidiaries, sells high-purity gallium, pyrolytic boron nitride (pBN) crucibles, boron oxide and related materials. **All substrates and raw materials are manufactured in China** by its subsidiary Beijing Tongmei Xtal Technology (Tongmei) at Beijing (InP crystal growth and wafering), Dingxing (GaAs wafer processing) and Kazuo (GaAs and Ge crystal growth), plus a network of raw-material companies. AXT sells to epitaxy houses and device makers; it does not grow epitaxial layers. It reports one segment, disaggregated into Substrates and Raw Materials and Other.

**Ownership basis warning.** AXT owns about 85.5% of Tongmei at 2026-06-30: noncontrolling and redeemable noncontrolling interests together are about 14.5% (10-Q Note 7). On 2026-07-23 AXT agreed to buy 5,135,475 Tongmei shares for RMB 40,621,600 (about $6.0M) from Bomeilian, a related party, which would take it to about 86.09%; the purchase had not closed at the 10-Q date. Every revenue and margin figure below is consolidated; about 14% of Tongmei's profit belongs to others. **Update (2026-11-02):** On 2026-10-27 Tongmei and AXT signed private equity agreements (8-K 2026-10-28): investors subscribe for Tongmei ordinary shares of a minimum of RMB 1.91235B (about $285M) and a maximum of RMB 3.66864B (about $545M); AXT concurrently subscribes for 342,998,797 shares for the RMB equivalent of $400M as a recapitalization. **At the maximum AXT owns about 73.40%** and keeps consolidating (the CFO: "down approximately 12.1%", call). Closing is expected in December 2026 and is not assured. About 26.6% of Tongmei's profit would then belong to others; the §10 grid uses that ratio.

## 2. Industry and Competitive Position <!-- report-module:competition -->

InP substrates sit at the very start of the optical chain: an epitaxy house grows laser or photodetector layers on the wafer, a device maker (Coherent, Lumentum, Chinese makers such as Casela) builds EMLs, CW lasers for silicon photonics and photodetectors, and module makers (Innolight, AAOI, Coherent itself) assemble transceivers. The CEO describes the near-term driver as "the industry migration to 100G [transcript as written; read as 800G] and 1.6T transceiver modules. For which indium phosphide based lasers and detectors are essential" and the longer-term driver as near- and co-packaged optics (call). ## 3. Export Licensing, Tariffs and Geopolitics <!-- report-module:competition -->

This is the section on which the thesis turns, and it cuts both ways: **China's licensing regime is simultaneously AXT's pricing lever (the substrate is scarce and controlled) and a lever over AXT itself (a regulator decides which customer receives which order, and when).**

The CEO said materials are deployed across "multiple U.S. hyperscalers" (call), but this report has no evidence that the first US InP permits have arrived.

**Why it is also a lever for AXT.** Because licensing slows every China-origin shipment, customers who need InP now are signing prepaid, take-or-pay contracts; the CEO links the favorable pricing to diameter mix, and the capacity-in-dollars targets rose faster than the physical capacity. A licensing regime that makes Chinese InP scarce outside China helps a China-based supplier's price — as long as its own permits keep flowing.

**Cross-check framing.** The chain rule `export-control-escalation` in the coverage map is written for US listings of Chinese suppliers; AXT is the mirror case — Chinese export licensing of a Chinese supplier's output to non-Chinese customers. The observable evidence the rule asks for (denied licences, delivery slippage) exists here as the Q4 2025 shortfall and as the absence of North American InP revenue. The chain-level implication for aaoi-2026, coherent-2026 and lumentum-dependent modules is that part of the InP supply behind the 800G/1.6T ramp is licensed quarter by quarter.

## 12. Conclusion <!-- report-module:conclusion -->

**(1) The question this report answers on the AI infrastructure chain.** AXT carries the `common-constraint` role in the optical layer. The shared constraint is InP substrate supply for the lasers and photodetectors in 800G/1.6T modules, which spans aaoi-2026, coherent-2026 and innolight-2026 rather than any one of them. The 2026 answer is **yes, it is binding, on two layers**: physical (backlog beyond two quarters, capacity targets raised in one quarter, three customers prepaying) and regulatory (every AXT wafer leaves China under a MOFCOM permit, and North American InP revenue is effectively zero). For the module reports this means part of the input to the 800G/1.6T ramp is licensed quarter by quarter. **Update (2026-11-02):** The answer is stronger on the physical layer: backlog above $500M, lead times of 9+ months, every customer on allocation (call); the regulatory layer has no new InP-specific measure, but there is still no evidence of US permits.
