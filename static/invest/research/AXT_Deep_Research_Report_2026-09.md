## Executive Summary <!-- report-module:overview -->

**One-line thesis:** AXT is one of three primary suppliers of indium phosphide (InP) substrates — the starting wafer for the EML lasers and photodetectors inside 800G/1.6T optical modules — and 2026 has turned it from a loss-making compound-semiconductor wafer maker into a supply-constrained one: Q2 revenue $47.6M (+165% Y/Y), GAAP gross margin 44.9%, InP revenue $30.7M (call), and a call-level Q3 revenue floor of about $66M. But every wafer is grown in China and needs a per-customer export permit from China's Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM), and at the 2026-09-23 close of $72.98 the market already prices roughly the company's own exit-2027 capacity target, sold in full at about 50% gross margin.

**Investment judgment:**
- **Stance:** cautious
- **Confidence:** medium — the demand, the customer prepayments and the balance sheet are in primary filings; the negative skew comes from an exit multiple and a capacity path that exist today only as call-level targets, and from a regulator that already cut one quarter's revenue (Q4 2025)
- **Key catalysts:** Q3 2026 results (expected late October 2026, date not announced by the issuer; Q3 2025 was reported 2025-10-30) against the call-level $66M floor and $0.30-$0.32 non-GAAP EPS; the exit-2026 InP capacity target of about $60M per quarter (call); the first US InP export permit; receipt of Lumentum's $43.5M deposit; Tongmei's Hong Kong filing
- **Monitoring window:** 12-18 months (through the exit-2027 capacity target of about $130M per quarter)

This report carries the `common-constraint` role for the optical layer. The question it answers is whether InP substrate supply becomes a shared constraint on the 800G/1.6T ramp across aaoi-2026 (modules), coherent-2026 (a direct AXT customer and an InP device maker) and innolight-2026 (module architecture check). The 2026 evidence says **yes, and the constraint has two layers**: physical capacity (backlog "well over $100 million" that "exceeds 2 quarters", call; capacity targets raised in one quarter from about $35M to $60M per quarter for exit-2026) and regulatory permission (China's MOFCOM licenses each export; North America was $161K of Q2 revenue). Customers are paying to jump the queue: Coherent agreed a $22.3M prepayment, Casela committed RMB 173M with 50% up front, and Lumentum agreed to two $43.5M deposits.

Valuation decides the stance. At $72.98, subtracting $9.58 per share of net cash (on the 67.79M fully diluted count) leaves $63.40 of operating value; at a 28x exit multiple that needs about $2.26 per share of attributable after-tax operating earnings, or about $234M of EBIT — for example, FY2028 revenue of roughly $620M at a 50% gross margin, which is the company's own exit-2027 InP capacity target sold in full. A 30 / 40 / 30 scenario grid weights to **$55.86** per share, **-11.1% annualized** over 2.27 years to 2028-12-31, 19.1 points below an 8% required return. The bull case alone returns **+16.6% annualized**, so the stance is **cautious**, not bearish-avoid. The hypothesis that AXTI's outperformance against AAOI reflects a better financing model is tested in §5: the answer is **partly on the shape of the dilution, not on its size, and mostly on per-share results**.

## 1. Business Overview and Unit Economics <!-- report-module:business -->

AXT Inc. (Fremont, California; incorporated in Delaware, founded 1986, public since 1998) makes compound and single-element semiconductor substrates — InP, gallium arsenide (GaAs) and germanium (Ge) — and, through consolidated raw-material subsidiaries, sells high-purity gallium, pyrolytic boron nitride (pBN) crucibles, boron oxide and related materials. **All substrates and raw materials are manufactured in China** by its subsidiary Beijing Tongmei Xtal Technology (Tongmei) at Beijing (InP crystal growth and wafering), Dingxing (GaAs wafer processing) and Kazuo (GaAs and Ge crystal growth), plus a network of raw-material companies. AXT sells to epitaxy houses and device makers; it does not grow epitaxial layers. It reports one segment, disaggregated into Substrates and Raw Materials and Other.

**Ownership basis warning.** AXT owns about 85.5% of Tongmei at 2026-06-30: noncontrolling and redeemable noncontrolling interests together are about 14.5% (10-Q Note 7). On 2026-07-23 AXT agreed to buy 5,135,475 Tongmei shares for RMB 40,621,600 (about $6.0M) from Bomeilian, a related party, which would take it to about 86.09%; the purchase had not closed at the 10-Q date. Every revenue and margin figure below is consolidated; about 14% of Tongmei's profit belongs to others.

## 2. Industry and Competitive Position <!-- report-module:competition -->

InP substrates sit at the very start of the optical chain: an epitaxy house grows laser or photodetector layers on the wafer, a device maker (Coherent, Lumentum, Chinese makers such as Casela) builds EMLs, CW lasers for silicon photonics and photodetectors, and module makers (Innolight, AAOI, Coherent itself) assemble transceivers. The CEO describes the near-term driver as "the industry migration to 100G [transcript as written; read as 800G] and 1.6T transceiver modules. For which indium phosphide based lasers and detectors are essential" and the longer-term driver as near- and co-packaged optics (call). AXT says "there are only three primary suppliers, including AXT" (10-K) and names its substrate competitors as Sumitomo, JX, Freiberger, Umicore, Vital and CCTC (10-K risk factors; the list spans GaAs and Ge as well as InP).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| AXT (Tongmei) | China (Beijing) | One of three primary InP suppliers (10-K); claims "lowest defect densities" and a lead in 6-inch | MOFCOM per-customer export permits since 2025-02-04; US tariffs | Only supplier with owned raw-material subsidiaries; capacity targets raised to about $60M per quarter exit-2026 (call) |

| Freiberger, Umicore, Vital, CCTC | Germany, Belgium, China | Named as substrate competitors (10-K); InP roles not separated | Vital and CCTC appear to be China-based (this report's identification, not the 10-K's); if so, the same permit regime applies | — |
| Captive InP (device makers) | Various | Not disclosed in AXT filings | — | Whether coherent-2026 or others grow their own InP substrates is not established here |

**What the moat is, stated both ways.** In favor: InP crystal growth is a proprietary-process business with few qualified suppliers; customers are prepaying for capacity (three agreements in seven weeks), which they rarely do for a commodity; and AXT is the one supplier whose raw-material chain (gallium, InP starting material, pBN crucibles, quartz) is inside its own group. Against: the two Japanese suppliers sit outside China's licensing regime, so every MOFCOM delay is an argument for customers to dual-source away from AXT; the capacity metric AXT reports is dollars, so "tripling" includes price; and the three agreements are with only three customers while the top five customers were 30% of Q2 revenue (no customer above 10%).

**Market reaction as a revealed preference.** On 2026-07-29 both stocks fell about 13%; on 2026-07-30, before AXT's release that evening and a day after the Lumentum 8-K (filed 2026-07-29), AXTI rose 27.0% ($36.97 to $46.94) and AAOI 17.8%. On 2026-07-31, the first session after the Q2 release, AXTI rose another 28.7% (to $60.43) while AAOI rose 4.7% — the AXT-specific move is that second day. At 2026-09-23 the market pays AXT 33.0x EV/TTM revenue against AAOI's 13.9x and 15.7x the call-level Q3 floor annualized against AAOI's 7.6x its annualized Q3 guidance midpoint.

## 3. Export Licensing, Tariffs and Geopolitics <!-- report-module:competition -->

This is the section on which the thesis turns, and it cuts both ways: **China's licensing regime is simultaneously AXT's pricing lever (the substrate is scarce and controlled) and a lever over AXT itself (a regulator decides which customer receives which order, and when).**

### Timeline (10-Q and 10-K unless stated)

| Date | Event | Effect on AXT |
|---|---|---|
| 2018-09 | US Section 301 tariffs on Chinese goods, including AXT's wafer substrates, at 25% | Cost of selling into the US |
| 2023-08-01 | China requires export permits for GaAs and Ge (gallium and germanium controls announced 2023-07-03) | GaAs permits to US customers "considered 'dual use'" have **never been approved** |
| 2024-12-03 | China tightens exports of antimony, gallium, germanium and superhard materials | — |
| 2025-01-01 / 02-01 / 03-04 | US tariffs on AXT's substrates rise to 50%, 60% and 70% | North America falls from 10% of revenue (2023) to 8% (2024) and 2% (2025); tariffs paid $1.0M / $1.0M / $0.3M |
| **2025-02-04** | **InP substrates added to China's export control list**; all three AXT substrate families now need MOFCOM permits | "InP export permits represent the most significant challenge we currently face" |
| 2025-03 | MOFCOM permit portal opens | Applications submitted immediately |
| 2025-06-11 | First InP export permits, for "certain customers in Europe and Japan" | Q3 2025 InP revenue up "more than 250 percent sequentially" (release) |
| 2026-01-08 | Q4 2025 revenue pre-announced at $22.5M-$23.5M, "primarily due to fewer export control permits for indium phosphide being issued by China's Ministry of Commerce than previously expected" | Q4 2025 revenue $23.0M against Q3 $28.0M — **the regulator's control demonstrated in the P&L** |
| 2026-02-20 / 02-24 | US Supreme Court invalidates IEEPA tariffs; CBP ends them | Section 301 and 232 tariffs remain; replacement tariffs possible |
| 2026-03-01 | Revised PRC Foreign Trade Law takes effect, widening MOFCOM powers to restrict exports for security, shortage, industrial-policy and other reasons | AXT "do[es] not believe" it adds effects beyond current licensing; "no assurances" |
| 2026-Q2 | "Additional export approvals granted by the China government" drive substrate revenue +233% Y/Y | North America still $161K of Q2 revenue |

**What is known and what is not.** Known: AXT "continue[s] to receive permits" but has "a backlog of orders for which we have not yet received permits" and "cannot predict when a permit application will be reviewed and approved"; permits to US InP customers have not been granted and the company "can reasonably expect" that they "will eventually be granted" because InP "is rarely used in military applications" (10-Q). Not known: which customers hold permits, what share of backlog is unpermitted, and — for the two US-domiciled agreements (Coherent, Lumentum) — to which locations the wafers are to be delivered; the 8-Ks do not say, and this report does not infer it. The Q3 floor was phrased around exactly this boundary: $66M "for which we either already have a permit to ship or for which an export permit is not required", with "significant upside ... should we receive permits for additional orders which we have the inventory to support" (call).

**Why it is also a lever for AXT.** Because licensing slows every China-origin shipment, customers who need InP now are signing prepaid, take-or-pay contracts; the CEO links the favorable pricing to diameter mix, and the capacity-in-dollars targets rose faster than the physical capacity. A licensing regime that makes Chinese InP scarce outside China helps a China-based supplier's price — as long as its own permits keep flowing.

**Cross-check framing.** The chain rule `export-control-escalation` in the coverage map is written for US listings of Chinese suppliers; AXT is the mirror case — Chinese export licensing of a Chinese supplier's output to non-Chinese customers. The observable evidence the rule asks for (denied licences, delivery slippage) exists here as the Q4 2025 shortfall and as the absence of North American InP revenue. The chain-level implication for aaoi-2026, coherent-2026 and lumentum-dependent modules is that part of the InP supply behind the 800G/1.6T ramp is licensed quarter by quarter.

## 5. Financing Model Compared with AAOI <!-- report-module:financial -->

## 7. Bull Case <!-- report-module:bullBear -->

1. **The constraint is real and customers are paying for it.** Backlog is "well over $100 million" and "exceeds 2 quarters" (call); three customers committed prepaid or take-or-pay contracts in seven weeks (Casela RMB 173M, Coherent $22.29M, Lumentum 2 x $43.5M), and long-term-agreement remaining performance obligations were $98.5M at 2026-06-30. Device makers do not prepay for substrates they can buy elsewhere.

2. **The capacity path was raised, and the Q2 print beat the previous call's floor.** The Q1 call said about $34M was realizable in Q2; Q2 revenue was $47.6M. The Q2 call raised exit-2026 capacity from about $35M to about $60M per quarter and exit-2027 from $65-70M to about $130M (call). If capacity is sold, FY2028 InP revenue could approach $520M.

3. **Operating leverage and margin have already shown up.** GAAP gross margin went from 8.0% to 44.9% in four quarters; Q2 incremental gross margin was 67%; management's gross-margin target "begins with a 4" and, with volume and diameter mix, the CFO said "we should definitely be targeting a number that begins with a 5" (call).

4. **The balance sheet is paid for.** Net cash of $649.1M ($9.90 per share) covers the analyst-restated $100M 2027 capex and the ~$49M Tongmei redemption right several times; the next capacity step does not require more equity.

5. **Licensing can become a tailwind.** The first US InP permit would open the largest end market ("our material are being used in multiple U.S. hyperscalers", call), and a regime that makes China-origin InP scarce outside China supports price.

**Valuation implication of the bull case:** FY2028 revenue $620M (InP near $130M per quarter, plus about $100M of GaAs, Ge and raw materials), 55% gross margin, $80M of operating expense, 18% tax, 85% attributable, 67.79M shares → $2.68 per share of attributable operating earnings; at 35x plus $9.58 of net cash per share, **$103.50** — +41.8% total, **+16.6% annualized** to 2028-12-31.

## 8. Bear Case <!-- report-module:bullBear -->

1. **One regulator can cut a quarter.** Q4 2025 revenue fell to $23.0M from $28.0M "primarily due to fewer export control permits"; the Foreign Trade Law revision effective 2026-03-01 widened MOFCOM's powers; US InP permits have never been granted. Every dollar of the $66M Q3 floor is conditional on permits already in hand.

2. **The market already pays for the exit-2027 target.** Net of cash, $63.40 per share of operating value at 28x needs about $234M of EBIT — roughly FY2028 revenue of $620M at 50% gross margin, the full exit-2027 capacity target sold. The base case below reaches $151M.

3. **The capacity metric is in dollars.** Capacity "per quarter" is revenue capacity, so price and diameter mix are inside it; a price normalization when Sumitomo and JX add capacity would shrink "capacity" without any tool being removed.

4. **Dilution is ongoing at two levels.** AXT's share count rose 39.2% from before the December offering; authorized shares were raised to 120M; the quorum threshold was cut; and a Hong Kong listing of Tongmei would dilute AXT inside its only operating subsidiary. The RMB 324.4M redemption right is a contingent claim on cash.

5. **Cash flow has not arrived.** H1 operating cash flow was -$0.9M despite $11.5M of net income, because receivables, inventory and prepaid items absorbed $31.4M; inventory is about 335 days of annualized Q2 cost of revenue. Without $12.0M of customer advances, H1 operating cash flow would have been about -$12.9M.

6. **Insiders sold into the rally.** About $29.8M of discretionary sales in June at around $110, none under a 10b5-1 plan box, some inside the 60-day lock-up window, no purchases.

**Valuation implication of the bear case:** FY2028 revenue $250M (the Q3 floor annualized, no growth after a permit or price reset), 38% gross margin, $55M of operating expense, 75% attributable, net cash reduced by the ~$49M redemption → $0.36 per share of attributable operating earnings; at 18x plus $8.85 of net cash, **$15.38** — -78.9% total, -49.6% annualized. It is not a return to 2025: revenue is still 2.8x FY2025's $88.3M.

## 9. Key Uncertainties and Thesis-Breaking Conditions <!-- report-module:uncertainties -->

**The core uncertainty is not whether InP is scarce — it is — but how much of AXT's share of that scarcity survives a licensing regime it does not control, and what multiple the market pays when capacity arrives.** By §10, earning 8% annualized from $72.98 on the base-case FY2028 path requires the market to pay **52.9x** attributable operating earnings at the end of 2028.

The unknowns, each with a confirmation point:

1. **Permits.** Known: the Q3 floor counts only permitted or permit-exempt orders; North America was $161K of Q2 revenue. Unknown: the unpermitted share of backlog and the US timetable. Confirmation: Q3 revenue against $66M (late October 2026) and the first North American InP revenue.
2. **Capacity versus pricing.** Known: dollar capacity targets of about $60M per quarter (exit-2026) and about $130M (exit-2027) (call). Unknown: how much is wafers versus price. Confirmation: Q4 2026 InP revenue (call) against $60M, and any disclosure of wafer volume.
3. **Gross margin.** Known: 44.9% in Q2. Unknown: whether 50%+ is reachable while capacity doubles. Confirmation: Q3 and Q4 2026 gross margin.
4. **Cash deployment.** Known: H1 capex $8.6M; $303.6M in securities. Unknown: 2026-2027 capex (the $14M and $100M figures are analyst-restated). Confirmation: the Q3 and Q4 cash-flow statements.
5. **Tongmei's cap table.** Known: ~14.5% minority; RMB 324.4M redemption right; no HKEX filing at 2026-09-24. Unknown: redemption elections, IPO size and timing. Confirmation: 10-Q Note 18 and HKEXnews.
6. **Customer deposits.** Known: $12.7M received at 06-30; Lumentum's $43.5M due within 30 business days of 2026-07-26. Unknown: whether Coherent's $22.29M and Lumentum's first deposit were received. Confirmation: Q3 10-Q contract liabilities.

**Thesis-breaking conditions (any one moves the stance to bearish-avoid):**
- Q3 2026 revenue below $60M, or two consecutive sequential revenue declines, with the company attributing either to export permits
- Any new MOFCOM restriction specific to InP, or loss of permits for Europe or Japan
- GAAP gross margin below 40% for two consecutive quarters
- Withdrawal of the exit-2026 capacity target of about $60M per quarter, or Q4 2026 InP revenue below $45M (call)
- Termination or refund of the Coherent or Lumentum agreements, or current contract liabilities falling while revenue also falls
- Full redemption of the RMB 324.4M PE stake combined with a Hong Kong offering that cuts AXT below 75% of Tongmei

**Upgrade conditions (to neutral-watch or constructive):**
- Two consecutive quarters of revenue at or above $80M with GAAP gross margin at or above 50%
- First US InP export permits with North American revenue above 5% of a quarter
- Or a price near $50, around the base scenario's value, without a change in fundamentals

## 12. Conclusion <!-- report-module:conclusion -->

**(1) The question this report answers on the AI infrastructure chain.** AXT carries the `common-constraint` role in the optical layer. The shared constraint is InP substrate supply for the lasers and photodetectors in 800G/1.6T modules, which spans aaoi-2026, coherent-2026 and innolight-2026 rather than any one of them. The 2026 answer is **yes, it is binding, on two layers**: physical (backlog beyond two quarters, capacity targets raised in one quarter, three customers prepaying) and regulatory (every AXT wafer leaves China under a MOFCOM permit, and North American InP revenue is effectively zero). For the module reports this means part of the input to the 800G/1.6T ramp is licensed quarter by quarter.

**(2) The financing question.** AXT and AAOI both financed 2026 mainly with equity, about 12% of market value each, and AXT diluted faster. What differs is form (one discounted block against a standing ATM), deployment ($8.6M of capex against about $625M) and, above all, per-share results (revenue per share +53.9% Q/Q at AXT against +18.4% at AAOI). The price ratio moved mainly on AXT's Q2 print and on AAOI's new ATM, not on who funds capacity.

**(3) The expectation gap.** At $72.98 the market prices FY2028 revenue near $620M at a 50% gross margin — the exit-2027 target, fully sold and permitted. The base case reaches $450M.

**(4) Stance and confidence.** The 30 / 40 / 30 grid weights to $55.86, **-11.1% annualized**, 19.1 points below 8%; +4x on every exit multiple gives -7.0%, and adding revenue and margin as well gives +0.6%. The bull case alone returns +16.6% and the business has already inflected, so the stance is **cautious / medium conviction**, not bearish-avoid. Confidence is medium because the decisive variables — permits and the capacity path — are regulator- and call-level, not reported.

**(5) Upgrade and downgrade triggers.** Upgrade: two quarters of revenue at or above $80M with gross margin at or above 50%; first US InP permits with North America above 5% of revenue; or a price near $50 without a change in fundamentals. Downgrade to bearish-avoid: Q3 revenue below $60M or two sequential declines attributed to permits; a new InP-specific MOFCOM measure; gross margin below 40% for two quarters; the exit-2026 capacity target withdrawn or Q4 InP revenue below $45M; termination or refund of the Coherent or Lumentum agreements; or AXT falling below 75% of Tongmei.

AXT has the scarce input, the customers queueing for it and the cash to expand it. It does not control the permit that turns a wafer into revenue, and the market is already paying for the capacity it has promised to build by the end of 2027.
