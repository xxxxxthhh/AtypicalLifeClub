## 2. Business Overview and Revenue Structure <!-- report-module:business -->

**What AppLovin is after the Apps sale.** The company describes itself as a provider of "end-to-end advertising solutions" (Q2 2026 release boilerplate). After the sale there is **one operating segment** (10-Q Note 11), and revenue is "substantially all" from AppLovin Ads.

- **AppLovin Ads:** the advertiser-facing product. It was renamed in June 2026 from "Axon Ads Manager"; "Axon" remains the name of the AI recommendation engine.
- **MAX:** the in-app mediation and auction layer used by publishers. MAX gives AppLovin a view of the auction it competes in, which is both its central advantage and the subject of the Unity dispute (§3).
- **Adjust:** a measurement and attribution business.

The economic engine is simple to state. Axon predicts, impression by impression, which user is most likely to install an app or make a purchase, and bids accordingly. The better the predictions, the more an advertiser can afford to pay per install, and the more of that spread AppLovin keeps.

## 3. Industry, Competition and Moat <!-- report-module:competition -->

**Industry backdrop.** Morgan Stanley (2026-09-14) sizes the mobile-app advertising market at about $80B, and argues it is large enough for both AppLovin and Unity to grow. AppLovin's own risk factors state the concentration plainly: revenue is concentrated in the mobile app ecosystem, "specifically mobile gaming". The CEO said on the Q2 call that gaming "is still the majority of our revenue". The structural question for 2026-2028 is whether AppLovin is a gaming-ad monopoly approaching its ceiling or a general performance-advertising platform at the start of a second curve. **No disclosed number answers that today.**

AppLovin grows about twice as fast as Meta and Alphabet with a far higher margin, yet trades at a lower trailing P/E than Meta and only 27% above Alphabet (2026-10-01 historical comparison). **That gap is the market's verdict that APP's growth is less durable, not a mispricing that is obvious on its face.**

**Moat assessment, both sides:**

- **What holds up:**
  - **Data scale through MAX.** Mediation sits on the auction and sees the bids. BofA (2026-08-11) estimates APP's relative share at about twice the next competitor.
  - **Model quality, measured in advertisers' wallets.** NRPI rose 72% in FY2025, a price advertisers paid because returns justified it.
  - **Cost structure.** Datacenter costs were $181.0M in Q2 2026, 9.4% of revenue. Opex excluding cost of revenue was $203.6M against $1,923.7M of revenue, producing a 77.7% GAAP operating margin that no listed peer approaches.
  - **Capital-light reinvestment.** Capex was $1.4M in Q2.
- **What does not hold up as well as it did a year ago:**
  - **Rival models are getting good.** Unity's strategic Grow is now growing faster (+63%) than APP (+53%), and the Unity dispute itself is AppLovin's own admission that it sees Unity's models as a threat worth litigating.
  - **Platforms are adjacent competitors.** Meta and Google are both "significant partners" and competitors (10-Q risk factors), and each launched an AI game-building tool in 2026.
  - **The mediation layer is contestable.** CloudX was built by the people who built MoPub and MAX.
  - **The new engine depends on a tool that has slipped.** The company's own March blog targeted broader AI video for "early Q2"; in August it was still "a work in progress". The moat in gaming is real. The moat in e-commerce is still a hypothesis.

## 10. Conclusion <!-- report-module:conclusion -->

**(1) What this report answers.** AppLovin is not an AI-infrastructure company, and this report sits outside the book's chain layers. The question it answers is how to value a business with an 84% EBITDA margin when the market has decided its growth is no longer self-compounding. The analytical spine is the 10-Q's own decomposition. From mid-2025 to Q2 2026, revenue growth came entirely from net revenue per install, on flat-to-shrinking volume; in Q3 2026 installs grew 2%. The question is therefore not "is AppLovin a good business" — it plainly is — but **how long monetization intensity can keep compounding before competitors and the gaming ceiling cap it, and whether e-commerce arrives before it does.**

**(2) The expectations gap in one sentence (updated 2026-11-06).** At $356.10, earning the 8% hurdle requires revenue growth of about 20% a year from 2026 to 2028 at a 13x exit (holding value needs about 10.5%), or about 35% at a 10x exit. Our central case is 21% at 13x. The price now asks for roughly our central case.

**(3) Stance and conviction, derived from the scenario weights (updated 2026-11-06).**
- **The grid:** the rebuilt 30/40/30 grid (optimistic $605.82 / central $427.59 / pessimistic $189.24) weights to **$409.55 per share, +15.0% against $356.10, or +6.7% annualized** over 2.152 years. That is 1.3 points below the 8% hurdle. The central case alone returns +8.9% a year, at the hurdle; the optimistic case is +70.1% and the pessimistic case −46.9%. Hence **stance: neutral-watch**.
- **Why neutral-watch and not constructive:** constructive in this book means a positive skew at today's price, neutral-watch a balanced skew after the scenario work, and cautious a negative skew. At $356.10 the weighted return does not clear the hurdle, and it would need a close at or below about $322 to reach 12%. In this book's published stances as of 2026-11-06, the constructive reports that state a weighted return sit at +12.7% to +14.2% (Spotify, Reddit, PayPal, all on 2026-10-01 anchors), and the neutral-watch reports at +2.8% to +9.5% (AXT, Lumentum, the SMH benchmark); Netflix is labelled neutral-watch at +14.7% because a pre-committed downgrade leg fired, so its label was not set by its return and is not used as a reference here. +6.7% sits inside the neutral-watch range.
- **Why neutral-watch and not cautious:** cautious means negative skew. The weighted value is still 15.0% above the price, the central case is at the hurdle, and the balance sheet is close to unlevered (0.06x net debt to EBITDA). The cautious reports that state a weighted return sit at +5.5% (Airbnb) and below; the gap from +6.7% is small, which is one more reason conviction is low. The price would have to rise above about $410 for the weighted return to turn negative.
- **Why conviction is low:**
  - (a) Every main sensitivity falls below the 8% hurdle and two turn negative: −3 turns −4.5%, slower growth −1.5%, a 20/40/40 weighting +1.5%. In the other direction the label rests on holding the 30% pessimistic weight: moving 5 points from pessimistic to optimistic (35/40/25) gives +9.2%, above the hurdle, and 40/40/20 gives +11.6%.
  - (b) The growth driver is opaque. There is no revenue split, the model rollout is partial with no stated share, and the consumer figures are call statements from a third-party transcript.
  - (c) The largest risks are outside the grid: platform enforcement, the Unity dispute, two securities class actions and two sets of derivative suits.
- **What changed and what did not:** no published downgrade leg tripped; Q3 was strong on every disclosed measure. The stance moved because the price rose 26.6% (from the historical $281.31 to $356.10) while the weighted value rose 4.4% (from the historical $392.41 to $409.55). This follows the book's policy of re-deriving stances at the current price.
