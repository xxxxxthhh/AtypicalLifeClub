## 1. Executive Summary and Current View <!-- report-module:overview -->

**One-line thesis:** AppLovin is still one of the most profitable growth businesses in listed technology. Q2 2026 revenue was $1,923.7M (+53% year over year) at an 83.9% adjusted EBITDA margin, and trailing-twelve-month free cash flow was $4.51B. Yet the stock has fallen 58.25% this year, to $281.31, because the market has stopped believing the growth engine compounds on its own. Every dollar of growth in the 10-Q comes from monetization per install (net revenue per installation +58% in Q2) on flat-to-shrinking install volume (−2%). The tool that was supposed to open a second engine, e-commerce advertising through generative AI video creatives, is still "a work in progress". At $281.31 the shares trade at 16.5x trailing adjusted EBITDA and 21.6x trailing GAAP earnings. **On a 13x exit multiple, earning the 8% hurdle from here requires revenue growth of only about 10% a year from 2026 to 2028**, against a base case of 21%; merely holding value requires about 1%. The disagreement with the market is therefore mostly about the exit multiple, not the next quarter.

**Investment view:**

- **Stance:** constructive
- **Conviction:** low. The probability-weighted arithmetic clears the hurdle by a wide margin at central assumptions, with +16.0% annualized against an 8% hurdle. But it is multiple-fragile: cutting every exit multiple by three turns drops it to +3.8%, and slower growth alone takes it to +7.1%. Three inputs are also weaker than this book normally requires:
  - **Undisclosed revenue split.** There is no gaming-versus-e-commerce revenue split anywhere in the filings.
  - **Unobservable growth driver.** Growth depends on a model-improvement cadence that outsiders cannot observe.
  - **Tail risks outside the grid.** Platform enforcement and litigation risks are not in the scenario grid.
- **Key catalysts:**
  - The Q3 2026 print in early November (estimated, not yet announced), against $2,055-2,085M guidance.
  - The Q4 guide, which will show whether the Edgewater "+8-9% q/q" deceleration view is right.
  - The outcome of AppLovin v. Unity.
  - The ruling on the motion to dismiss in the 2025 securities class action.
- **Monitoring window:** 3-15 months.
- **This is a valuation-and-skew judgment, not a claim that the business is undamaged.** Growth has decelerated for four straight reported quarters (five counting the Q3 guide), and competition is visibly sharper (Unity Vector, Meta, Google, CloudX). Insiders have sold about $363M this year with zero open-market purchases. The constructive call rests on the price already discounting most of that, not on dismissing it.

## 2. Business Overview and Revenue Structure <!-- report-module:business -->

**What AppLovin is after the Apps sale.** The company describes itself as a provider of "end-to-end advertising solutions" (Q2 2026 release boilerplate). After the sale there is **one operating segment** (10-Q Note 11), and revenue is "substantially all" from AppLovin Ads.

- **AppLovin Ads:** the advertiser-facing product. It was renamed in June 2026 from "Axon Ads Manager"; "Axon" remains the name of the AI recommendation engine.
- **MAX:** the in-app mediation and auction layer used by publishers. MAX gives AppLovin a view of the auction it competes in, which is both its central advantage and the subject of the Unity dispute (§3).
- **Adjust:** a measurement and attribution business.

The economic engine is simple to state. Axon predicts, impression by impression, which user is most likely to install an app or make a purchase, and bids accordingly. The better the predictions, the more an advertiser can afford to pay per install, and the more of that spread AppLovin keeps.

**The read-across:** since mid-2025, every incremental dollar of revenue has come from earning more per install on flat-to-falling volume. That is consistent with management's explanation that better models raise advertisers' return on spend and therefore their bids. It is also consistent with the bear reading from Edgewater Research (2026-09-23): MAX's share has reached "a functional ceiling" and "competition is compressing APP's net revenue spreads". In the first reading, NRPI growth is a durable product of model quality. In the second, it is a stock of pricing power being drawn down. The filings cannot distinguish the two. **What they do show is that in Q2 2026 NRPI growth slowed from +93% to +58% while volume did not recover**, which is why the deceleration from 59% to 53% revenue growth landed so hard.

## 3. Industry, Competition and Moat <!-- report-module:competition -->

**Industry backdrop.** Morgan Stanley (2026-09-14) sizes the mobile-app advertising market at about $80B, and argues it is large enough for both AppLovin and Unity to grow. AppLovin's own risk factors state the concentration plainly: revenue is concentrated in the mobile app ecosystem, "specifically mobile gaming". The CEO said on the Q2 call that gaming "is still the majority of our revenue". The structural question for 2026-2028 is whether AppLovin is a gaming-ad monopoly approaching its ceiling or a general performance-advertising platform at the start of a second curve. **No disclosed number answers that today.**

AppLovin grows about twice as fast as Meta and Alphabet with a far higher margin, yet trades at a lower trailing P/E than Meta and only 27% above Alphabet. **That gap is the market's verdict that APP's growth is less durable, not a mispricing that is obvious on its face.**

**Moat assessment, both sides:**

- **What holds up:**
  - **Data scale through MAX.** Mediation sits on the auction and sees the bids. BofA (2026-08-11) estimates APP's relative share at about twice the next competitor.
  - **Model quality, measured in advertisers' wallets.** NRPI rose 72% in FY2025, a price advertisers paid because returns justified it.
  - **Cost structure.** Datacenter costs were $181.0M in Q2 2026, 9.4% of revenue. Opex excluding cost of revenue was $203.6M against $1,923.7M of revenue, producing a 77.7% GAAP operating margin that no listed peer approaches.
  - **Capital-light reinvestment.** Capex was $1.4M in Q2.
- **What does not hold up as well as it did a year ago:**
  - **Rival models are getting good.** Unity's strategic Grow is now growing faster (+63%) than APP (+53%), and the Unity dispute itself is AppLovin's own admission that it sees Unity's models as a threat worth litigating.
  - **Platforms are adjacent competitors.** Meta and Google are both "significant partners" and competitors (10-Q risk factors), and each launched an AI game-building tool in 2026.
  - **The mediation layer is contestable.** CloudX was built by the people who built MoPub and MAX.
  - **The new engine depends on a tool that has slipped.** The company's own March blog targeted broader AI video for "early Q2"; in August it was still "a work in progress". The moat in gaming is real. The moat in e-commerce is still a hypothesis.

**AppLovin v. Unity (the live competitive and legal event):**

| Date | Step | Detail |
|------|------|--------|
| 2026-08-21 | Cease-and-desist letter | AppLovin alleges Unity's Ad Quality SDK, bundled in LevelPlay SDK 8.9.0 (2025-05-28), collects per-impression revenue, waterfall data, creatives, device identifiers and touch coordinates from impressions AppLovin wins via MAX |
| 2026-09-11 | Unity offer | Unity offered to disable collection within five business days, conditional on the dispute being treated as resolved; AppLovin did not accept and moved to arbitration and court |
| 2026-09-27 | JAMS arbitration filed | Breach of contract, interference with contractual relations and prospective economic advantage, trade-secret misappropriation, California UCL §17200 |
| 2026-09-29 | Temporary restraining order application | San Francisco Superior Court, case CPF-26-520212; seeks a stop to collection within five business days and SDK changes within 30 days |
| 2026-09-30 | TRO hearing (scheduled) | 11 a.m. PT before Judge Joseph M. Quinn, Dept. 302. **Outcome not found as of this report** |
| Unity's response | "a classic case of a dominant incumbent resorting to litigation and intimidation to address increased competition and slowing growth"; "Ad Quality does not collect any data from AppLovin" | ppc.land, Digiday |

**Conclusion:** we agree with management that the gaming business has a scale and model-quality advantage that is not disappearing. But we think the advantage is now **contested at the margin**, with rivals' growth rates above APP's and APP litigating to protect its auction data. A contested advantage is worth a lower multiple than an uncontested one, and that is what our exit multiples assume (§8).

## 6. Bull and Bear Cases <!-- report-module:bullBear -->

**Bull case (the 30% weight in the scenario grid)**

1. **The engine is not broken; the cadence slipped.** Q2's shortfall was $1.2M of EBITDA against the bottom of the guide. Management said the next model step-up "landed just after quarter end", and the Q3 guide of +46-48% excludes any further model releases. Morgan Stanley's read on 2026-08-06 was "short term misexecution, not structural headwinds". If Q3 lands at or above the $2,085M top, the "growth is over" narrative loses its main evidence.
2. **E-commerce is real, early, and not in the base numbers.** AppLovin Ads opened to all advertisers only on 2026-06-22, so Q2 contained a week of general availability. Citi/Store Leads counted 13,105 e-commerce clients by 2026-09-18, up from 10,471 on 2026-07-10. Management says consumer spend was 28% above the Q4 2025 peak, and the CEO has claimed "well over $70,000 a year" per new customer. Even a few points of the web performance-advertising market would be a second curve. The bull scenario needs 2027 growth of +32%, close to management's own "roughly 30%" compounding claim.
3. **The cash machine funds its own re-rating.** On base-case FCF, AppLovin generates about $15.1B of free cash flow from Q4 2026 through 2028, roughly 16% of today's market cap. At $281.31 the same buyback dollars retire about 68% more shares than they did in H1 2026.
4. **The multiple is already low for the quality.** At 21.6x trailing earnings, APP trades below Meta's 25.1x trailing P/E despite roughly twice the revenue growth and a far higher margin; on forward numbers it is 13.97x FY2026E EBITDA and 13.9x FY2027 consensus EPS. BofA called it "reasonable" at 17x CY2027 EBITDA as recently as July. The bull case assumes only that today's 16x trailing multiple holds through 2028.

**Upside-case valuation implication:**
- **Revenue:** FY2026 at $8,150M, then +32% in 2027 and +27% in 2028, to $13,663M in FY2028 (a 29.5% two-year CAGR).
- **Earnings:** an 82% EBITDA margin gives $11,203M of EBITDA.
- **Exit and per-share value:** at a 16x exit with 318.6M exit shares and about $6.35B of exit net cash, value per share is **$582.62**: +107.1%, or **+38.3% annualized** over 2.248 years.

**Bear case (the 30% weight in the scenario grid)**

1. **MAX has hit its ceiling and the spread is compressing.** Edgewater (2026-09-23) argues MAX's share has reached "a functional ceiling" and competition is compressing net revenue spreads. It calls August model validation "spotty" and models Q4 at +8-9% q/q. Install volume fell year over year in every quarter disclosed after Q2 2025 (−1%, −18%, −2%). When the NRPI gains slow, as they did from +93% to +58%, nothing replaces them.
2. **The competitors are now growing faster.** Unity's strategic Grow is +63%, guided to +68-70%. Vector passed a $1B run rate two quarters early. BofA's downgrade put it directly: "we need more evidence that it can grow 30% Y/Y, on a much higher base of revenue". Meta and Google own the distribution platforms and both launched AI game-creation tools in 2026.
3. **E-commerce is a 2027 story at best.** Wells Fargo's 2026-09-30 data says the pixel spike is a "false start": about 85% of new-pixel sites have no traffic. The gating technology, reliable AI video creative, slipped from "early Q2" to "a work in progress". Without a disclosed revenue figure, investors cannot verify the second curve. A 2026 class action now alleges the timing was misrepresented.
4. **Governance offers outsiders no protection.** Founder-controlled voting is at 66.9%. Insiders sold about $363M with no purchases; the CEO's sales are not under a 10b5-1 plan. The chair, CTO, chief legal officer and principal accounting officer changed within four months. Two securities class actions and a derivative suit are open, with no accrual.

**Downside-case valuation implication:**
- **Revenue:** FY2026 at $7,950M (Q4 only +2% q/q), +8% in 2027, then flat at $8,586M in 2028.
- **Earnings:** the EBITDA margin compresses to 76% as spreads narrow, giving $6,525M of EBITDA.
- **Exit and per-share value:** at an 8x exit, as for an ex-growth ad network, with 315.5M exit shares and about $3.99B of exit net cash, value per share is **$178.10**: −36.7%, or **−18.4% annualized**.

**What the bear case is not:** it is a competitive-erosion scenario, not a platform-enforcement scenario. If Apple or Google restricted how AppLovin's SDK collects or uses device and attribution data, revenue could fall far more than this grid assumes. **That tail is deliberately excluded from the grid and treated as a thesis-breaking event in §7**, because it is binary and not estimable from disclosed data. A reader who puts material probability on it should not rely on the weighted return in §8.

## 10. Conclusion <!-- report-module:conclusion -->

**(1) What this report answers.** AppLovin is not an AI-infrastructure company, and this report sits outside the book's chain layers. The question it answers is how to value a business with an 84% EBITDA margin when the market has decided its growth is no longer self-compounding. The analytical spine is the 10-Q's own decomposition. Since mid-2025, revenue growth has come entirely from net revenue per install, on flat-to-shrinking volume. The question is therefore not "is AppLovin a good business" — it plainly is — but **how long monetization intensity can keep compounding before competitors and the gaming ceiling cap it, and whether e-commerce arrives before it does.**

**(2) The expectations gap in one sentence.** At $281.31, earning the 8% hurdle requires revenue growth of about 10% a year from 2026 to 2028 at a 13x exit (holding value needs about 1%), or about 24% at a 10x exit. Our base case is 21% at 13x: we agree growth decelerates, but not to around 10% within two years at an 80%-plus margin.

**(3) Stance and conviction, derived from the scenario weights.**
- **The grid:** the 30/40/30 grid (bull $582.62 / base $410.48 / bear $178.10) weights to **$392.41 per share, +39.5% against $281.31, or +16.0% annualized** over 2.248 years. That is 8.0 points above the 8% hurdle. The skew is positive: +107% in the bull case against −37% in the bear case, with the base case at +18.3% annualized. Hence **stance: constructive**.
- **Why not bullish:** the skew is multiple-fragile. Three turns of exit-multiple compression take the weighted return to +3.8%. Slower growth alone (+12% base-case 2027) takes it to +7.1%. Both together give −4.1%.
- **Why conviction is low:**
  - (a) The fragility above.
  - (b) The growth driver is opaque. There is no revenue split, and the model-release cadence that management itself blames for Q2 is undisclosed.
  - (c) The largest risks are outside the grid: platform enforcement, the Unity dispute, and two securities class actions.
- **Why not neutral-watch:** at central assumptions the weighted return clears the hurdle by 8 points. The base case is set below consensus. The balance sheet is nearly unlevered. A neutral label would describe the uncertainty, not the arithmetic.

**This is a valuation-and-skew judgment, made after a 58% decline.** It does not dismiss the evidence of deceleration, competitive pressure and insider selling; it holds that the price at $281.31 already discounts most of it.

**(4) Upgrade and downgrade triggers (identical clause by clause to `stanceTriggers` in reports.json).**

- **Upgrade to bullish:** all of the following:
  - Q3 2026 revenue at or above the $2,085M top of guidance.
  - A Q4 guide midpoint implying at least +10% q/q.
  - The company disclosing a quantitative consumer or e-commerce revenue metric showing at least 10% of revenue.
  - *Brownback* dismissed, or the Unity dispute resolved without restricting MAX data.
- **Downgrade to neutral-watch:** any one of the following:
  - Q3 2026 revenue below the $2,055M low end of guidance, or a Q4 guide midpoint implying less than +5% q/q.
  - Adjusted EBITDA margin below 80% for two consecutive quarters.
  - NRPI growth below +20% y/y while install volume is still declining.
  - An Apple or Google platform-policy or enforcement action the company describes as material.
  - A ruling or counter-claim that restricts the use of MAX data.
  - A regulator's enforcement action against the company.
