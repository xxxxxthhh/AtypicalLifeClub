## 1. Executive Summary and Current View <!-- report-module:overview -->

**One-line thesis:** In twelve months Ondas used roughly $2 billion of equity issuance to buy itself from a company with $50.73M of annual revenue into a defense-autonomy group that management now guides to $525-550M — the orders are real (backlog of $613M at June 30, including the orders Mistral has captured under the **ceiling** of its $982M U.S. Army LUS IDIQ — the ceiling itself is not an order), but almost all of the revenue growth was purchased rather than grown (pro forma same-portfolio first-half growth was 112.4% against a reported 1,172.5%), and the full-year target requires fourth-quarter revenue of $251.1-261.1M, roughly tripling the $83.77M actually delivered in Q2 within two quarters. At $7.39 the shares already price a 2026-2029 revenue CAGR of 35.3%-55.6%; our base case is 30.0%.

**Investment view:**

- **Stance:** cautious
- **Conviction:** medium. The negative-skew arithmetic is very robust — widening both the exit multiples and the revenue paths to generous settings still leaves the probability-weighted annualized return between −0.9% and +1.0%, all below the hurdle. But the input quality is weaker than usual: backlog is a company-defined measure with no reconciliation to GAAP remaining performance obligations, and the acquired entity carrying most of the second-half ramp (DZYNE) cannot yet produce standalone historical financials.
- **Key catalysts:** the Q3 2026 print in mid-November (against $140-155M guidance — the first time the order book is tested by delivery), the fourth-quarter shipment cadence in late December, and the 44,999,998 deferred DZYNE consideration shares due 2027-01-04
- **Monitoring window:** 3-15 months
- **This is a valuation and capital-structure judgment, not a "the business is fake" judgment.** The orders, customers and products all verify against primary filings. The problem is that the price has pre-paid for an execution path materially more aggressive than our base case, while every round of growth simultaneously expands the denominator.

## 2. Business Overview and Revenue Structure <!-- report-module:business -->

**Business units (as described in the Q2 2026 10-Q):** the company organizes around two units. **Ondas Autonomous Systems (OAS)** covers unmanned and autonomous aerial and ground systems and integrated mission solutions for defense, homeland security, public safety and critical infrastructure, spanning Counter-Unmanned Aerial Systems (CUAS), aerial Intelligence, Surveillance and Reconnaissance (ISR), and Unmanned Ground Vehicles (UGV). **Ondas Capital** handles strategic investments, partnerships and capital formation. ## 3. Industry, Competition and Moat <!-- report-module:competition -->

**Industry backdrop.** The Israel-Middle East conflict and the war in Ukraine have moved small drones, counter-drone systems and autonomous effects from peripheral equipment to procurement priorities. Ondas's asset base is tightly concentrated on exactly that line: kinetic and protocol counter-UAS (Iron Drone Raider, Sentrycs CoRF), long-endurance ISR and strike (DZYNE ULTRA/IonStrike), unmanned ground vehicles (Robo-Team), and the propulsion that powers them (Rotron rotary engines, Apeiro motors). This is a market with **very strong demand and an extremely fragmented supply side** — and it is precisely that fragmentation that opened the window for Ondas to consolidate using its stock.

Ondas's TTM multiple sits alongside only Red Cat, the other micro-cap drone narrative stock, and is four to five times the multiples of two mature defense-electronics companies with billion-dollar revenue and visible cash flow. Using the FY2026 guidance midpoint to stretch the denominator to $537.5M brings P/S to 7.99x and EV/Sales to 6.50x — still roughly 35%-60% above AVAV and KTOS, at the cost of having to believe the guidance is delivered in full.

**Moat assessment, both sides:**

- **What holds up:** (a) **a position inside the Israeli defense supply chain** — Bird Aerosystems, Sentrycs, 4M, Omnisys, Apeiro, SPO, Insight and Gate/Bron are all Israeli or Eastern European targets whose capabilities are hard to replicate from scratch in the United States and are scarce in the current geopolitical environment; (b) **certification and program assets** — American Robotics' FAA Type Certificate and DZYNE's existing U.S. Air Force and Army program relationships are access rights that take years to earn; (c) **integration leverage in the sales motion** — bundling detection (Sentrycs), interception (Iron Drone), ISR (ULTRA) and ground systems (Robo-Team) into a "system of systems" genuinely is more attractive to government buyers than point suppliers, and that is the defensible core of management's "Core + Strategic" narrative.
- **What does not hold up:** (a) **most of the moat was bought, not grown** — at least thirteen acquisitions closed between August 2025 and September 2026, and goodwill went from $27.752M at 2025-06-30 to $661.362M at 2026-06-30 (with DZYNE's $493.460M still to be added in Q3); a portfolio that grew goodwill 24x in fourteen months has not had its synergies tested through a single full cycle; (b) **pricing power is unproven** — GAAP gross margin was 43.1% in Q2 2026 versus 53.1% in Q2 2025 (adjusted gross margin 50.4% versus 51.5%), so scale has not brought margin expansion; (c) **Customer A is still 40%** — inside a portfolio that is supposed to be diversified, one customer remains four-tenths of revenue and four-tenths of receivables, so concentration risk has not been diluted away by the deal-making; (d) **counter-UAS is a fast-iterating category in which every government is sponsoring domestic suppliers**, so today's interception approach may not be the standard in three years.

**Conclusion:** we agree with management on the demand-side industry call. On the supply side, we think what Ondas currently owns is a **combined channel-and-access advantage rather than a structural technology or cost moat**. That distinction is what argues for exit multiples converging toward AVAV and KTOS rather than staying near 20x EV/Sales.

## 6. Bull and Bear Cases <!-- report-module:bullBear -->

**Bull case (the 30% weight in the scenario grid)**

1. **The orders are verifiable and have names attached.** The orders Mistral has captured under the ceiling of its $982M U.S. Army LUS IDIQ (the ceiling is not an order, and the captured amount is undisclosed), the $140M combat engineering vehicles program, DZYNE's existing U.S. Air Force and Army relationships, Cyberhawk's PG&E and Shell customers — these are not TAM slides but specific contract vehicles readable in 8-Ks and the earnings release. Backlog of $613M is 1.14x the FY2026 guidance midpoint.
2. **Pro forma same-portfolio growth is still strong.** Stripping out the acquisition base effect, first-half growth was still 112.4%; management says Q2 pro forma organic growth was +85% and that the FY2026 midpoint implies ">30%" on that basis. In a window where demand is driven by active conflict and inventory replenishment across multiple countries, 30%-plus organic growth is sustainable.
3. **The balance sheet buys time to execute.** Pro forma net cash is roughly $934M against $6.4M of interest-bearing debt. At the Q2 operating burn rate of $86.081M, cash supports about ten quarters — while management guides platform-level adjusted EBITDA (OAS plus Ondas Sentinel) to break even in Q4 2026 and company-wide in Q4 2027. **Nothing forces the company's hand**, which is the key difference from most small-cap defense-technology names.
4. **The multiple has already compressed a great deal.** The shares are 47.3% below their 2026-01-08 closing high of $14.01, while revenue went from $30.1M in Q4 2025 to $83.8M in Q2 2026 (+178%). On the FY2026 guidance midpoint, EV/Sales is already down to 6.50x, narrowing the gap to AVAV's 4.73x and KTOS's 4.89x to 35%-60% — a premium that a delivered FY2026 plus another 40% in FY2027 could absorb.
5. **The warrant strikes are not near-term dilution but a long-dated cap.** At $7.39 the $20.00 and $28.00 strikes are deeply out of the money, and **in none of this report's three scenario exits are they in the money** (see §8). They therefore neither dilute nor deliver cash within the horizon; they simply hand part of the extreme upside to warrant holders.

**Valuation implication of the upside case:** if FY2026 lands at the $550M top of guidance and FY2029 revenue reaches $1,450M (**a 39.2% three-year CAGR measured from the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $537.5M**; measured instead from the $550M actual landing point it is 38.1%), exiting at 5.0x EV/Sales (slightly above AVAV/KTOS for the growth premium), with roughly 793M exit shares and about $700M of exit net cash, equity value per share is about $10.03 — 35.7% above the 2026-09-18 close of $7.39, or **+9.8% annualized** over 3.28 years. **Note that even the bull case only just reaches the low end of the 8%-10% required return.**

**Bear case (the 30% weight in the scenario grid)**

1. **The full-year guide demands an unusual back-end.** The FY2026 target of $525-550M against first-half revenue of $133.894M and Q3 guidance of $140-155M **implies fourth-quarter revenue of $251.1-261.1M** — a further 68.5%-79.4% sequential increase on Q3, and roughly a tripling of the $83.772M actually delivered in Q2 within two quarters. The second half must produce $391.1-416.1M, 2.92-3.11x the first half. Any slip in a delivery milestone breaks the guide, and the guide is the entire denominator supporting the current valuation.
2. **The ramp is carried by assets bought weeks ago with no standalone history.** DZYNE closed 2026-07-02, and the 10-Q states plainly that its historical results on a conforming basis "are not available" as of the filing date, with pro forma revenue and earnings deferred to the Q3 10-Q; Cyberhawk's initial accounting (closed 8/10) is likewise "incomplete," so the ASC 805 disclosures cannot be provided. **Investors are being asked to believe that an asset whose financial diligence disclosures are not yet finished will deliver the bulk of the second-half increment.**
3. **Unit economics have not been shown to scale.** Revenue went from $6.3M in Q2 2025 to $83.8M in Q2 2026 (13x), yet GAAP gross margin fell from 53.1% to 43.1%, adjusted cash operating expense rose from $9.352M to $93.244M (111.3% of revenue), and the adjusted EBITDA loss widened from $5.827M to $50.629M. **So far, scale has produced a larger absolute loss, not leverage.**
4. **The share count is a dilution machine that is still running.** Shares outstanding went from 206,732,666 at 2025-06-30 to 581,241,996 at 2026-09-14, **+181.2% in fifteen months**. That excludes: the 44,999,998 DZYNE deferred consideration shares that must be delivered on 2027-01-04, roughly $22.5M of additional Gate/Bron shares within nine months plus up to $185M of stock-settled earn-out, the 116,806,574 contingently issuable shares already in the Q2 anti-dilutive table, 27,866,387 options and 33,178,399 RSUs. Fully diluted, that is about 955,350,116 shares — 1.64x the current count, leaving only 244,649,884 shares of headroom under the 1,200,000,000 authorization.
5. **Goodwill is an unexploded charge.** Goodwill of $661.362M plus intangibles of $583.268M equals 41.6% of total assets, with DZYNE adding another $493.460M. Every acquisition's purchase-price allocation is preliminary, and the 10-K and 10-Q repeatedly warn the final allocation "could differ materially." If the second-half ramp disappoints, the triggers for an impairment test — market capitalization below carrying equity, or a reporting unit missing forecast — are easily met.
6. **Accounting income runs opposite to shareholder interests.** The $1,043.740M warrant liability is carried at fair value, so a falling share price produces book income — the $639.7M first-half fair-value gain is exactly the result of the price falling from the $16.45 issue level toward $8.24. **The brightest number in the financial statements is generated by the thing shareholders least want.**

**Valuation implication of the downside case:** if FY2026 misses at roughly $450M and FY2029 revenue is only $600M (**a 3.7% three-year CAGR measured from the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $537.5M**; measured instead from the $450M actual landing point it is 10.1%), with the market pricing a stalled, impaired roll-up at 1.5x EV/Sales, roughly 765M exit shares (including one dilutive financing) and about $150M of exit net cash, equity value per share is about $1.37 — 81.4% below $7.39, or **−40.2% annualized** over 3.28 years.

## 10. Conclusion <!-- report-module:conclusion -->

**(1) What this report answers.** Ondas is a rare case in this research book of a company built in fourteen months out of capital-markets instruments. The question it answers is not "is defense autonomy a good sector" (we think it is) but **at what price a roll-up that converted high-priced equity issuance and serial cash-and-stock acquisitions into revenue is worth owning**. That question generalizes to any small-cap growth company whose primary growth mechanism is M&A.

**(2) The expectations gap in one sentence.** $7.39 already prices a 2026-2029 revenue CAGR of 35.3%-55.6% (depending on a 3.5x-5.0x exit multiple), and the starting point of that compounding — the $537.5M FY2026 guidance midpoint — itself requires a fourth quarter of $251.1-261.1M, close to a tripling of the $83.772M actually delivered in Q2 within two quarters; our base case is a 30.0% CAGR at a 3.5x exit multiple.

**(3) Stance and conviction, derived from the scenario weights.** The 30/40/30 grid weights to $5.86 per share, −20.7% against the 2026-09-18 close of $7.39, or **−6.8% annualized** over 3.28 years — **14.8 to 16.8 points below the 8%-10% required return**. Lifting every exit multiple a full turn gives −0.9%; raising the whole FY2029 revenue path by 27%-38% gives +1.0%; **both generous settings remain 7 to 9 points short of the hurdle**, so the negative skew is insensitive to the assumptions. More tellingly, the bull case itself — 30% weight, an implied 39.2% three-year CAGR measured from the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $537.5M — delivers only +9.8% annualized, i.e. it merely reaches the bottom of the hurdle, while the base case is already −5.6% and the bear case −40.2%. A distribution whose upside is only a pass and whose downside loses four-fifths of capital does not support initiating a position. Hence **stance: cautious**.

**Conviction is set to medium rather than high** for three reasons: (a) the negative-skew arithmetic is robust, but the FY2029 revenue path of a roll-up is intrinsically hard to forecast, and the acquisition cadence itself is a variable we cannot model; (b) the starting point of the whole analysis — $613M of backlog — is a company-defined measure never reconciled to GAAP RPO, a lower evidentiary grade than we normally require; (c) DZYNE, which carries the second-half ramp, cannot yet produce standalone historical financials. **Setting conviction to low would be excessive:** the five quantities that determine the conclusion — revenue, gross margin, cash flow, share count and goodwill — all come straight from the 10-Q and the earnings release without derivation.

**A necessary clarification: this is a valuation and capital-structure judgment, not a business-quality judgment.** The orders are real, the customers are real, and the geopolitical demand is real; with roughly $934M of pro forma net cash and only $6.4M of interest-bearing debt, no outcome is forced within the monitoring window. What we object to is not the business but the fact that **at $7.39 investors are paying for a 35%-56% compound growth expectation on an integration story that no complete cycle has yet tested**, while carrying a dilution machine that is still running (fully diluted shares are 1.64x the current count) and $1.24B of goodwill and intangibles equal to 41.6% of total assets.

**(4) Upgrade and downgrade triggers (identical clause by clause to `stanceTriggers` in reports.json).**

- **Upgrade to neutral-watch or constructive:** Q3 2026 revenue at or above the $155M top of guidance with management maintaining the FY2026 $525-550M target; adjusted cash operating expense falling below 80% of revenue and the adjusted EBITDA loss narrowing sequentially to within $30M; Customer A's share no longer rising; and the DZYNE pro forma data first disclosed in the Q3 10-Q confirming that its standalone operating quality is commensurate with the $878.994M consideration.
- **Downgrade to bearish-avoid:** Q3 2026 revenue below the $140M low end of guidance, or management cutting the FY2026 target; or a first recognized goodwill impairment; or an equity issuance priced below $7.39 while pro forma cash is under $500M; or Customer A falling below 10% of quarterly revenue with no replacement; or twelve-month growth in shares outstanding (excluding the DZYNE deferred consideration shares) exceeding 25%.
