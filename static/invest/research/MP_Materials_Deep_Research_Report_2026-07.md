## Executive Summary

> **Framework note:** MP Materials joins the AI-infrastructure book's **resources** layer as a **common constraint**. The label names the report's *job* — test whether one input gates several layers at once — not its answer. `cameco-2026` set that precedent in this layer by carrying the same label and returning a negative reading on uranium. MP tests a different candidate: NdFeB permanent magnets. `almonty-2026` already covers the strategic-metals-and-policy chain from the tungsten side, but it is a development-stage story about a mine that has not ramped. MP is the same policy chain with the mine already producing, the government cheque already cashed, and the accounting already filed — which makes it the place where the policy mechanism itself can be audited rather than argued about.

**One-line thesis:** MP Materials is the only rare-earth mine-to-magnet platform of scale in the Western hemisphere, and since the fourth quarter of 2025 it has been, in accounting substance, a government-underwritten industrial asset rather than a commodity producer. Strip the US Department of War price-floor payment out of its own segment disclosure and the Materials segment — Mountain Pass, the mine, the whole upstream — produced **negative** adjusted EBITDA in every reported period since the floor began: **−$5.5M** in Q1 2026, **−$10.8M** in Q4 2025, **−$34.2M** for FY2025.

**Current view:** **Neutral-watch, medium conviction.** This is not a hedge; it is the mechanical consequence of a contract structure almost nobody has read. The Department of War arrangement removes MP's left tail — a $110/kg NdPr floor running to 2035, a **$140 million annual EBITDA guarantee** on the 10X magnet facility, and $1.74B of liquidity — and it *also* clips the right tail: above the floor MP eventually pays the DoW **30%** of the NdPr excess, and above the guarantee the DoW takes **the first $30 million** of 10X excess EBITDA and then **50%** of everything beyond. At US$41.66 (2026-07-30 close, 57.8% below the October 2025 high) the market caps this at about **US$7.42B**, an enterprise value of about **US$7.19B**, roughly **28.3x** trailing-twelve-month revenue, **20.7x** revenue plus price-floor income, and **141.8x** trailing adjusted EBITDA — while the same trailing window shows adjusted EBITDA of **−$42.6M** if the government payment is removed. The company has been converted into something closer to a regulated utility with an embedded option on Chinese export policy, and it is still being priced as a growth commodity equity.

**The AI-linkage finding, stated up front:** the AI exposure in MP's own filings is **embodied/physical AI and robotics, not data-center compute**. The string "data center" appears **zero** times in the FY2025 10-K and **zero** times in the Q1 2026 10-Q. "Artificial intelligence" appears twice in the 10-K: once as "physical artificial intelligence ('AI') and robotics," once as a cybersecurity risk factor. Named counterparties are General Motors (automotive), Apple (consumer devices) and the Department of War (defense). §3 works this through in full.

Four structural points matter for this book.

**First, the government payment is not a subsidy line — it is the profit.** Take the Materials segment exactly as MP reports it and subtract the PPA income that sits beside it:

| Period | Materials segment revenue | PPA income | Materials segment adjusted EBITDA | Segment adjusted EBITDA excluding PPA income |
|--------|---------------------------|------------|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Q1 2026 | $72.2M | $42.3M | $36.7M | **−$5.5M** |
| Q4 2025 | $35.6M | $51.0M | $40.3M | **−$10.8M** |
| FY2025 | $160.4M | $51.0M (Q4 only) | $16.8M | **−$34.2M** |

In Q4 2025 the floor payment was **larger than the segment's entire revenue**. At the consolidated level, FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of $11.4M becomes **−$39.6M** without it, and the trailing twelve months ended 2026-03-31 go from $50.7M to **−$42.6M**.

**Second, MP paid for the floor, and the price is disclosed.** This is the single most misunderstood item in the story. The July 2025 press release described "a $400 million equity investment by the Department of Defense," which reads as the government putting money in. The 10-K's allocation table says something different:

| DoW transaction, 2025-07-09 | Measured value | Allocated transaction costs | Recognized amount |
|-----------------------------|----------------|-----------------------------|-------------------|
| **Received** — cash | $550.0M | — | $550.0M |
| **Received** — PPA Upfront Asset (the price-floor right) | $218.6M | $2.5M | $221.1M |
| **Total consideration received** | **$768.6M** | $2.5M | $771.1M |
| **Given** — Series A convertible preferred | $418.4M | $(4.8)M | $413.6M |
| **Given** — Warrant (11,201,659 shares at $30.03) | $264.2M | $(3.0)M | $261.2M |
| **Given** — Samarium Project Loan | $86.0M | $(1.0)M | $85.0M |
| **Total consideration given** | **$768.6M** | $(8.8)M | $759.8M |

MP's own critical-accounting-estimate disclosure states it plainly: "The PPA Upfront Asset was initially measured as the excess of the relative fair value of the Series A Preferred Stock, Warrant and the Samarium Project Loan over the cash consideration received from the DoW." In plain terms, MP issued **$218.6M more securities value than the cash it received**, and that excess *is* the price it paid for the floor. The warrant alone was measured at $264.2M — a larger number than the preferred's $400M cash and one that appeared in no headline. The asset is now being amortized back through depreciation and amortization at roughly $11.2M a quarter, so the *net* pre-tax contribution of the floor in Q1 2026 was $42.3M − $11.2M = **$31.1M** — while adjusted EBITDA, which excludes D&A, counts the full $42.3M.

**Third, the growth asset is cost-plus with a capped upside.** The 10X Facility is the entire 2028+ story: 10,000 MT/year of NdFeB, roughly 3.3x the Independence Facility's committed 3,000 MT. Under the DoW Offtake Agreement the DoW "will acquire the magnets at a price equal to their production costs … plus the guaranteed EBITDA," guarantees at least **$140M of annual EBITDA** escalating at 2%, pays 25% of that quarterly subject to annual true-up, and — if MP instead sells to third parties after the Production Milestone Date — receives **the first $30M** of any excess EBITDA and then **50%** of the rest. A 10,000-tonne magnet plant whose economics are a floor of $140M and a half-share above roughly $170M is a bond with an equity kicker, not an operating-leverage story.

**Fourth, the China revenue line went to zero on purpose.** To align with the DoW agreements, MP ceased all product sales to China in July 2025. Rare earth concentrate revenue fell from $30.1M in Q1 2025 to **$0** in Q1 2026 and from $144.4M in FY2024 to $42.0M in FY2025. The headline "+49% revenue growth" in Q1 2026 is therefore understating the underlying separated-products ramp and overstating nothing — but it is measuring a company that deliberately amputated a third of its 2024 revenue base for policy reasons. Bill-and-hold arrangements accounted for $43.3M of the $90.6M recognized in Q1 2026.

## 2. Industry & Competitive Position

**The asset.** Mountain Pass, San Bernardino County, California, is the only rare-earth mining and processing site of scale in North America. MP produced a record **50,692 MT** of REO in concentrate in 2025 (+12%) and a record **2,599 MT** of NdPr oxide (+101%), and sold 1,994 MT of NdPr (+75%). The "Upstream 60K" programme targets roughly 60,000 MT of REO through better beneficiation and use of lower-grade ore. Downstream, Independence in Fort Worth is the first fully integrated US rare-earth metal, alloy and magnet plant; it began commercial-equipment NdFeB production in December 2025 and is committed under the DoW agreements to expand to 3,000 MT/year.

**The competitive position is a policy position.** MP's moat is not cost or grade in the way Cameco's is; it is that the alternative suppliers are Chinese and the buyer of last resort is the US government. That cuts both ways, and 2026 has demonstrated both directions inside six months:

| Development | Date | What it does to the position |
|-------------|------|------------------------------|
| DoW public-private partnership executed | 2025-07-09 | Converts MP from a price-taker into a floored, capped, state-underwritten platform |
| MP ceases all product sales to China | 2025-07 | Removes concentrate revenue; ends the Shenghe offtake channel |
| Apple definitive long-term supply agreement | 2025-07 | First blue-chip commercial magnet customer; $200M of milestone-based prepayments; shipments expected 2027 |
| China suspends the expanded export controls after a US-China trade deal | 2025-11 | Reduces the scarcity premium that had been MP's tailwind |
| $200M incentive package for 10X, anchored by the Texas Semiconductor Innovation Fund | announced with FY2025 results | Third-party validation of the 10X capital plan |
| NdPr offtake with an unnamed "leading U.S. technology and industrial company" | 2026-02 | A commercial counterparty MP has not identified; a real monitoring item |
| 10X site selected (120 acres, Northlake, TX); land closed April 2026 for ~$80M | 2026-02 / 2026-04 | The 2028 story becomes a construction project with a balance sheet |
| **China's Ministry of Commerce adds MP Materials to its dual-use export control list** | **2026-06-22** | Beijing prohibits exports of dual-use items from China *to* MP, with immediate effect — a direct threat to equipment, tooling and heavy-rare-earth feedstock for the buildout |

That last item is the one to watch and it is the least quantified. MP had already stopped selling *to* China; the June 2026 listing restricts what China will sell *to MP*. Magnet-making equipment, sintering furnaces and heavy-rare-earth feedstock are areas where Chinese supply is dominant, and MP's own 10-K notes that the DoW has agreed to assist in procuring HREE feedstock for the 10X. This is press-reported (Reuters, Bloomberg, Benchmark Mineral Intelligence, 2026-06-22); MP has filed no 8-K on it and has not quantified an impact.

**Price environment.** The PPA makes MP's NdPr economics approximately **max($110/kg, market)** on designated volumes — which is why the 2026 rare-earth price rally, real as it is, changes the *composition* of MP's NdPr income far more than its level. On this report's derivation (§9), the Q1 2026 benchmark price implied by MP's own disclosure was roughly **$68/kg**, against a $70.71/kg realized price and $42.02/kg of floor income, for combined proceeds of about $112.73/kg. Third-party coverage puts the NdPr benchmark materially higher by mid-2026 and at times above the $110 floor. If that holds through Q2, PPA income should fall sharply and product revenue should rise to replace it — which is exactly the test that makes the 2026-08-06 print worth watching.

## 3. The AI-Linkage Test: Do Rare-Earth Magnets Gate the AI Buildout?

This is the section that justifies MP's place in this book, and — as with `cameco-2026` — the chain role names the *job*, not the answer.

**The claim under test.** MP is routinely discussed as an AI-infrastructure name. The book's power layer (`constellation-energy-2026`, `vistra-2026`, `nrg-2026`, `gevernova-2026`), its facility layer (`vertiv-2026`, `eqix-2026`, `digital-realty-2026`) and its compute layer all assume large volumes of motors, pumps, fans, generators and actuators. If NdFeB magnets were a binding input to that buildout, a Western magnet monopolist would be a genuine cross-layer constraint and every one of those reports would need a fuel-style check it currently lacks.

**The evidence, stated against the claim:**

| Observable | What MP's own disclosure says | What it does and does not prove |
|------------|-------------------------------|---------------------------------|
| Literal term search, FY2025 10-K | "data center": **0 occurrences**. "artificial intelligence": **2** — one is "physical artificial intelligence ('AI') and robotics," one is a cybersecurity risk factor | The company does not describe itself as selling into data centers. It does not prove no magnet reaches one |
| Literal term search, Q1 2026 10-Q | "data center": **0 occurrences**. "artificial intelligence": **0** | Unchanged one quarter later, in the most recent filing |
| The demand-driver list MP actually publishes | "electric mobility; **physical AI**; industrial, consumer and professional service robotics; renewable power generation; energy-efficient motors, pumps, and compressors; consumer and medical applications; critical defense systems; and catalysts and phosphors" | MP *does* name AI — as embodied AI and robotics, alongside humanoid and service robots. That is a different capex cycle from the one this book tracks |
| The Item 1 end-market taxonomy | Electric Mobility; Robotics; Renewable Power Generation; **Energy-Efficient Motors, Pumps and Compressors (HVAC, elevators, escalators, appliances)**; **Consumer and Medical (smartphones, tablets, laptops, hard disk drives, speakers)**; Critical Defense Systems; Catalysts and Phosphors | The two adjacencies a data-center bull would reach for — HVAC and hard disk drives — appear as generic categories with no sizing, no named customer and no revenue attribution |
| Independence Facility's stated end markets | "electric vehicles, robotics, **semiconductor manufacturing**, clean energy, electronics and defense technologies" | The one explicit semiconductor reference in the file, unsized and unattributed |
| Named counterparties | **GM** (automotive traction), **Apple** (consumer devices), **DoW** (defense), plus one unnamed "leading U.S. technology and industrial company" | Three of four named demand relationships are automotive, consumer and defense |
| Where the 10X output is contractually going | 100% to the DoW under the offtake agreement, unless syndicated with DoW consent | The single largest planned magnet capacity in the West is contracted to a defense buyer, not to an AI buildout |
| The revenue that exists today | Q1 2026 magnetics revenue of $21.1M, entirely magnetic precursor (NdPr metal) under the **GM** agreement | Actual magnet-chain revenue is automotive |

**The answer, stated plainly.** On MP's own 2026 disclosure, **rare-earth magnets are not a demonstrated constraint on the AI *data-center* buildout, and MP is not a data-center supplier.** The AI exposure that MP itself claims is real but different: **physical AI, robotics and humanoids** — an embodied-AI capex cycle whose timing, buyers and unit economics are not the ones this book's power, facility and compute layers are modelling. The demand that is contracted and revenue-generating today is automotive (GM), consumer (Apple, from 2027) and defense (DoW).

Two honest qualifications, because the negative finding should be no stronger than the evidence:

1. **Absence of a keyword is not absence of a molecule.** Data centers plainly consume NdFeB in CRAH/CRAC fan motors, liquid-cooling pumps, and the voice-coil and spindle motors inside nearline hard disk drives. MP lists HVAC and hard disk drives in its taxonomy. What is missing is any evidence — sizing, customer, contract, revenue line — that this is a material or gating channel, and no filing makes the connection.
2. **The constraint could be real without MP being the read.** If NdFeB *were* to bind the AI buildout, the binding step would be finished-magnet capacity, which today is overwhelmingly Chinese. MP's committed Western capacity is 3,000 MT at Independence plus 10,000 MT at 10X in 2028 — and the 10X tonnes are already sold to the DoW. Even in a world where magnets gate AI infrastructure, MP's incremental tonnes are contractually unavailable to it.

**What this changes in the other reports.** Three read-throughs:

1. **No power- or facility-layer report needs to add a rare-earth constraint check, and the silence is symmetric — measured, not assumed.** `cameco-2026` earned its equivalent claim by checking that `constellation-energy-2026` contains no reference to uranium anywhere in its body, so this report ran the reciprocal check rather than asserting it. Searching all seven power- and facility-layer report bodies in **both** languages — `constellation-energy-2026`, `vistra-2026`, `nrg-2026`, `gevernova-2026`, `vertiv-2026`, `eqix-2026`, `digital-realty-2026` — for "rare earth", "NdFeB" and "magnet", plus their three Chinese equivalents (rare earth, NdFeB, permanent magnet), returns **zero occurrences in all fourteen files**. Neither side of the chain thinks the other is a constraint on it, and now both directions have been checked instead of assumed. There is nothing to grade.
2. **`almonty-2026` and MP are the same policy trade at different stages, and MP is the better instrument for auditing it.** Almonty's tungsten thesis rests on Sangdong ramping; MP's rests on a contract already signed, already accounted for, and already visible in segment EBITDA. Anyone underwriting "Western critical-minerals reshoring" should read MP's Note 3 first, because it prices what the policy support actually costs the equity holder.
3. **The AI label on strategic-metals names deserves a standing discount in this book.** MP is the largest and best-documented rare-earth name in the market and its own filings mention data centers zero times across two consecutive filings. That is a reasonable prior to carry into any future rare-earth, lithium or specialty-materials initiation.

## 5. Management & Governance

James Litinsky's MP has executed one idea with unusual consistency since the 2020 SPAC: buy the only Western rare-earth asset, integrate forward one process step at a time, and let the geopolitics come to you. Judged on that plan, the record is strong. Mountain Pass went from concentrate-only to 2,599 MT of NdPr oxide in 2025. Independence went from construction in 2022 to electrowinning in late 2024, strip casting in 2025, and commercial-equipment NdFeB magnets in December 2025. Very few US industrial startups of this scale have hit that sequence.

The DoW transaction is the decision that will define the company, and it deserves to be assessed on its terms rather than its headline. What management bought was certainty: a floor under the commodity that had twice nearly killed the business, a guaranteed-EBITDA offtake for a plant that does not exist yet, and a $150M twelve-year loan. What it sold was the upside and a slice of the equity: 30% of NdPr proceeds above $110/kg once 10X is at full capacity, the first $30M plus half of all excess 10X EBITDA, 24.5 million shares' worth of conversion and warrant claims struck at $30.03, and — per the allocation table — $218.6M of securities value handed over above the cash received. For a company that had never earned a full-year profit and was staring at Chinese price-setting, that is a defensible trade. It is not the free money the July 2025 headlines implied, and an investor underwriting MP today is underwriting a capped-return industrial contract.

Capital-markets execution has been opportunistic and, so far, well-timed. The July 2025 equity offering sold 13,590,908 shares at **$55.00** to the public for **$724.2M** net — above the anchor price today, which is to management's credit and to the current shareholder's cost. The $1B JPMorgan/Goldman commitment letter was allowed to expire undrawn on 2025-08-26 because the equity raise and a new revolver reduced it dollar-for-dollar; MP chose equity over committed construction debt at a price it will not see again soon.

## 6. Bull Case

The bull case is that MP owns an irreplaceable asset in a category the US government has decided it cannot do without, that the floor has already removed the failure mode, and that the market is pricing a de-rated commodity equity rather than a contracted industrial platform with an option on Chinese policy.

1. **The downside is contractually removed, which is rare in mining.** A $110/kg NdPr floor running to 2035, a $140M annual EBITDA guarantee on 10X escalating at 2%, $1.74B of liquidity, and a 12-year unsecured government loan. Almost no resource equity has this shape. In a bear commodity tape MP still collects.
2. **MP keeps 100% of the NdPr upside until 10X reaches full capacity.** The 30% excess-sharing clause is explicitly conditioned on the 10X Facility reaching full production capacity — targeted 2028 and realistically later. Every quarter the benchmark spends above $110/kg between now and then accrues entirely to MP. Third-party coverage indicates NdPr traded above the floor at points in mid-2026.
3. **China has already shown it will weaponize the supply chain, twice.** The 2025 export-control expansion, the November 2025 suspension, and the June 2026 dual-use listing of MP itself are three data points in twelve months. Every escalation raises the strategic value of the only non-Chinese integrated platform, and the customers who most need insurance — Apple, GM, the DoW, one unnamed technology and industrial company — have already signed.
4. **Magnetics is already profitable and the volume ramp has not started.** The Magnetics segment produced $26.4M of adjusted EBITDA on $66.9M of FY2025 revenue and $9.6M on $21.1M in Q1 2026 — on *precursor* sales alone, before a single finished magnet was sold. Independence is committed to 3,000 MT and 10X to 10,000 MT.
5. **Embodied AI is a genuine, large and early demand vector.** MP names physical AI, humanoid and service robotics in its own demand list. If that market develops on anything like the trajectory its proponents claim, magnet demand per unit is high, the supply base is Chinese, and MP is the Western default.

Upside frame: with Mountain Pass at Upstream 60K throughput, Independence at 3,000 MT and 10X at 10,000 MT, a mature platform plausibly generates $450-550M of EBITDA around 2030 — roughly $250-300M from floored NdPr, $60-80M from Independence magnets, and $140-170M from the 10X guarantee and its shared excess. Against today's $7.19B enterprise value that is 13-16x on a business four years out; a sustained benchmark price well above $110/kg, or capacity beyond what is currently committed, is what turns that into a genuinely cheap entry.

## 7. Bear Case

The bear case is not that MP is a bad company or a bad asset. It is that at US$41.66 you are paying 141.8x trailing adjusted EBITDA for a business whose trailing adjusted EBITDA is **negative** without a government cheque, whose largest growth asset is contractually cost-plus, and whose upside is shared with the counterparty that provides the floor.

1. **There is no earnings support of any kind.** TTM net loss $(71.2)M, TTM adjusted net loss $(14.3)M, no dividend, no buyback. FY2025 free cash flow was −$303.9M and 2026 capex is guided at $500-600M. Every multiple in §9 is a revenue or EBITDA multiple because there is no E.
2. **Strip the floor and the mine loses money.** Materials segment adjusted EBITDA excluding PPA income has been negative in all three reported periods since the floor began. That is a statement about market-price cost coverage at Mountain Pass in 2025-26, and it means the equity's current profitability is a policy transfer, not an operating result.
3. **The upside is contractually shared, and the sharing is severe on the growth asset.** Above the $140M threshold, the DoW takes the first $30M of 10X excess EBITDA and then half of everything above that. Above $110/kg, MP eventually pays 30% of NdPr excess. The 10X plant — the entire capacity story — cannot generate outsized returns by design.
4. **The floor's political durability is disclosed by MP as a risk, not asserted as a certainty.** The 10-K cites "the unconventional use of DPA Title III authority," "the need for the DoW to secure additional funds in the future in order to meet its obligations," and states there "can be no assurances that the authorization of and continued support for the DoW Transactions will not be modified, challenged or impaired." A ten-year commitment funded by future appropriations is not the same instrument as a ten-year commercial contract.
5. **The funding path to 2028 is not closed.** $1.74B of liquidity against $500-600M/year of capex before the 10X build peaks, with FY2025 operating cash flow of −$155.8M and the $1B bank commitment lapsed undrawn. MP raised equity at $55.00 in July 2025; raising it again near $41.66 costs materially more, and the DoW's 24.5 million conversion and warrant shares at $30.03 sit in the money above that.
6. **China escalated directly against MP six weeks ago.** The 2026-06-22 dual-use export ban restricts what China may sell *to* MP at precisely the moment MP is building two magnet plants. Sintering equipment, tooling and heavy-rare-earth feedstock are areas of Chinese dominance, and no impact has been quantified by the company.
7. **The AI label is doing work the filings do not support.** If part of the multiple reflects an assumed data-center demand channel, §3 shows there is nothing in either the 10-K or the 10-Q to support it.

Downside frame: if the 10X slips to 2029-2030, the benchmark price settles back toward the floor so that PPA income persists at Q1-2026-like levels, and equity is raised to close the capex gap, a mature 2030 EBITDA nearer $350M on a diluted share base of 215-230M at 10-12x EV/EBITDA implies roughly $3.5-4.2B of enterprise value, or about **US$21-26 per share** — 37-50% below the anchor and below the pre-DoW-announcement trading range.

**The Q1 2026 NdPr unit economics, derived rather than disclosed.** MP does not publish a realized NdPr price per kilogram, so this report derives one and labels every step. Materials-segment NdPr oxide and metal revenue of $71.136M over 1,006 MT of NdPr sales volume gives a realized **$70.71/kg**. PPA income of $42.273M over the same 1,006 MT gives **$42.02/kg**, for combined proceeds of **$112.73/kg** against the $110/kg floor — and an implied Benchmark Quarterly Average Volume Weighted Price of roughly **$68/kg**.

That derivation carries one real ambiguity and one internal check. The ambiguity: the PPA pays on *designated* volume, which MP may elect as stockpile, affiliate sales or third-party sales, and MP disclosed only that "a substantial majority" of Q1 PPA income pertained to third-party sales — designated volume is therefore not established as equal to sales volume, and if it were larger the implied benchmark would be higher. The check: combined proceeds of $112.73/kg sit only 2.5% above the $110 floor, and NdPr metal carries a premium to oxide, which is what you would expect if designated volume were approximately equal to sold volume. Third-party price coverage placing the Q1 2026 China NdPr oxide benchmark in the $60-75/kg range early in the quarter is directionally consistent. No conclusion in this report depends on the exact figure; what matters is the structure — **MP's NdPr proceeds are approximately max($110/kg, benchmark)**, which makes this a volume story with a floored price rather than a commodity-price story.

**What's priced in & the expectation gap:** at US$41.66 the market has already marked MP down **57.8%** from its October 2025 high of US$98.65 and about 24% year to date, and the anchor itself is a 9.3% bounce off a low set the previous session. What today's ~$7.19B enterprise value underwrites is roughly this: that Mountain Pass reaches Upstream 60K throughput, that Independence delivers its committed 3,000 MT, that the 10X commissions on time in 2028 and earns at least its $140M guarantee, and that the DoW package survives a decade of appropriations — i.e. an EBITDA level around $450-550M reached in about 2030, capitalized today at 13-16x. That is a fair price for flawless execution and no more. What is *not* priced in either direction is the one variable with genuine asymmetry: how long the benchmark NdPr price stays above $110/kg *before* the 10X reaches full capacity, because that is the only window in which MP keeps the entire commodity upside. Everything else about this equity has been contractually flattened — the floor removes the left tail, the 30% share and the $30M-then-50% split remove the right.

A symmetric 30/40/30 grid is therefore the finding rather than a hedge, and for a different reason than in `cameco-2026`. There the symmetry came from timing — bullish evidence deferred, bearish evidence already printed. Here it comes from **structure**: the counterparty that guarantees the downside also owns a large share of the upside. A company whose distribution has been compressed on both sides by the same contract, trading at a price that already discounts more than half its peak value, is the definition of neutral-watch.

## 11. Conclusion

MP Materials enters this book as the resources layer's second common-constraint report, and like the first it earns its place with a negative finding backed by primary evidence. The candidate constraint here is NdFeB permanent magnets. On MP's own FY2025 10-K and Q1 2026 10-Q, the phrase "data center" appears zero times, "artificial intelligence" appears twice in the 10-K — once as "physical artificial intelligence and robotics" and once as a cybersecurity risk — and every named demand relationship is automotive, consumer or defense. The AI that MP sells into is embodied AI: motors, actuators, brakes and sensors for industrial, service and humanoid robots. That is a real market and possibly a large one, but it is not the data-center capex cycle this book's power, facility and compute layers are modelling, and no report in those layers needs to add a rare-earth check on this evidence.

At the chain level the read-through to `almonty-2026` is the more useful one. Both are Western critical-minerals reshoring trades underwritten by policy; MP is simply further along, which makes it the place where the mechanism can be audited rather than argued. What MP's Note 3 shows is that the policy support is a *trade*, not a gift: MP received $550.0M of cash and a price-floor right measured at $218.6M, and handed over securities measured at $768.6M. Anyone underwriting the reshoring theme anywhere in this book should read that table before assuming government backing is free.

At the company level MP is the genuine article — the only integrated rare-earth platform of scale outside China, a record production year, a profitable magnetics segment before finished magnets have shipped, a $1.74B balance sheet, and blue-chip customers who have prepaid. Its disclosure of the DoW transaction is unusually honest and works against its own press framing, which is creditable and is why this report's conviction is medium rather than low.

At the stock level the discipline point is simple, and it is structural rather than cyclical. The Department of War arrangement converted MP from a commodity equity into something closer to a regulated industrial contract: a $110/kg floor to 2035 and a $140M annual EBITDA guarantee on one side, and 30% of NdPr upside plus the first $30M and then half of all 10X excess EBITDA on the other. Remove the floor payment and the mine has produced negative adjusted EBITDA in every period since the floor began. Pay $7.19B of enterprise value — 28.3x trailing revenue, 20.7x revenue plus floor income, 141.8x trailing adjusted EBITDA — and you are paying today for a 2030 EBITDA of roughly $450-550M whose upper half belongs partly to the counterparty. That may well work; it is not a margin of safety.

The initiation stance is **neutral-watch, medium conviction**, from a symmetric 30% bull / 40% base / 30% bear grid. Medium, not high, because the *mechanism* evidence is primary and exact — the allocation table, the offtake terms, the segment EBITDA arithmetic — while the *magnitude* evidence is weak: MP publishes no volume, revenue or EBITDA guidance and no realized NdPr price, so the mature-EBITDA range in §9 rests on this report's own unit assumptions. Neutral-watch, not cautious, because a ten-year price floor, a $140M EBITDA guarantee and $1.74B of liquidity genuinely remove the downside tail a "cautious" rating should imply; and not constructive, because the same contract that removes the tail also takes a large share of everything above it.

Upgrade trigger: Q2 2026 shows product revenue replacing PPA income roughly one-for-one at a benchmark above $110/kg, first finished-magnet revenue lands in H2 2026, and the 10X stays on its 2028 path inside the $500-600M capex envelope without dilutive equity — upgrade to constructive. Downgrade trigger: the DoW package is modified, challenged or its appropriation lapses; PPA receivables stop converting to cash; the 10X slips past 2029 or its capex is materially raised; equity is issued below the anchor price; or China's export-control listing is disclosed to have delayed the buildout — downgrade to cautious.
