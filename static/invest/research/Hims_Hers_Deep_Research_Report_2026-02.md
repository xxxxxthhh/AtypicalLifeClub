> **2026-08-02 litigation update.** On **2026-07-29**, the U.S. Federal Trade Commission, joined by Utah and Los Angeles County, filed a federal complaint against Hims & Hers in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California. The complaint alleges unlawful sharing of sensitive health information with advertising platforms, misleading privacy representations, deceptive subscription billing and an obstructive cancellation flow; it seeks injunctive and monetary relief and civil penalties. Hims & Hers responded the same day, denied the allegations, said the complaint disregards evidence supplied during a nearly three-year investigation, and said it would defend vigorously. Hims & Hers Health*) was also filed. Primary sources: FTC, [press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/ftc-states-act-against-hims-hers-deceptive-unlawful-privacy-practices) and [case page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/hims-hers) (2026-07-29); Hims & Hers, [company response](https://investors.hims.com/news/news-details/2026/Hims--Hers-Responds-to-FTC-Lawsuit/default.aspx) (2026-07-29).

## 1. Business Overview

Founded in 2017 and public via SPAC in 2021, Hims & Hers operates a direct-to-consumer telehealth platform spanning online consultation, prescription fulfillment, and medication delivery, with owned-brand treatments and its own pharmacy network (vertical integration from consult to delivery).

**Core business lines:**

- **Men's health (Hims):** hair loss (finasteride/minoxidil), ED (sildenafil/tadalafil), skincare.
- **Women's health (Hers):** birth control, skincare, hair loss, menopause.
- **Mental health:** online therapy, antidepressant prescriptions.
- **Weight management:** compounded GLP-1 (semaglutide) — the epicenter of controversy. From March 2026 the U.S. business moved to branded GLP-1s (Novo Nordisk's FDA-approved drugs available from 2026-03-26), with compounded GLP-1s offered only on a limited scale (10-Q); the company has not disclosed the resulting weight-management share of revenue. *2026-11-10 addition (company newsroom, 2026-11-06):* Hims & Hers and Eli Lilly announced an agreement under which patients prescribed on the platform can obtain Zepbound (self-pay from $299 a month) and Foundayo (from $149 a month) at Lilly's self-pay prices; Hims said it has never offered and has committed not to offer compounded copies of Lilly's GLP-1s. The release discloses no financial terms and triggers no monitoring item. The Q3 10-Q says a majority of Q3 U.S. revenue came from non-GLP-1 offerings.
- **Emerging:** testosterone, biomarker testing (Labs), longevity specialty (2026 launch).
- **International:** the June 2026 acquisition of Australia's Eucalyptus (accounting purchase price $968.5M, with operations in Australia, the UK, Germany, Ireland, Canada and Japan) took Q2 Rest of World revenue to $131.4M; the Hims brand launched in Australia on 2026-08-31.

**Business model — subscription-first:** users complete online consultations, receive prescriptions, and subscribe to monthly/quarterly delivery. This yields highly predictable recurring revenue, strong retention (>85%), and steadily rising ARPU ($50 → $64 → $80/month across 2023-2025; $83 for full-year 2025 on the company's measure, $92 in Q2 2026 and $105 in Q3 2026).

## 2. Industry & Competition

**Market size:** the U.S. telehealth market was ~$123.3B in 2024 and is projected to reach ~$455.3B by 2030 (CAGR ~24.68%), driven by demand for convenient, destigmatized care. Telehealth demand remains structurally higher than pre-pandemic, but low-quality platforms have been exposed.

**Competitive landscape:**

| Competitor | Positioning | Threat |
|------------|-------------|--------|
| Ro (Roman) | Full-category DTC telehealth | High — direct |
| LifeMD | DTC telehealth platform | Medium-High |
| Nurx | Women's health focused | Medium |
| Keeps | Men's hair loss | Medium-Low |
| Teladoc | Enterprise telehealth, expanding DTC | Medium — indirect |
| Traditional / Amazon Clinic | Clinics, pharmacies, pharma direct | Low-but-persistent |

**Moat: moderate.** Strengths: strong brand (esp. younger men), vertical integration lowering cost, subscriber scale (the 2.5M level cited here is the historical February baseline; 2.891M as of Q2 2026 and 2.788M as of Q3 2026, the first sequential decline in this coverage), and personalized plans raising switching costs. Weaknesses: core products are generic and undifferentiated, low technology barriers, limited loyalty in price-sensitive categories, and a GLP-1 business that relies on regulatory gray areas. Industry competition is now about compliant medical delivery, not just user growth. After the March 2026 move to branded GLP-1s, weight management shifted from a regulatory gray area to a pharma-partnered channel model, and gross margin came under pressure (64% in Q2 2026, 57% in Q3 2026).

## 5. Bull Case / Upside

**Core thesis:** the core business (ex-GLP-1) is robust, and the brand plus subscription model is a sustainable engine; the stock overreacted to GLP-1 risk at the February trough.

**Supporting evidence:**

1. **Non-GLP-1 is ~70% of revenue:** even if GLP-1 went to zero, ~$1.6B of annual revenue remains in hair loss, ED, skincare, and mental health — categories with minimal regulatory risk (February-baseline caliber; the company has not updated that ratio since).
2. **ARPU expansion:** $50 → $64 → $80/month, reflecting personalization and cross-sell; $92 in Q2 2026 (+21%) and $105 in Q3 2026 (+30%). Since subscribers fell in Q3, this leg now carries more of the growth: price and mix rather than volume.
3. **Subscriber base and >85% retention:** highly predictable revenue and stickiness. *Historical judgment (2026-08-12, on Q2 2026 actuals): 2.891M subscribers (+19% YoY) at $92 of monthly revenue per average subscriber (+21%) meant this leg had strengthened.* **Current judgment (2026-11-11, on Q3 2026 actuals): the leg has weakened.** Subscribers fell to **2.788M**, down 103K (−3.56%) from Q2 and only +13% YoY, while revenue per subscriber rose to $105; the bull case now needs subscribers to grow again from 2.788M in Q4 2026 (the upgrade trigger in section 10). 6. **The Novo suit is gone:** Novo dismissed without prejudice on 2026-03-09 and now partners with the company on branded drugs, removing February's most acute binary risk (though Novo reserved the right to refile).

**Key assumptions:** GLP-1 risk does not spill into other lines; international contributes within 12-18 months; brand keeps acquisition efficient amid competition. **Upside scenario (re-quantified 2026-11-11, see section 8):** FY2027 revenue +24% and FY2028 +20% to about $4.69B, a 15% FY2028 Adjusted EBITDA margin and a 20x exit give about $52.55 per share, +60.4% a year from $19.13. *(The 2026-10-02 version — +27% / +22% to about $5.04B, 16%, $62.14 and +39.7% a year at $29.30 — is historical.)*

## 6. Bear Case / Downside Risk

## 10. Conclusion & Review

Hims is a well-executed DTC telehealth company with **2.788M subscribers as of Q3 2026** (down from 2.891M in Q2; the 2.5M level cited earlier is the historical February baseline) and Q3 revenue growth of 49%, helped by Eucalyptus and the branded-GLP-1 assortment. But the quality of that growth deteriorated in Q3: GAAP gross margin fell to 57% from 64% in Q2, subscribers declined sequentially, and FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance was cut to $257-277M. The regulatory pressure has not gone away either: the DOJ referral is still open and the FTC privacy-and-billing case is in litigation, with about $60M accrued and a motion-to-dismiss hearing set for 2027-01-14.

**Core tension:** excellent core business vs self-inflicted regulatory trouble, now joined by a margin reset from the branded-GLP-1 shift. This rerun reframes the stock from an acute GLP-1 binary into a growth-quality audit. This is a monitoring view, not allocation advice: the thesis is highly binary and event-driven; any position should sit within a risk budget appropriate for a high-volatility, event-driven asset rather than a fixed weight. The current **$4,484.3M** market cap (2026-11-10 close of $19.13; the $5,377.4M pre-release reference and the $6,836.1M, $7.12B, $7.41B, $7.77B and $6.29B figures elsewhere are old-caliber levels) depends on whether revenue is durable, compliant, and convertible into cash flow.

**What Q2 2026 changed (recorded 2026-08-12, historical):** durability and compliance were still open, but *convertible into cash flow* moved the wrong way — a 64% gross margin, an $86.3M quarterly net loss and negative quarterly free cash flow, set against re-accelerated growth and raised FY2026 guidance. Recomputed on the 2026-08-11 close at the time, the stance stayed neutral / watchlist at medium confidence.

**What Q3 2026 changed (recorded 2026-11-10, re-derived at the 2026-11-10 close on 2026-11-11):** the two downgrade legs that tested growth quality — quarterly GAAP gross margin below 62% and a sequential subscriber decline — both fired, while the revenue, Adjusted EBITDA, cash and legal legs did not; the revolver-draw leg is unclear because the 10-Q discloses borrowings at some point in the nine months without dates, which does not change the stance outcome. The $267M midpoint of FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance now sits below the old bear case's $275M, with the $277M top of the range only slightly above it. Q3 free cash flow was positive at $26.0M, but operating cash flow included $433.3M of receivables sold under the new JPMorgan receivables purchase agreement, against a $323.9M nine-month build in manufacturer discount and rebate receivables.
