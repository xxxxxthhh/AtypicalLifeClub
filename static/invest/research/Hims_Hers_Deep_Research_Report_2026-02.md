## 1. Business Overview

Founded in 2017 and public via SPAC in 2021, Hims & Hers operates a direct-to-consumer telehealth platform spanning online consultation, prescription fulfillment, and medication delivery, with owned-brand treatments and its own pharmacy network (vertical integration from consult to delivery).

**Core business lines:**

- **Men's health (Hims):** hair loss (finasteride/minoxidil), ED (sildenafil/tadalafil), skincare.
- **Women's health (Hers):** birth control, skincare, hair loss, menopause.
- **Mental health:** online therapy, antidepressant prescriptions.
- **Weight management:** compounded GLP-1 (semaglutide) — the epicenter of controversy. From March 2026 the U.S. business moved to branded GLP-1s (Novo Nordisk's FDA-approved drugs available from 2026-03-26), with compounded GLP-1s offered only on a limited scale (10-Q); the company has not disclosed the resulting weight-management share of revenue.
- **Emerging:** testosterone, biomarker testing (Labs), longevity specialty (2026 launch).
- **International:** the June 2026 acquisition of Australia's Eucalyptus (accounting purchase price $968.5M, with operations in Australia, the UK, Germany, Ireland, Canada and Japan) took Q2 Rest of World revenue to $131.4M; the Hims brand launched in Australia on 2026-08-31.

**Business model — subscription-first:** users complete online consultations, receive prescriptions, and subscribe to monthly/quarterly delivery. This yields highly predictable recurring revenue, strong retention (>85%), and steadily rising ARPU ($50 → $64 → $80/month across 2023-2025; $83 for full-year 2025 on the company's measure and $92 in Q2 2026).

## 2. Industry & Competition

**Market size:** the U.S. telehealth market was ~$123.3B in 2024 and is projected to reach ~$455.3B by 2030 (CAGR ~24.68%), driven by demand for convenient, destigmatized care. Telehealth demand remains structurally higher than pre-pandemic, but low-quality platforms have been exposed.

**Competitive landscape:**

| Competitor | Positioning | Threat |
|------------|-------------|--------|
| Ro (Roman) | Full-category DTC telehealth | High — direct |
| LifeMD | DTC telehealth platform | Medium-High |
| Nurx | Women's health focused | Medium |
| Keeps | Men's hair loss | Medium-Low |
| Teladoc | Enterprise telehealth, expanding DTC | Medium — indirect |
| Traditional / Amazon Clinic | Clinics, pharmacies, pharma direct | Low-but-persistent |

**Moat: moderate.** Strengths: strong brand (esp. younger men), vertical integration lowering cost, subscriber scale (the 2.5M level cited here is the historical February baseline; 2.891M as of Q2 2026), and personalized plans raising switching costs. Weaknesses: core products are generic and undifferentiated, low technology barriers, limited loyalty in price-sensitive categories, and a GLP-1 business that relies on regulatory gray areas. Industry competition is now about compliant medical delivery, not just user growth. After the March 2026 move to branded GLP-1s, weight management shifted from a regulatory gray area to a pharma-partnered channel model, and gross margin came under pressure (64% in Q2 2026).

## 4. Management & Governance

**CEO Andrew Dudum** (founder, leading since 2017) expanded Hims from single-category men's health to a multi-category platform and took an aggressive public stance on the GLP-1 controversy — confronting the FDA and Novo Nordisk and calling the Novo lawsuit a "blatant attack by Big Pharma on consumer choice." ## 5. Bull Case / Upside

**Core thesis:** the core business (ex-GLP-1) is robust, and the brand plus subscription model is a sustainable engine; the stock overreacted to GLP-1 risk at the February trough.

**Supporting evidence:**

1. **Non-GLP-1 is ~70% of revenue:** even if GLP-1 went to zero, ~$1.6B of annual revenue remains in hair loss, ED, skincare, and mental health — categories with minimal regulatory risk (February-baseline caliber; the company has not updated that ratio since).
2. **ARPU expansion:** $50 → $64 → $80/month, reflecting personalization and cross-sell; $92 in Q2 2026 (+21%).
3. **Subscriber base and >85% retention:** highly predictable revenue and stickiness. Updated on Q2 2026 actuals, subscribers are **2.891M** (+19% YoY) at **$92** monthly revenue per average subscriber (+21%), so this leg strengthened rather than weakened; the 2.5M figure elsewhere in this report is the historical February baseline.
4. **International optionality:** ZAVA gives a European footprint, and Eucalyptus adds Australia, Japan and deeper UK, German and Canadian positions; Rest of World revenue reached $131.4M in Q2 2026.
5. **New categories:** testosterone, Labs, and longevity could become the next engines.
6. **The Novo suit is gone:** Novo dismissed without prejudice on 2026-03-09 and now partners with the company on branded drugs, removing February's most acute binary risk (though Novo reserved the right to refile).

**Key assumptions:** GLP-1 risk does not spill into other lines; international contributes within 12-18 months; brand keeps acquisition efficient amid competition. **Upside scenario (quantified on 2026-10-02, see section 8):** FY2027 revenue +27% (with a full year of Eucalyptus) and FY2028 +22% to about $5.04B, a 16% FY2028 Adjusted EBITDA margin and a 20x exit give about $62.14 per share, +39.7% a year.

## 6. Bear Case / Downside Risk

**Core thesis:** the GLP-1 storm is unresolved and could cascade, while the core advantage (generic drugs + convenient delivery) lacks a true moat.

**Supporting evidence:**

1. **Novo Nordisk patent lawsuit (filed Feb 9, 2026; dismissed 2026-03-09):** Delaware federal court, which had sought a permanent injunction and damages. Novo voluntarily dismissed without prejudice and the court closed the case, but Novo reserved the right to refile; as a bear point it has moved from an immediate-injunction risk to a refiling tail risk.
2. **DOJ referral (Feb 6, 2026):** HHS referred Hims to the DOJ for potential FD&C Act violations — a threat to overall compliance reputation, not just GLP-1. No further action was disclosed as of the Q2 10-Q.
3. **Gross-margin deterioration — confirmed and worse:** the old 80% → 74% path continued to **64% in Q2 2026** (from 76% a year earlier), on high-cost injectables, the branded-GLP-1 channel structure, personalization complexity and the newly consolidated international mix. This is now the single most quantified bear point.
4. **Growth deceleration — partly refuted:** the historical point was Q3 2025 sequential revenue growth of only ~2.2% with subscriber growth flattening. Q2 2026 revenue instead grew +38% YoY with subscribers +19%, and guidance was raised; the deceleration concern has shifted from the top line to margins and cash. Note that Q2 U.S. revenue grew +16%, with the rest of the growth coming from the acquisition.
5. **Convertible dilution — enlarged:** sustained stock weakness could force large dilution on conversion, and principal is now **$1,402.5M** as of 2026-06-30 ($1,365.3M carrying), not the old $1.1B — roughly $393M more convertible debt against $324.1M of book equity. The 2032 notes' $29.53 conversion price is almost exactly today's share price, though the capped calls offset dilution up to $50.15.
6. **Competition:** Ro, LifeMD, and others keep closing the gap as traditional systems digitize.
7. **FTC/state privacy and billing complaint (filed 2026-07-29):** allegations concerning health-data sharing, privacy representations, subscription billing and cancellation add a compliance and brand-trust risk beyond GLP-1. The company denies the allegations and is defending the case, and has accrued about $60M; there are also a privacy class action on the same facts and a securities class action (the latter per law-firm notices, a third-party source). The Visa Acquirer Monitoring Program reported by Bloomberg (third-party) points at the same subscription-billing problem.
8. **Acquisition consideration and liquidity:** about $703.0M of Eucalyptus deferred consideration plus contingent consideration, $866.0M in total, falls due in installments through around December 2027; paid in cash, it would consume most of the $841.0M of liquidity, and paid in stock at a low price it dilutes more.

**Downside scenario:** the February baseline wrote that if the DOJ formally prosecutes and Novo wins an injunction, GLP-1 (~30% of revenue) goes to zero, and combined with brand damage slowing non-GLP-1 growth to 15-20%, the stock could fall materially from current levels. Quantified on 2026-10-02 (see section 8): FY2027 revenue +8%, FY2028 +3%, margins stuck at 8.5-9%, a 10x exit and an extra $100M of legal outflows leave about $1.5B of net claims against about $3.1B of enterprise value, or about $6.11 per share (−50.2% a year). The severity of the bear case comes mainly from leverage, not from the revenue assumptions themselves.

## 7. Key Uncertainties and Thesis-Breaking Conditions

**What we don't know:** the DOJ trajectory (referral stage in February — escalation to a formal case would reach far beyond GLP-1; no development as of the Q2 10-Q); whether Novo refiles (its 2026-03-09 dismissal was without prejudice); the size of the FTC case and its non-monetary remedies (about $60M accrued, which the company says may change materially); the FDA's final compounded-drug policy; the revenue share and gross margin of weight management after the branded-GLP-1 shift (not disclosed); and the true growth rate of the non-GLP-1 segment, which disclosures make hard to isolate.

**Thesis-breaking conditions:** bear invalidated if the DOJ referral fizzles and the FTC case settles for a limited amount; bull severely damaged if the FDA bans GLP-1 compounding entirely and the DOJ formally prosecutes. Other breakers: non-GLP-1 growth slowing, rapid GLP-1 compression with no replacement, a brand-trust-damaging regulatory event, or low-quality growth driven by excessive marketing spend. From 2026-10-02 the quantified thresholds are the monitoring checklist in section 9 and the stance triggers in section 10.

Hims is a well-executed DTC telehealth company with **2.891M subscribers as of Q2 2026** (the 2.5M level cited earlier is the historical February baseline) and a steadily growing non-GLP-1 core (~70% of revenue on the February measure). But the regulatory pressure did not disappear with the Novo dismissal: the DOJ referral is still open, and the FTC privacy-and-billing case is now in litigation with about $60M accrued; management's aggressive GLP-1 strategy also showed questionable judgment.

**Core tension:** excellent core business vs self-inflicted regulatory trouble. This rerun reframes the stock from an acute GLP-1 binary into a growth-quality audit. This is a monitoring view, not allocation advice: the thesis is highly binary and event-driven; any position should sit within a risk budget appropriate for a high-volatility, event-driven asset rather than a fixed weight. The current **$6,836.1M** market cap (2026-10-01 close of $29.30; the $7.12B, $7.41B, $7.77B and $6.29B figures elsewhere are old-caliber levels) depends on whether revenue is durable, compliant, and convertible into cash flow.

**What Q2 2026 changed (recorded 2026-08-12, historical):** durability and compliance were still open, but *convertible into cash flow* moved the wrong way — a 64% gross margin, an $86.3M quarterly net loss and negative quarterly free cash flow, set against re-accelerated growth and raised FY2026 guidance. Recomputed on the 2026-08-11 close at the time, the stance stayed neutral / watchlist at medium confidence.

**(1) The expectations gap in one sentence.** At $29.30, earning the 8% hurdle at a 15x exit and a 13% FY2028 margin requires revenue growth of about 24.5% a year in 2027-2028 (FY2028 about $4.96B), faster than the roughly 19.4% implied by management's 2030 target; our base case is 17.5% — we agree growth continues, but not that it outruns management's own long-term path while gross margin is 64%, GAAP results are loss-making and the FTC case is live.

**(2) Stance and conviction, derived from the scenario weights.**
- **The grid:** the 30/40/30 grid (bull $62.14 / base $30.31 / bear $6.11) weights to **$32.60 per share, +11.3% against $29.30, or +4.9% annualized** over 2.248 years — 3.1 points below the 8% hurdle; the base case alone earns only +1.5% a year. Hence **stance: cautious**.
- **Why not neutral-watch:** both the weighted and the base-case returns sit below the hurdle at central assumptions; softening the bear case to $9.28 per share still leaves only +6.2%; and the reverse test shows the price needs growth faster than management's own long-term path. A neutral label would hide that arithmetic.
- **Why not bearish-avoid:** the bull case's +112.1% is a real upside path; revenue growth has re-accelerated, guidance was raised, narrow EV is only 2.31x FY2026 revenue, and the weighted return is still positive.
- **Why conviction is low:** (a) single inputs flip it: exit multiples +3 turns give +14.1%, growth +5 points gives +9.4%, a FY2028 margin 2 points higher gives +12.5%, and 40/40/20 weights give +12.5%; (b) roughly $1.4-1.5B of net claims magnify the equity's sensitivity to operating assumptions; (c) the post-shift business mix is undisclosed, and the FTC and DOJ outcomes sit outside the grid (the bear case carries only an extra $100M of legal outflows).

**This is a valuation-and-skew judgment at today's price, not a denial of the growth evidence.** The 2026-08-12 neutral / watchlist call was made without a scenario grid to 2028; once the grid and hurdle test were done, the conclusion changed to cautious.
