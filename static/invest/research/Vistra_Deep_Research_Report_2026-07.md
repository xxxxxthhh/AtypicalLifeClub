## 1. Business And Chain Role

Vistra is an integrated generation and retail power platform with nuclear, gas, coal / transition assets, and retail power capabilities. AI data centers do not need only "green power"; they need dispatchable capacity that can come online quickly and match load volatility. VST therefore carries more merchant power and gas-capacity beta than CEG.

The chain role is to prevent the power layer from becoming a single clean-nuclear story. If real data-center growth requires a mixed stack, VST should show it through Meta nuclear agreements, Cogentrix gas capacity, PJM/Texas capacity prices, and cash-flow support for leverage. If VST weakens while CEG holds, the power thesis may be real but narrower and cleaner than the market's broad trade.

## 2. Competition And Industry Structure

VST competes in the part of the power stack where reliability, speed and geography matter. It does not have CEG's cleaner nuclear-led quality profile, and it does not have NRG's retail/VPP breadth. Instead, VST is the direct test of whether existing nuclear and gas assets can be repriced together as data centers compete for reliable power.

## 5. Bull Case

The bull case is that AI data centers need both clean baseload and dispatchable capacity. VST owns both, and the Meta agreements validate direct hyperscaler procurement from existing nuclear assets. Cogentrix adds about 5,500MW of gas generation, which can become more valuable if PJM/Texas capacity markets continue to price data-center load growth.

VST also has higher equity beta than CEG. If CEG anchors the quality trade and NRG proves broader merchant/retail scarcity, VST can sit in the middle: more contracted than NRG, more levered and capacity-sensitive than CEG. A strong bull read requires Meta execution, Cogentrix conversion, firm capacity prices, and FCF that covers leverage rather than merely funding expansion.

## 6. Bear Case

The bear case is that VST is a power-scarcity trade with too much leverage and too much commodity/regulatory exposure. Net debt was about $19.0B at 9-30 and about $23.9B pro forma after the Cogentrix close and preferred redemption, and acquisitions plus capex can absorb cash quickly. Even with $4,330M of nine-month operating cash flow, the equity has less room for error if gas prices, capacity prices, or regulatory decisions move against the company.

The PPA details also remain important. Meta's involvement validates the category, but pricing, cost allocation, uprate timing, and regulatory approval can determine whether the economics match the market's expectations. If AI capex or data-center growth expectations cool, VST can de-rate faster than CEG because it is the higher-beta expression of the same power theme.

VST's uncertainties are sharper than CEG's because the upside is more directly tied to capacity markets, gas assets, and leverage. The report should be re-rated only when the uncertainty table changes, not when the AI power narrative gets louder.

### Conclusion And Review

Vistra is the high-beta comparison to Constellation. CEG is the steadier nuclear-led common-constraint anchor; VST is the nuclear plus gas plus merchant-capacity dashboard. Covering it prevents the power layer from becoming only a clean nuclear story when real data centers may need a mixed power stack.

VST's chain-validation job is to test whether AI power demand spreads from clean baseload PPAs into dispatchable gas capacity, PJM/Texas capacity value, and the repricing of existing generation assets. It is higher beta than CEG and more pure-power than NRG; that lets it validate power scarcity faster, but also makes it reverse faster when gas, regulation, capacity prices, or leverage move the wrong way.

Current basis (2026-11-15): at the 2026-11-13 close of $133.01, about 3.43x EV/TTM revenue, about 18.0x TTM GAAP P/E, about $23.9B of pro forma net debt and about $70.70B of EV, the market still pays a premium for Meta nuclear PPAs, Cogentrix gas capacity and PJM/Texas capacity scarcity; the $137.15 weighted value is only about 3.1% above the price and about 2.7% annualized, below the 8% reference return, so the base case recognizes real upside but does not ignore the faster downside from leverage and merchant exposure.

The current stance is **cautious, medium conviction**. The 30% bull / 40% base / 30% bear grid says the upside requires Meta PPA, Cogentrix conversion, capacity pricing, and FCF to all deliver; the bear path only needs one or two of those variables to move against the thesis. Q3 actuals and higher guidance have improved the earnings base, but Cogentrix and the preferred redemption add about $4.9B to pro forma net debt versus 9-30, and the $137.15 weighted value is only about 3.1% above $133.01, or about 2.7% annualized, below the 8% reference return. Cautious is not a bearish call on the mixed power stack; it is the judgment that at the current price high beta's downside speed still exceeds the certainty of upside.

Upgrade trigger: review for neutral-watch if a regular close is at or below about $125.71 (which would lift the current weighted annualized return to the 8% reference) with scenarios and funding intact, or if the Meta PPA capacity / price / uprate path becomes clearer, Cogentrix converts about 5,500MW into data-center or capacity-market earnings, PJM/Texas capacity prices keep rising, and FCF supports deleveraging.
