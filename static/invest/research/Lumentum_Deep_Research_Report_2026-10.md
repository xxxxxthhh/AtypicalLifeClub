## Executive Summary <!-- report-module:overview -->

**One-line thesis:** Lumentum supplies EML/CW lasers for pluggable transceivers, external laser sources for CPO and optical circuit switches (OCS), while also manufacturing modules through Cloud Light. FY2026 revenue growth of 83.2% and Q4 non-GAAP operating margin of 36.6% show that bottleneck economics have reached its financial statements. At $824.13, however, working diluted EV/revenue remains 27.5x. The model needs roughly $12B of FY2029 revenue and a 43% adjusted operating margin merely to approach an 8% annualized return hurdle. Investment appeal still depends on how quickly capacity becomes cash.

**Investment judgment:** Neutral-watch, low conviction. The 30% bull / 40% base / 30% bear FY2029 grid produces a weighted future scenario value of $1,007.63 per share, 22.3% above the 2026-10-16 close, or 7.7% annualized over 2.705 years, slightly below the 8% hurdle. Four fewer turns on every exit multiple reduce that return to 1.3%. These are conditional valuation outcomes, not price targets.

**Catalysts and window:** Q1 FY2027 results after the close on 2026-11-05, followed by two quarters of OCS, CPO laser, margin and cash-conversion evidence. The longer window extends to architecture production in late 2027 and the Greensboro fab ramp in 2028.

**Customers are buying different functions.** Module customers buy qualified, reliable laser sources and yields; CPO customers buy higher-power shared external sources and lifetime reliability; OCS customers buy reconfigurable optical paths. OCS does not process Ethernet packets and should not be subtracted one-for-one from all switch-ASIC revenue. Lumentum makes modules itself while supplying components to competing module vendors, so customers and competitors overlap.

**200G EML versus CW/silicon photonics:** EML integrates light generation and modulation capabilities in the device. Silicon-photonics architectures still need a laser, but source count, power, packaging and dollar content per port can change. Supplying both EML and CW reduces single-route risk without guaranteeing that revenue and margins are retained proportionately. The 10-K supplies a useful contrast: laser-chip ASPs increased modestly while cloud-module ASPs declined. Our inference is that upstream scarcity economics are currently more visible. Continuing margins, procurement prices and cash must confirm durability; an architectural diagram alone cannot.

**Cross-check conclusion:** LITE best tests upstream component profits and new architectures; COHR tests wider photonics expansion and its cash cost; AAOI tests actual high-speed module manufacturing, qualification and financing cost. LITE component price increases alongside AAOI module-margin pressure could indicate profits moving upstream. Simultaneously accelerating inventory and capex with worsening cash collection across all three could instead indicate a capacity race. It should not be treated as three independent bullish signals.

## 5. Bull Case <!-- report-module:bullBear -->

1. **Upstream profit has arrived.** Q4 GAAP gross margin was 47.4%, versus COHR's 38.5% and AAOI's 27.7%. Mix differs, but LITE's rising chip ASPs alongside falling module ASPs support the inference of greater current component scarcity.
2. **Growth sources are broadening.** FY2026 OCS revenue exceeded $90M, with the October letter updating to a quarterly run rate above $100M. 1.6T modules and external CPO sources can add revenue outside the traditional telecom cycle.
3. **Near-term guidance exceeds the formal model.** At $1.25B of quarterly revenue, 40% guided operating margin exceeds the OFC model's 33%–37%. If that efficiency persists, FY2029 scenario margins of 43%–46% are not entirely disconnected from current leverage.
4. **Route coverage reduces single-point risk.** EML, CW, UHP, OCS and the newly demonstrated VCSEL route address different distances and topologies. Silicon-photonics growth need not eliminate laser revenue.

Quantified upside scenario: FY2027–FY2029 revenue of $6.6B / $11B / $16B, adjusted operating margins of 41% / 44% / 46%, 108M FY2029 shares, a 16.5% tax rate and a 28x operating-earnings exit multiple yield $1,647.20 per share at exit, +99.9% versus $824.13 and +29.2% annualized. This requires materially exceeding the company's $8B annualized model and rapid new-fab delivery. It receives only 30% weight and is not company guidance.

## 6. Bear Case <!-- report-module:bullBear -->

1. **High growth remains necessary to support the price.** At a 24x exit and 43% margin, merely preserving the share price needs approximately $9.78B of FY2029 revenue, a 48.0% three-year CAGR from FY2026. Earning 8% requires about $12.17B and 59.2% CAGR.
2. **Capacity leads revenue.** Capex was $451.3M and purchase commitments $2.35B; COHR and AAOI are expanding too. Long-term demand contracts do not eliminate qualification, yield, pricing, delay or customer-insourcing risks.
3. **Shareholder outcomes trail adjusted earnings.** $281M of cash withholding coexists with unvested awards and excess-conversion settlement. Historical $8.67 non-GAAP EPS understates today's share base if used alone.
4. **Architectural hedging does not hedge margins.** CW and VCSEL can expand opportunities while introducing competitors. Module ASPs declined despite revenue growth above 173%. Current laser premiums may fade as supply expands.
5. **Customer and financing links increase correlation.** The top two customers account for 41.6%. NVIDIA supports both LITE and COHR, so a major customer's financing, technology or topology change can affect multiple suppliers together.

The bear case still permits $7.5B of revenue rather than a collapse. A 33% FY2029 adjusted operating margin, 18x exit and 108M shares produce $376.61, -54.3% versus the close and -25.1% annualized. If growth matures quickly after FY2029, the cash-flow stress test can imply even less, as shown in Section 8.

[Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1158114/000143774926026278/aaoi20260630_10q.htm), [property 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1158114/000168316826007057/aaoi_8k.htm), [ATM completion](https://investors.ao-inc.com/news-releases/news-release-details/applied-optoelectronics-announces-completion-previously).

The addition to existing research is to pair COHR's scale and lower multiples with negative FCF, AAOI's module upside with post-financing shares and cash burn, and LITE's higher margin with almost three times COHR's annualized-guidance revenue multiple. ### Multiple methods and limitations

| Method | Conditional outcome | Assumptions and purpose | Main limitation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| FY2029 operating-earnings scenarios | $376.61–$1,647.20; weighted $1,007.63 | 18x–28x exits plus net cash, 108M shares; detailed below | Long-term growth, margins and exit multiples all matter |
| Weighted grid discounted to price date | $818.26 at 8%; $741.59 at 12% | 2.705-year discount; return-hurdle interpretation of the same grid | Not an independent valuation proof; probabilities are judgments |
| FY2029 EV/revenue cross-check | $932.64–$1,377.08 at exit | Base $12B revenue at 8x–12x sales plus base net cash | Still substantial hardware premiums; does not establish that the multiples are justified |
| Mature-cash-flow stress test | About $266–$466 at the price date | Same base operating profits; 38% conversion initially; 4% growth and 60%–80% conversion from FY2030; 10%–12% discount rate | Assumes relatively quick maturation after 2029; not the central case, but exposes reliance on a longer growth runway |

The cash-flow stress uses FY2027/FY2028/FY2029 after-tax operating earnings of $2,096.7M / $3,156.3M / $4,308.6M, discounting annual cash flows approximately 0.704 / 1.706 / 2.705 years from the price anchor. Terminal value is final after-tax operating earnings × 60%–80% × 1.04 / (discount rate − 4%). Current net cash is added and the result divided by 108M shares. The material gap from the exit-multiple method means a **24x exit assumes more years of high growth or better conversion after FY2029**. If the business matures soon after the new fab comes online, the central exit multiple cannot be retained.

**Multiple and probability rationale:** The 18x/24x/28x exits are research assumptions about remaining FY2029 growth, not peer spot multiples transplanted directly. Stagnant bear revenue and a 33% margin receive the lowest multiple. The base still grows beyond the $8B annualized model, but revenue growth slows from 45% in FY2028 to 33% in FY2029, warranting substantial compression from today's 49.1x annualized guided EPS. The bull retains more premium for $16B revenue and 46% margin. A mature perpetuity with 60%–80% cash conversion and 10%–12% capital cost needs roughly 6.5%–9.3% long-term growth to support 24x earnings. That is not a conservative mature-hardware multiple: the central grid implicitly requires another growth stage after FY2029.

The 30/40/30 weights are judgments under limited evidence, not historical frequencies. Q4 delivery and new products retain upside, while unverified new-fab output, concentration and cash absorption give the bear the same 30% weight as the bull. The base deliberately lacks a majority because it already incorporates strong growth and a margin above the company's $8B model ceiling. A 20/40/40 weighting produces just 2.5% annualized, so the main grid is not a robust purchase case. The 8% hurdle is a comparison floor; a 12% requirement for volatile hardware implies a price reading around $742.

**Reading the return:** +7.7% annualizes the probability-weighted future dollar value. It is neither a promised compound return nor the average of scenario annualized returns. With the same 30/40/30 probabilities, the arithmetic average of scenario annualized returns is about +4.2%, and annualized probability-weighted log growth is about +1.9%. The large upside outcome raises expected dollars. All these readings depend on subjective probabilities and expose fragile return asymmetry.

Construction: after-tax operating earnings = revenue × adjusted operating margin × (1 − 16.5%). EPS equals those earnings divided by 108M shares. Net cash equals current $1,091.3M plus 38% of cumulative FY2027–FY2029 after-tax operating earnings, approximating historical FCF/adjusted-income conversion and excluding interest income. Future scenario value = EPS × exit multiple + cash/share. This is an **operating-earnings multiple excluding cash interest**, avoiding double-counting cash income when adding cash. The 108M future denominator is a research dilution assumption, not guidance.

The base case's **58.5%** three-year revenue CAGR and roughly fourfold FY2026 revenue are already demanding. The $8B annualized model plus $5B theoretical fab capacity is not a $13B sales guarantee; the grid makes strong assumptions about qualification, yields, customers and utilization. A 38% cash-conversion assumption uses the expansion-year experience but does not separately deduct future RSU withholding, potentially overstating retained cash. The zero-conversion sensitivity shows the exposure.

**Priced-in expectations and the gap:** Holding the base terminal cash balance fixed, a 43% margin, 24x exit and 108M shares require FY2029 revenue of approximately **$9.78B** merely to preserve the price (48.0% FY2026–FY2029 CAGR), **$12.17B** for an 8% annualized return (59.2% CAGR), and **$12.82B** for 10% (62.0% CAGR). ## 10. Conclusion <!-- report-module:conclusion -->

**(1) Chain-validation job.** LITE is an optical-layer architecture check: EML/CW component profits, OCS and CPO revenue test upstream profit migration and additive architectures. COHR/AAOI comparisons show real margin differences, but common hyperscaler spending means broader coverage does not establish independent demand. October's VCSEL demonstration also requires room for short-reach solutions outside InP.

**(2) Expectations gap.** At the 2026-10-16 close of $824.13, working EV of $82.97B and 27.5x TTM sales, FY2029 revenue of approximately $12.17B and a 43% adjusted operating margin are needed to earn 8% annualized at a 24x exit. Our base is $12B. Revenue growth alone is insufficient evidence of excess returns.

**(3) Stance and conviction.** The 30/40/30 grid weights to $1,007.63 and +7.7% annualized over 2.705 years, close to the 8% hurdle: **neutral-watch**. Four fewer exit turns reduce return to +1.3%, 20% lower revenue to -0.7%, and mature-cash-flow values are much lower: **low conviction**. Operating improvement warrants attention, but the model does not establish robust positive return asymmetry.

**(4) Upgrade and downgrade conditions.** Upgrade to constructive around $740 or below without operating/cash deterioration, or if a higher long-term model and near-term revenue/margin above guidance lift the rebuilt annualized grid to at least 12%. Downgrade to cautious for Q1 revenue below $1.225B or non-GAAP operating margin below 39.5%; withdrawn/materially delayed OCS objectives; two quarters of GM below 47% with component price cuts; dilution above 104M without better cash retention; or a price above roughly $910 without stronger earnings/cash assumptions. Each trigger requires recalculation rather than mechanical trading.
