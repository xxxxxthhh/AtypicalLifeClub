### 1.3 Baseline Business Profile and Model

AMD is a fabless high-performance computing chip company. Its core assets are not manufacturing capacity, but CPU/GPU/SoC architecture design, chiplet integration, software and hardware roadmaps, and the ability to productize custom platforms for large customers. Manufacturing depends mainly on foundry partners such as TSMC, so AMD has lower capital intensity than an IDM model, while advanced-node, advanced-packaging, and geopolitical supply-chain concentration remain long-term risks.

The four operating segments carry different economics:

| Segment | Role | Economic traits | Current importance |
|---------|------|-----------------|--------------------|
| Data Center | EPYC server CPUs + Instinct AI GPUs | High ASPs, concentrated customers, long validation cycles, highest margin upside | Core valuation driver |
| Client | Ryzen desktop and mobile CPUs | More cyclical; affected by PC shipments and channel inventory | Scale and cash-flow stabilizer |
| Gaming | Radeon GPUs + semi-custom console chips | Discrete GPU competition is intense; semi-custom follows console cycles | Non-core, but broadens ecosystem reach |
| Embedded | Xilinx FPGA / adaptive SoC assets | Long product lives, sticky customers, steadier demand profile | Portfolio resilience |

Commercially, AMD sells chips and platform products to OEMs, cloud providers, system integrators, enterprise customers, and distribution channels. The AI data center business looks more like project-based platform selling: customers assess GPU performance, CPU attach, networking and rack architecture, software stack, supply assurance, and total cost of ownership together. Client and gaming remain more standard product-cycle businesses, with stronger exposure to macro demand and channel inventory.

AMD's moat can be separated into three layers:

1. **CPU moat:** the x86 duopoly, EPYC performance-per-watt gains, and cloud qualification cycles give server CPU share gains persistence.
2. **AI GPU moat:** still much weaker than NVIDIA's; the ROCm / CUDA ecosystem gap remains the biggest constraint. AMD's opening comes from multi-vendor sourcing, price/performance windows, and customized platform partnerships.
3. **System-level moat:** Helios, MI450/MI455X, 6th Gen EPYC Venice, advanced packaging, and regional supply-chain investments show AMD trying to move from "chip substitute" to "second AI infrastructure platform."

AMD analysis therefore cannot be reduced to the AI GPU line alone. The AI narrative drives valuation elasticity, but EPYC, Client, Embedded, and the Xilinx assets determine whether AMD has enough cash flow and customer depth to move through an AI investment cycle.

## 2. Business and Competitive Position <!-- report-module:competition -->

### 2.1 AMD Is Moving From Chip Supplier to AI Infrastructure Platform Vendor

The key change is not simply that AMD is selling more GPUs. The strategic narrative is moving from single-chip substitution toward rack-scale AI infrastructure:

- EPYC CPUs continue to gain server share and provide the platform attach point.
- Instinct GPUs ramped through MI300/MI350. The 10-Q lists the MI450 launch among Data Center drivers. On the call, the issuer said Q3 2026 was the first quarter it shipped MI455 and Helios racks for revenue (third-party transcripts). The 10-Q says AMD does "not manufacture or sell the completed Helios rack-scale solutions," so what AMD sells is the products it supplies. No shipped-gigawatt figure was given.
- ROCm still trails CUDA materially, but multi-supplier strategies give AMD a real entry window.
- Helios rack-scale architecture ties CPU, GPU, networking, software, and systems partners into a more complete AI infrastructure offering. The 10-Q draws the boundary: AMD develops and licenses Helios, supplies certain products used in it, and does not manufacture or sell the completed racks, so third-party networking, memory, power and cooling can delay AMD's revenue.

### 2.2 Meta and OpenAI Are Both Contracted Anchors; Neither Has a Published Shipped-GW Figure

On February 24, 2026, AMD and Meta announced a multi-year, multi-generation partnership to deploy up to **6GW** of AMD Instinct GPUs. The first **1GW** deployment was expected to begin shipping in H2 2026 and is based on custom MI450-architecture GPUs, 6th Gen EPYC "Venice" CPUs, ROCm, and Helios rack-scale architecture. Source: [AMD and Meta 6GW Partnership](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1279/amd-and-meta-announce-expanded-strategic-partnership-to-deploy-6-gigawatts-of-amd-gpus).

The filings give the same structure for both customers. In the [OpenAI 8-K of 2025-10-06](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2488/000119312525230895/d28189d8k.htm), OpenAI made a binding commitment for the initial 1GW of MI450 Series products and received a warrant for up to 160M shares at $0.01. In the [Meta 8-K of 2026-02-24](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2488/000000248826000045/amd-20260223.htm), Meta made a binding commitment for the initial 1GW equivalent and received the same size of warrant. For both, the first tranche vests after the initial 1GW is delivered or shipped, full vesting needs 6GW of purchases, stock-price thresholds escalate to $600 for the final tranche, and each tranche also needs technical and commercial conditions to be met before exercise.

The [Q3 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2488/000000248826000191/amd-20260926.htm) adds current status. As of 2026-09-26 no warrant shares had vested or become exercisable. The warrant liability was $1.2B, and the cash-flow statement shows a $1,179M non-cash warrant impact against a $1,097M fair-value charge, which leaves about $82M that we infer is the first reduction of revenue. The 10-Q describes the liability as "attributable to qualifying customer revenue recorded to date," so qualifying revenue has begun; it does not give a gigawatt count. Slide 24 of Ex 99.2 says Q4 share count is not expected to be affected by the warrants, and Jean Hu said AMD will guide on vesting during 2027 (call).

AMD's [2026-10-27 release with Meta](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1302/amd-and-meta-advance-strategic-partnership-to-power-metas-superintelligence-infrastructure) gives the latest dated status for Meta: initial Helios racks "are installed and being tested by Meta on the path to a broader deployment in the near future," Meta will deploy a custom Instinct GPU, and AMD will invest $1 billion in Meta's equity offering. So in late October the first Meta racks were in testing rather than broad deployment. A week later the Q3 release listed Meta among the customers running models and workloads on the first Helios racks. Neither document gives a gigawatt figure.

AMD's Q2 2026 release also referred to up to 2GW of MI450 with Anthropic. The 10-Q lists warrants to two customers only.

This is a major strategic validation, but it also concentrates the thesis:

- If first deployments finish cleanly, AMD's AI platform credibility improves sharply.
- If timing slips, scale is reduced, or margins disappoint, the 2027 marker and the valuation resting on it become fragile.

### 2.4 Market Size, Share, and Ecosystem Gap

**Addressable market (February baseline):**

- AI accelerators (data center GPU): about $27-37B in 2026, $125-325B by 2034, CAGR ~22-36%.
- Server CPU: about $13.9B in 2026, mid-single-digit growth, x86 dominance challenged by Arm. This February figure is well below AMD's own run rate; on the 2026-11-03 call management put the server CPU TAM at about $220B by 2030, growing at about a 50% CAGR over the next 3-4 years. That is the company's projection, not an independent estimate.
- Broader AI infrastructure: 2026 hyperscaler AI capex ~$450B; NVIDIA's estimated 2030 data center capex TAM ~$3-4T.

**Market share (February baseline):**

- AI accelerators: NVIDIA ~86-90%, AMD ~5-8% (growing fast off a low base), Intel ~2-3%.
- Server CPU: AMD gaining share since 2018, estimated ~20-25% (Intel ~75-80%); Arm (AWS Graviton, etc.) is the medium-term threat.

**Ecosystem gap:** CUDA has ~4M developers and 18+ years of optimization; ROCm targets 100K developers in 2026 (~1/40 the size), with a real-world performance gap of ~10-30%; AMD typically prices ~15-40% below NVIDIA to win deals. This is the quantitative reason the AI GPU moat is still fragile while the CPU moat is comparatively stable.

### 3.2 Red Flag Review

## 4. Management and Execution Quality <!-- report-module:management -->

Lisa Su has served as AMD's CEO since 2014 and led the company from a fragile balance-sheet, product-disadvantaged cyclical semiconductor business into the main x86 server CPU challenger and a credible second-tier AI accelerator platform. Zen, the EPYC roadmap, the Xilinx acquisition, chiplet strategy, and the data center product cadence are the clearest evidence of management execution quality.

Current management strengths:

1. **Roadmap continuity:** CPU and GPU product cadence has avoided major disruptive gaps, and EPYC/Ryzen have already shown multiple cycles of execution.
2. **Mostly rational capital allocation:** Xilinx expanded AMD's Embedded / FPGA / adaptive computing footprint. The integration cycle is long, but it improves portfolio resilience.
3. **Relatively disciplined communication:** AMD generally avoids the kind of aggressive far-future extrapolation common in early-stage growth software stories, which matters in a volatile semiconductor cycle.
4. **Large-customer programs are in the proof phase:** Meta and OpenAI 6GW agreements, Helios, MI450, and Taiwan/UK ecosystem investments require management to prove system-level delivery in 2026-2027. Qualifying revenue in Q3 and a revenue result above the guide are real evidence; first-1GW completion is still unconfirmed.

Management risks to monitor:

- Dilution from stock compensation and the Meta and OpenAI warrant milestones.
- Whether AI platform investment compresses near-term margins.
- Whether ROCm ecosystem catch-up takes longer than the market expects.
- If AI capex slows, whether management can adjust $11,463M of inventory, $48,761M of purchase commitments, and cost structure quickly enough.
- Capital allocation is shifting toward customer and ecosystem support and stock-funded deals: about $11.1B of pending acquisitions, up to $8.5B of guarantees, up to $5.1B of equity commitments, and $1 billion put into the equity offering of Meta, its anchor customer (2026-10-27 release).

Overall, AMD's management remains high quality within semiconductors, and the 2027 markers raise the stakes on that record. They now carry most of the valuation. The next evidence needed is quarterly proof from Meta/Helios, MI450, EPYC Venice, and ROCm.

## 5. Bull Case <!-- report-module:bullBear -->

The bull case is not that AMD is cheap. It is that AMD can evolve from NVIDIA's secondary alternative into a durable second AI infrastructure platform, and that 2027 is a step on the way rather than the peak.

The case requires:

1. Meta's and OpenAI's first 1GW deployments complete and become visible in revenue. Qualifying customer revenue has begun (10-Q); completion is unconfirmed.
2. MI450/Helios meets hyperscaler requirements on performance, power, stability, and delivery.
3. ROCm keeps narrowing the real-world usability gap with CUDA, at least in selected inference and private-cloud use cases.
4. EPYC server CPUs continue gaining share and improve platform attach economics.
5. Non-GAAP gross margin holds around 55% or better while opex grows less than half as fast as revenue, as the CFO described for 2027.

If those conditions hold together, AMD could reach about $170B of FY2028 revenue and retain a platform premium. The valuation section prices that path at $891.49-$893.26 per share after about 160M warrant shares vest, because success under these contracts also dilutes.

### 5.1 Bull-Case Quantitative Evidence (February baseline; see body for updates)

1. **AI accelerator inflection:** Q4 2025 Data Center $5.4B (+39%); MI300X memory bandwidth ~60% higher than H100; Oracle launch cluster of 27,000 GPUs, Crusoe 13,000 MI355 (~$400M order); sell-side estimated ~$10-12B AI GPU revenue in 2026, scaling toward "tens of billions" in 2027. Capturing 10-15% of the AI accelerator market in 2027-2028 implies roughly $15-25B in annual revenue at 55%+ gross margin.
2. **CPU cash cow:** EPYC server share ~20-25% (Intel ~75-80%); Client Q4 2025 $3.1B (+34%); the CPU business can sustain ~10-15% annual growth and contribute roughly $15-18B.
3. **Margin expansion:** Q4 2025 non-GAAP operating margin 28%, FY2025 gross margin 52% (57% in Q4), with ~55%+ gross margin on each incremental AI GPU dollar; non-GAAP operating margin could rise to 30-35% by 2027.
4. **Balance sheet:** net cash ~$9.1B, Debt/Equity 0.06, FCF $5.5B (2026E $7.2-7.4B), ~$9.4B remaining buyback authorization.
5. **ROCm catch-up:** the performance gap narrowed from ~40-50% to 10-30%, targeting 100K+ developers in 2026, at ~15-40% lower price than NVIDIA.

## 6. Bear Case <!-- report-module:bullBear -->

The bear case is also not that AMD is a bad company. It is that the current price needs the 2027 ramp and a further strong 2028, while NVIDIA's ecosystem and scale advantage remain very hard to break and a few customers carry the growth.

Main risks:

1. NVIDIA remains the overwhelming AI accelerator leader, with AMD used mainly for price discovery and supply-chain diversification.
2. Meta deployment is smaller, slower, or less profitable than the market extrapolates.
3. MI450/Helios underperforms on system-level reliability, software compatibility, or total cost of ownership.
4. AI capex growth slows in 2026-2027, hitting marginal suppliers harder.
5. Any quarterly miss triggers multiple compression from a very elevated starting point.
6. Taiwan supply-chain and export-control risks remain structural.
7. Customer-financing dependence: AMD has guaranteed up to $8.5B of partners' lease and debt obligations, committed up to $6,175M to backstop cloud capacity, and signed $13.3B of data center leases it expects to pass to partners (10-Q). It is also putting $1 billion into Meta's equity offering (2026-10-27 release). An analyst raised data-center financing directly on the call; Lisa Su said AMD is investing selectively with lead partners. This support helps the ramp happen, and it also means a weaker counterparty hurts AMD twice, through lost revenue and through the guarantee.
8. Cash conversion: Q3 FCF was 4% of revenue, inventory rose to $11,463M, and the CFO expects continued investment in inventory and capacity into 2027.
9. Margin mix: data center AI is "a little bit dilutive" to the corporate gross margin (call), and the 55% Q4 guide is below the 56% level this report has used as a test. Memory and component inflation is being shared with customers only as agreements allow (call).

### 6.1 Bear-Case Quantitative Evidence (February baseline; see body for updates)

1. **CUDA moat:** NVIDIA AI accelerator share ~86-90% (it actually rose in 2025); AMD lost about 1 point of data center share year over year in 2025 despite growth; CUDA ~4M developers vs ROCm's 100K target (~1/40); the real-world performance gap is still ~10-30%; AMD must discount ~15-40%, capping profitability. If NVIDIA holds 80%+ share, AMD's AI GPU TAM may be structurally capped around $5-10B rather than the bull case's $20-30B.
2. **AI spending cycle:** 2026 hyperscaler AI capex ~$450B with unproven ROI; if growth decelerates from ~40% to 15%, AMD AI GPU revenue could peak around $8-10B instead of $20B+.
3. **Execution and concentration:** the top 3-4 customers could be 60%+ of AI GPU revenue; Meta's first 1GW is not yet confirmed complete; AMD has a history of delays (Vega, Radeon VII).
4. **Geopolitics and supply chain:** 100% TSMC dependency; China MI308 requires annual licensing, and Q1 2026 China revenue was well below Q4 2025; regulatory change could erase ~$1-2B of revenue at once; a Taiwan Strait conflict scenario could cut revenue 15-20% and reprice the stock 40-50%.
5. **Valuation cushion:** the historical P/E range is ~15-50x, and the stock has repeatedly drawn down 30%+. The old February bear frame of $38-42B of 2027 revenue and EPS of $3.5-4.0 is stale: FY2026E revenue is already above it. The current downside path (section 8) uses FY2028 revenue of $85B, a 27% non-GAAP operating margin and 16x, which gives $186.82-$187.21 per share.

## 9. Catalysts, Monitoring, and Key Uncertainties <!-- report-module:catalysts -->

Over the next 12-18 months, delivery quality matters more than headline count:

1. Q4 2026 results against about $17B plus or minus 3% and a non-GAAP gross margin of about 55%, plus the first formal 2027 outlook (date not yet announced by the issuer).
2. Whether Data Center revenue continues to grow faster than company revenue, and whether the more-than-80% marker for 2027 holds or moves.
3. Whether Meta's and OpenAI's first 1GW deployments complete, shown by shipped-gigawatt disclosure or first-tranche warrant vesting. AMD says it will guide on vesting during 2027.
4. Whether the MI450/Helios ramp turns into repeatable orders, revenue, and margin as OEM partners begin shipping.
5. Auditable signs of ROCm developer adoption, customer migration, and model performance.
6. Net share count after stock compensation, buybacks, warrant milestones, and the Taalas and World Labs closings.
7. Whether NVIDIA Rubin / Vera Rubin compresses AMD's performance or price-performance window.
8. Any partner default, guarantee payment, or growth in the $8.5B guarantee and $13.3B lease totals, read from each 10-Q and 10-K.

### 9.1 Key Timeline and Run-Rate Targets (February baseline)

- **Key dates:** UBS Technology and AI Conference on December 1, 2026 and Barclays Global Technology Conference on December 9, 2026 (call); Q4 2026 results and 2027 outlook in early 2027 (Q4 2025 was reported on February 3, 2026; the issuer has not yet announced this date).
- **Milestone thresholds:** Data Center moving toward a $25-30B annual run rate; AI GPU revenue around $8-10B in 2026; non-GAAP operating margin above 30% by 2027; AI accelerator share toward 10%+ by end of 2026.
- **Product cadence:** MI400 series (CDNA 5) targeting 2nm, 20 PFLOPs FP8, 432GB HBM4; MI455X up to ~3 AI exaflops per rack; MI500 series planned for 2027; NVIDIA Rubin (H2 2026) about +3.3x compute over Blackwell B300.

### 9.2 Key Uncertainties and Thesis-Breaking Conditions

#### 9.2.1 AI Infrastructure Spending Sustainability

AMD's current valuation is highly dependent on hyperscalers and large internet customers continuing to expand AI infrastructure capex. The open questions are:

- Whether enterprise and platform AI workloads can keep absorbing incremental compute in 2026-2027.
- Whether hyperscalers are expanding against real demand or building ahead of demand.
- Whether model training efficiency, inference optimization, and custom ASICs reduce marginal demand for merchant GPUs.

If AI capex growth decelerates sharply, AMD may be more exposed than NVIDIA because it is still the second supplier trying to earn platform status.

#### 9.2.2 Meta Deployment Scale, Timing, and Economics

Meta 6GW is the most important verified customer anchor, but several variables remain open:

- Whether the first 1GW deployment begins shipping in H2 2026 as planned. AMD's 2026-10-27 release says initial Helios racks were installed and being tested by Meta "on the path to a broader deployment in the near future."
- GPU count, ASP, gross margin, and revenue recognition timing behind each gigawatt.
- Whether the broader 6GW roadmap scales linearly or depends on milestones, performance, and budget reconfirmation.
- The net effect of the Meta and OpenAI warrants after considering dilution, customer lock-in, and deployment visibility. The warrants carried a weighted fair value of $178.58 per share at 2026-09-26, against $14.25 at grant, so quarterly GAAP charges are expected to grow as the program advances (call).

The 6GW headline should not be mechanically translated into near-term revenue. Q3 showed qualifying revenue and a 34% Data Center margin, but no gigawatt count. The key evidence is whether Q4 2026 and early 2027 results show shipment, revenue, and margin validation together.

#### 9.2.3 ROCm and Software Ecosystem

ROCm remains the main bottleneck between hardware substitution and platform substitution. Watch:

- Whether developers, frameworks, models, and enterprise customers actually migrate, not just benchmark or pilot.
- Whether CUDA keeps high-value training workloads locked into NVIDIA.
- Whether AMD can build repeatable wins in inference, private cloud, customized systems, and multi-vendor procurement.

If ROCm progresses more slowly than expected, AMD can still benefit from supply-chain diversification, but the platform premium should compress. ROCm 10 and management's account of one Anthropic engineer bringing up a model on MI355X racks over a weekend (call) are product and anecdote evidence. They are not an auditable migration record.

#### 9.2.4 Competition and Supply Chain

Competitive risk is broader than NVIDIA alone:

- NVIDIA Rubin / Vera Rubin could widen the performance, software, and system-level gap again.
- Intel stabilization in server CPUs would cap EPYC's medium-term upside.
- Hyperscaler custom ASICs could reduce the long-term TAM for merchant GPUs.
- Advanced-node and advanced-packaging supply remain heavily tied to TSMC and Taiwan; geopolitical risk cannot be diversified away by near-term earnings strength.

#### 9.2.5 Customer Financing and AMD's Own Exposure

This is new since the last valuation review, and the 10-Q is the source:

- Maximum guarantees of $8.5B, of which $7.7B covers partners' data center leases for up to 15 years and $750M covers partners' debt for up to 6 months. No partner has defaulted and nothing has been paid.
- $6,175M of cloud-compute commitments, a maximum that falls as third parties pay.
- $13.3B of data center leases not yet commenced, expected to be assigned or subleased to partners from Q2 FY2027.
- Investments in entities that AMD treats as unconsolidated VIEs.
- A $1.0B investment in a public company in October 2026 (10-Q, unnamed). AMD's 2026-10-27 release says AMD will invest $1 billion in Meta's equity offering; we infer this is the same investment.

Against $8,697M of net cash, these are not a solvency issue while partners perform. They do link AMD's balance sheet to the same counterparties that carry its revenue growth. Lisa Su described the approach as selective and strategic (call). The disclosure does not name the partners.

#### 9.2.6 Thesis-Breaking Conditions

**Bull case invalidation signals:**

- Meta's first 1GW deployment slips materially beyond 2026, or the broader 6GW roadmap is reduced.
- Management withdraws the 2027 revenue growth marker, or cuts the marker or the formal 2027 revenue guidance midpoint to imply growth below 70%.
- Data Center revenue growth falls below company-level revenue growth for multiple quarters.
- Non-GAAP gross margin falls back toward 50%, suggesting AI platform pricing or cost pressure.
- ROCm customer cases and developer adoption fail to show auditable progress.
- NVIDIA's next platform narrows AMD's performance-per-dollar window.

**Bear case invalidation signals:**

- Meta/Helios ramps on schedule and shows up in both revenue and margin.
- AMD AI accelerator share reaches a stable double-digit level with new large customers beyond Meta.
- ROCm produces repeatable migration cases in inference and private-cloud workloads.
- Data Center mix lifts company non-GAAP operating margin toward 30%+.
- Valuation resets to a level more directly supportable by FCF, EPS, and verifiable orders.
- FY2028 visibility appears: multi-gigawatt MI500 commitments that are binding and in filings rather than described on a call.
